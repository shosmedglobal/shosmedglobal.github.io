Q1  [chem-369]  correct answer: B   (KEEP: unchanged)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In human cells, most enzymes of the citric acid cycle are located in the:

  A. Cytoplasm
  B. Mitochondrial matrix   <= key
  C. Inner mitochondrial membrane
  D. Endoplasmic reticulum

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Mitochondrial matrix
Most citric acid cycle reactions occur in the mitochondrial matrix. The important exception is succinate dehydrogenase, which is embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane and also functions as Complex II of the electron transport chain. The cycle oxidizes acetyl-CoA, releases CO₂ and transfers electrons to NAD⁺ and enzyme-bound FAD. These electrons can then support oxidative phosphorylation.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Cytoplasm: Glycolysis takes place in the cytosol. The citric acid cycle is mainly in the mitochondrial matrix, where acetyl-CoA can come from carbohydrate, fatty acid or amino acid metabolism. Entry into the cycle does not always require pyruvate as the source.
C. Inner mitochondrial membrane: The inner mitochondrial membrane contains the electron transport chain and ATP synthase. It also contains succinate dehydrogenase, but most citric acid cycle enzymes are in the matrix, which is what the stem asks.
D. Endoplasmic reticulum: The ER is involved in protein folding (rough ER) and lipid synthesis (smooth ER), not in the citric acid cycle.
Bottom Line: Per acetyl-CoA, a turn of the cycle yields 3 NADH, one FAD-linked reduction, 1 GTP or ATP, and 2 CO₂. The FAD is bound to succinate dehydrogenase. Most cycle enzymes are in the matrix; succinate dehydrogenase is the membrane-bound exception.

Q2  [chem-382]  correct answer: C   (KEEP: unchanged)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Two molecules with the same molecular formula but differing in the connectivity (order of bonding) of their atoms are called:

  A. Enantiomers
  B. Geometric (cis-trans) isomers
  C. Structural (constitutional) isomers   <= key
  D. Conformational isomers

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Structural (constitutional) isomers
Structural isomers share the same molecular formula but differ in which atoms are bonded to which - they have different connectivity. For example, butane (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃) and isobutane (CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃) both have the formula C₄H₁₀ but different structural arrangements, leading to different physical and chemical properties.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Enantiomers: Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers. They have the same connectivity but differ in spatial arrangement, whereas the molecules described in the stem differ in connectivity.
B. Geometric (cis-trans) isomers: These differ in arrangement around a rigid structure (double bond or ring) - cis vs. trans - but have the same connectivity.
D. Conformational isomers: These are different spatial arrangements of the same molecule achieved by rotation around single bonds (e.g., chair vs. boat cyclohexane); they have identical connectivity.
Bottom Line: Isomers share the same molecular formula. Structural isomers differ in connectivity. Stereoisomers (geometric and optical) have the same connectivity but differ in spatial arrangement. Enantiomers are particularly important in pharmacology - different enantiomers can have dramatically different biological activities.

Q3  [chem-396]  correct answer: C   (KEEP: unchanged)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which formula correctly represents calcium phosphate?

  A. CaPO₄
  B. Ca₂(PO₄)₃
  C. Ca₃(PO₄)₂   <= key
  D. Ca₃PO₄

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Ca₃(PO₄)₂
Calcium forms Ca²⁺ ions, and phosphate is the polyatomic ion PO₄³−. To balance the charges, we need the least common multiple of 2 and 3, which is 6. This requires three calcium ions (3 × +2 = +6) and two phosphate ions (2 × −3 = −6). The formula is therefore Ca₃(PO₄)₂.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. CaPO₄: This gives a total positive charge of +2 (from Ca²⁺) and a total negative charge of −3 (from PO₄³−), which are not balanced. Ionic compounds must be electrically neutral.
B. Ca₂(PO₄)₃: This gives +4 from calcium and −9 from phosphate, which is also not balanced. This formula reverses the correct subscripts.
D. Ca₃PO₄: Ca₃PO₄ contains three Ca²⁺ ions but only one PO₄³⁻ ion, giving a net charge of +3. The neutral formula requires two whole phosphate ions: Ca₃(PO₄)₂. The 4 counts oxygen atoms inside each phosphate, while the outside 2 counts phosphate groups.
Key Educational Points:
  - Balance ionic charges and use the simplest whole-number ratio of intact ions; reduce any common factor after criss-crossing charges
  - Parentheses are required around polyatomic ions when the subscript is greater than 1
  - The main mineral of bone and teeth is hydroxyapatite, Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂, a calcium phosphate mineral with a different formula from Ca₃(PO₄)₂
Bottom Line: Calcium phosphate is Ca₃(PO₄)₂ - three Ca²⁺ ions balance two PO₄³− ions for electrical neutrality.

Q4  [chem-409]  correct answer: A   (KEEP: unchanged)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Guanidine [HN=C(NH₂)₂] is a strong organic base in water. Why is its conjugate acid, the guanidinium ion, especially stable?

  A. The positive charge is stabilized by resonance delocalization over three equivalent nitrogen atoms   <= key
  B. It contains an aromatic ring that stabilizes the positive charge
  C. The sulfur atom in guanidine donates electrons to stabilize the cation
  D. Guanidinium is stabilized by intramolecular hydrogen bonding with a carbonyl group

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The positive charge is stabilized by resonance delocalization over three equivalent nitrogen atoms
When guanidine is protonated, the resulting guanidinium ion [C(NH₂)₃]⁺ has a remarkable symmetry: the positive charge is delocalized equally across three equivalent nitrogen atoms through resonance. This gives a symmetric, Y-shaped structure with three equivalent C–N bonds. The extensive delocalization makes the guanidinium ion exceptionally stable, which is why guanidine is such a strong base (pKₐ of conjugate acid ≈ 13.6).
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. It contains an aromatic ring that stabilizes the positive charge: Guanidine has no aromatic ring. It is a small, acyclic molecule. The stabilization comes from resonance across the three C–N bonds in a planar, non-cyclic arrangement.
C. The sulfur atom in guanidine donates electrons to stabilize the cation: Guanidine contains only carbon, nitrogen, and hydrogen - no sulfur. Its formula is HN=C(NH₂)₂.
D. Guanidinium is stabilized by intramolecular hydrogen bonding with a carbonyl group: Guanidine contains no oxygen and therefore no carbonyl group. Guanidinium is stabilized by charge delocalization, not hydrogen bonding to a carbonyl. Arginine's guanidinium-containing side chain also has no carbonyl group.
Key Educational Points:
  - The pKa of guanidinium, guanidine's conjugate acid, is about 13.6 in water, consistent with guanidine being a strong organic base
  - The guanidinium group is found in the amino acid arginine, which is positively charged at physiological pH
  - Three-fold symmetric resonance stabilization is the key to guanidinium's exceptional stability
Bottom Line: The guanidinium ion is exceptionally stable because its positive charge is delocalized symmetrically across three equivalent nitrogen atoms via resonance.

Q5  [chem-422]  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Mutarotation refers to the change in optical rotation that occurs when:

  A. A sugar is heated and undergoes caramelization
  B. A sugar is reduced to its corresponding sugar alcohol
  C. A polysaccharide is hydrolyzed into monosaccharides
  D. The α and β anomers of a sugar interconvert in solution through the open-chain form   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The α and β anomers of a sugar interconvert in solution through the open-chain form
Mutarotation is a change in optical rotation as a sugar's anomeric composition approaches equilibrium in solution. A cyclic hemiacetal or hemiketal can open to the carbonyl form and close again to form either anomer. The open-chain form is an aldehyde for an aldose such as glucose and a ketone for a ketose such as fructose. Once the equilibrium composition is reached, the optical rotation becomes constant.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. A sugar is heated and undergoes caramelization: Caramelization is a non-enzymatic browning reaction involving the decomposition and polymerization of sugars at high temperatures. It changes the sugar's chemical structure irreversibly and is not related to mutarotation.
C. A polysaccharide is hydrolyzed into monosaccharides: Hydrolysis of glycosidic bonds releases monosaccharides from polysaccharides. While the released monosaccharides can undergo mutarotation, the hydrolysis process itself is not mutarotation.
B. A sugar is reduced to its corresponding sugar alcohol: Reduction converts the sugar's carbonyl group to an alcohol, producing a sugar alcohol such as sorbitol from glucose. The carbon atom is still present, but it no longer forms the hemiacetal or hemiketal center needed for this anomeric interconversion. A sugar alcohol therefore does not undergo mutarotation.
Key Educational Points:
  - Mutarotation requires a free anomeric carbon (hemiacetal/hemiketal)
  - For glucose: α-D-glucose (+112°) ⇌ open-chain ⇌ β-D-glucose (+18.7°) → equilibrium at +52.7°
  - Sugars with no free anomeric carbon (e.g., sucrose, sugar alcohols) cannot undergo mutarotation
Bottom Line: Mutarotation is the change in optical rotation accompanying anomer interconversion through the open-chain form until equilibrium is reached. It requires a sugar that can open and reclose at its anomeric center.

STEM: In the conventional Haworth projection of β-D-glucopyranose, with the CH₂OH group at C5 drawn above the ring, where is the hydroxyl group at C1?

  A. Below the ring, on the opposite side from the CH₂OH group at C5
  B. In the plane of the ring
  C. Above the plane of the ring (on the same side as the CH₂OH group at C5)   <= key
  D. Absent, because C1 retains a carbonyl group in the cyclic form

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Above the plane of the ring (on the same side as the CH₂OH group at C5)
In β-D-glucopyranose, the anomeric OH at C1 is on the same side of the ring as the CH₂OH group attached to C5. With that CH₂OH group drawn above the ring, the C1-OH is also above. Alpha and beta describe this stereochemical relationship. Axial and equatorial describe positions in a particular chair conformation and should not be substituted for up and down in a Haworth projection.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Below the ring, on the opposite side from the CH₂OH group at C5: An OH below the ring would be on the opposite side from the upward CH₂OH group. That describes α-D-glucopyranose in this drawing, not the β anomer.
B. In the plane of the ring: The anomeric OH is represented above or below the ring in a Haworth projection. It is not drawn in the ring plane; here its same-side relationship to CH₂OH places it above.
D. Absent, because C1 retains a carbonyl group in the cyclic form: Glucose's C1 carbonyl becomes a hemiacetal center when the ring forms. The cyclic form has an OH at C1, whose orientation distinguishes the two anomers.
Key Educational Points:
  - In β-D-glucopyranose, C1-OH and the CH₂OH group at C5 are on the same side of the ring.
  - In α-D-glucopyranose, those groups are on opposite sides.
  - Haworth up/down describes ring-face orientation; axial/equatorial refers to a chair conformation.
Bottom Line: In β-D-glucopyranose, the C1 hydroxyl is above the ring plane, on the same side as the CH₂OH group at C5.

Q7  [chem-424]  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: What is the maximum number of stereoisomers for a molecule with three tetrahedral stereocenters and no other source of stereoisomerism?

  A. 4
  B. 6
  C. 8   <= key
  D. 16

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 8
Each tetrahedral stereocenter can have R or S configuration. Three centers therefore give at most 2³ = 8 configurations. The full maximum is reached when all eight are distinct; molecular symmetry can make some assignments identical and reduce the count. The question asks for the maximum, so the answer is 8.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 4: Four is 2², the maximum for two independent tetrahedral stereocenters. Three centers give a maximum of 2³ = 8.
B. 6: Some molecules can have six stereoisomers because symmetry reduces their count. Six is therefore not an impossible number, but it is not the maximum for the three-center case asked here.
D. 16: Sixteen is 2⁴, the maximum for four independent tetrahedral stereocenters. Three centers allow at most eight under the stated assumptions.
Key Educational Points:
  - For n tetrahedral stereocenters and no other stereoisomerism, the maximum count is 2^n.
  - Symmetry can reduce the number of distinct stereoisomers.
  - With three centers, the maximum is 2³ = 8.
Bottom Line: Three tetrahedral stereocenters allow a maximum of eight stereoisomers. The actual count can be smaller when symmetry makes configurations identical.

Q8  [chem-425]  correct answer: B

STEM: Tartaric acid has two chiral centers, yet only three stereoisomers exist instead of four. This is because one form is a:

  A. Racemic mixture that counts as a single stereoisomer
  B. Meso compound with an internal plane of symmetry, making it achiral despite having chiral centers   <= key
  C. Geometric (cis-trans) isomer that cannot be separated
  D. Structural isomer rather than a stereoisomer

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Meso compound with an internal plane of symmetry, making it achiral despite having chiral centers
Tartaric acid (2,3-dihydroxybutanedioic acid) has two chiral centers, so 2² = 4 stereoisomers might be expected. However, one configuration has an internal mirror plane that makes the two halves of the molecule mirror images of each other. This meso form is superimposable on its mirror image and is therefore achiral, reducing the total from 4 to 3 stereoisomers: (R,R), (S,S), and meso (R,S).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Racemic mixture that counts as a single stereoisomer: A racemic mixture is an equal mixture of two enantiomers - it is not a single stereoisomer. The (R,R) and (S,S) enantiomers can form a racemate, but this is a mixture, not an individual stereoisomer. The reduction from 4 to 3 is due to the meso form, not racemization.
C. Geometric (cis-trans) isomer that cannot be separated: Geometric isomerism requires restricted rotation (as in alkenes or cyclic structures). Tartaric acid's stereoisomerism arises from chiral centers, not restricted rotation. The three forms are all separable.
D. Structural isomer rather than a stereoisomer: All three forms of tartaric acid have the same connectivity (structural formula). They differ only in the spatial arrangement of atoms, making them stereoisomers, not structural isomers.
Key Educational Points:
  - Meso compounds are achiral despite containing stereocenters; in meso-tartaric acid, an internal mirror plane explains the achirality.
  - Meso forms reduce the number of stereoisomers below the 2^n maximum
  - Tartaric acid: (R,R) + (S,S) are enantiomers; meso (R,S) is a diastereomer of both
Bottom Line: The meso form of tartaric acid possesses an internal plane of symmetry, making it achiral despite having two chiral centers, reducing the total stereoisomers from 4 to 3.

Q9  [chem-426]  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Two molecules are non-superimposable mirror images of each other. They are best described as:

  A. Diastereomers
  B. Conformational isomers
  C. Constitutional (structural) isomers
  D. Enantiomers   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Enantiomers
Enantiomers are stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images. Under the same achiral conditions, they have the same properties such as melting point and boiling point. Pure enantiomers rotate plane-polarized light by equal amounts in opposite directions under the same measurement conditions. They can interact differently with chiral environments, including enzymes and receptors.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Diastereomers: Diastereomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images. Their physical properties generally differ. Examples include suitable cis/trans pairs and molecules that differ at some, but not all, corresponding stereocenters.
C. Constitutional (structural) isomers: Constitutional isomers have the same molecular formula but differ in the connectivity of their atoms (e.g., butane vs. isobutane). The molecules described have the same connectivity but differ in 3D spatial arrangement, making them stereoisomers.
B. Conformational isomers: Conformers differ through rotations about single bonds and related changes in molecular shape. Ordinary cyclohexane conformers interconvert readily. The relationship specified here, non-superimposable mirror images, is the definition of enantiomers.
Key Educational Points:
  - Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers.
  - Enantiomers have matching physical properties in achiral environments but can behave differently in chiral environments.
  - Chirality means that an object or molecule cannot be superimposed on its mirror image.
Bottom Line: Non-superimposable mirror images are enantiomers - the defining relationship of chiral molecules.

Q10  [chem-427]  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A tetrahedral carbon is bonded to OH, NH₂, CH₃ and H. With H pointing away from the viewer, the sequence OH → NH₂ → CH₃ runs counterclockwise. What is the configuration of this stereocenter?

  A. R configuration
  B. S configuration   <= key
  C. Z configuration
  D. E configuration

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: S configuration
In the Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP) system, the four substituents are ranked by atomic number. Here: OH (O=8) > NH₂ (N=7) > CH₃ (C=6) > H (H=1). When the lowest priority group (H) is directed away from the viewer (into the page), the remaining three groups are observed. If they decrease in priority in a counterclockwise direction, the configuration is S (sinister = left). Clockwise would be R (rectus = right).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. R configuration: R (rectus) would apply if the three groups decreased in priority in a CLOCKWISE direction when viewed with H pointing away. The question specifies counterclockwise, which gives S.
C. Z configuration: Z (zusammen = together) is used for geometric isomerism around double bonds (E/Z nomenclature), not for chiral centers. Z indicates that the two higher-priority groups are on the same side of the double bond.
D. E configuration: E (entgegen = opposite) is also used for double bond geometry, indicating that the two higher-priority groups are on opposite sides. It is not applicable to tetrahedral chiral centers.
Key Educational Points:
  - R/S designation: for chiral centers (sp³ carbon with 4 different substituents)
  - E/Z designation: for geometric isomers around double bonds
  - Counterclockwise priority decrease (with lowest priority away) = S; clockwise = R
Bottom Line: Counterclockwise decrease in priority (with the lowest-priority group pointing away) defines the S (sinister) configuration.

Q11  [chem-428]  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: 2-butene exists as cis and trans isomers. Using E/Z nomenclature, trans-2-butene is designated as:

  A. (Z)-2-butene
  B. (S)-2-butene
  C. (R)-2-butene
  D. (E)-2-butene   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: (E)-2-butene
In E/Z nomenclature, the priority of substituents on each carbon of the double bond is determined by CIP rules. In 2-butene, each double-bond carbon has a -CH₃ (higher priority) and -H (lower priority). In the trans isomer, the two methyl groups (higher priority on each carbon) are on opposite sides of the double bond, which is designated E (entgegen = opposite). In the cis isomer, they are on the same side, designated Z (zusammen = together).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. (Z)-2-butene: Z (zusammen = together) describes the arrangement where the higher-priority groups on each double-bond carbon are on the SAME side. This corresponds to cis-2-butene, not trans-2-butene.
C. (R)-2-butene: R is a stereodescriptor for chiral centers (tetrahedral stereochemistry), not for double bond geometry. 2-butene has a planar double bond and no chiral centers.
B. (S)-2-butene: Like R, the S designation applies to chiral centers and is not used for alkene geometry. Double bond stereoisomerism uses E/Z (or cis/trans) notation.
Key Educational Points:
  - E means the higher-priority substituents are on opposite sides of the double bond; trans-2-butene is an example.

  D. EC 2 - Transferases   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: EC 2 - Transferases
Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) transfers an amino group from alanine to α-ketoglutarate, forming pyruvate and glutamate. Aminotransferases are transferases, EC class 2. Classification is based on the overall reaction, which is amino-group transfer rather than hydrolysis or a net redox reaction.
| EC Class | Name | Reaction Type |
| EC 1 | Oxidoreductases | Oxidation-reduction reactions |
| EC 2 | Transferases | Transfer of functional groups |
| EC 3 | Hydrolases | Bond cleavage with water consumed in the overall reaction |
| EC 4 | Lyases | Non-hydrolytic, non-redox cleavage, or addition to a double bond |
| EC 5 | Isomerases | Intramolecular rearrangements |

| EC 7 | Translocases | Movement of ions or molecules across or within membranes |

C. EC 3 - Hydrolases: Hydrolases use water as a reactant in the overall bond-cleavage reaction. In the net ALT reaction, an amino group moves between alanine and α-ketoglutarate; there is no net consumption of water. ALT is therefore classified by its amino-group transfer.
B. EC 4 - Lyases: These catalyze bond cleavage without hydrolysis or oxidation (e.g., decarboxylases, aldolases, dehydratases). The ALT reaction does not involve elimination or non-hydrolytic cleavage but rather a transfer of a functional group.
Bottom Line: Aminotransferases are EC 2 transferases. ALT transfers an amino group from alanine to α-ketoglutarate. Increased serum ALT can indicate hepatocellular injury, but it is not by itself a diagnosis of a particular disease.

Number of cycles = (n/2) - 1 = (16/2) - 1 = 7 cycles
Number of acetyl-CoA = n/2 = 16/2 = 8 acetyl-CoA

CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l), ΔH°₃ = -890.4 kJ

Using Hess's law, what is the enthalpy change per mole of methane for C(s, graphite) + 2H₂(g) → CH₄(g)?

  A. -74.7 kJ/mol   <= key
  B. +74.7 kJ/mol
  C. -1569.7 kJ/mol
  D. -496.9 kJ/mol

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: -74.7 kJ/mol
Full worked solution using Hess's law:
Target reaction: C(s, graphite) + 2H₂(g) → CH₄(g)
We need to combine the given reactions to obtain the target:
Use reaction 1 as written:
C(s, graphite) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g), ΔH₁ = -393.5 kJ
Use reaction 2 × 2:
2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l), 2 × ΔH₂ = 2 × (-285.8) = -571.6 kJ
Reverse reaction 3:
CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l) → CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g), -ΔH₃ = +890.4 kJ
Add all three:
C(s, graphite) + O₂(g) + 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) + CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l) + CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g)
Cancel common species on both sides: CO₂, 2H₂O, 2O₂
Result: C(s, graphite) + 2H₂(g) → CH₄(g)
ΔH = -393.5 + (-571.6) + 890.4 = -74.7 kJ/mol
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. +74.7 kJ/mol: +74.7 kJ/mol has the magnitude of the correct answer but the sign of the reverse target reaction, CH₄ → C(graphite) + 2H₂. Formation in the direction asked gives -74.7 kJ/mol.
C. -1569.7 kJ/mol: This value does not follow from the combination needed to obtain the target reaction. Add reaction 1 to twice reaction 2 and subtract reaction 3: -393.5 - 571.6 + 890.4 = -74.7 kJ/mol.
D. -496.9 kJ/mol: The water-formation reaction must be used twice because the target reaction consumes two moles of H₂ per mole of CH₄. Reaction 3 must then be reversed so that CH₄ is a product. This gives -393.5 + 2(-285.8) + 890.4, not -496.9 kJ/mol.
Bottom Line: Hess's law: ΔH is path-independent. To use it: (1) Write the target reaction, (2) Manipulate given reactions (reverse = change sign, multiply = multiply ΔH), (3) Add manipulated ΔH values. Always verify that intermediate species cancel.

Sodium hydride is an ionic (saline) hydride containing Na+ and H- ions. When it reacts with water, the hydride ion (H-) acts as a strong base, abstracting a proton from water:
NaH + H2O → NaOH + H2 ↑
The H- ion combines with H+ from water to produce H2 gas, while Na+ and OH- remain in solution as sodium hydroxide. This reaction is vigorous and exothermic.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. Sodium oxide and water vapor: Sodium oxide (Na2O) forms when sodium metal burns in limited oxygen (4Na + O2 → 2Na2O), not when NaH reacts with water. NaH reacting with water is a hydrolysis/acid-base reaction, not a combustion or dehydration reaction.
C. Sodium metal and hydrogen peroxide: Neither sodium metal nor hydrogen peroxide is produced by the stated reaction. Hydride accepts a proton from water to form H₂, while sodium and hydroxide remain in the solution. Sodium metal would itself react with water rather than persist as this product.
D. Sodium chloride and hydroxide ions: There is no source of chloride ions in this reaction. Only Na, H, and O atoms are involved. This answer confuses NaH with NaCl or introduces a non-existent chlorine atom.
Key Educational Points:
  - Ionic hydrides are formed by alkali metals and heavier alkaline earth metals (NaH, KH, CaH2). They contain the hydride ion (H-) with hydrogen in the -1 oxidation state.
  - Ionic hydrides react with water to produce hydrogen and the corresponding metal hydroxide. Their uses depend on the compound: NaH is a strong base, while CaH₂ is used to dry selected organic solvents.
  - Contrast with covalent hydrides (e.g., CH4, NH3, H2O) which have shared electron pairs, and metallic hydrides (e.g., PdH, TiH2) used in hydrogen storage.
  - NaH is used in organic synthesis to deprotonate suitable compounds, such as alcohols, with hydrogen gas formed.
Bottom Line: NaH + H2O → NaOH + H2. Ionic hydrides contain H- (hydrogen with oxidation state -1), which is a powerful base that reacts with water to liberate hydrogen gas and form the corresponding metal hydroxide.

STEM: When calcium carbide (CaC2) reacts with water, the gas produced is:

  A. Methane (CH4)
  B. Ethylene (C2H4)
  C. Acetylene (C2H2)   <= key
  D. Carbon dioxide (CO2)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Acetylene (C2H2)
Calcium carbide reacts with water to produce acetylene gas and calcium hydroxide:
CaC2 + 2H2O → C2H2 ↑ + Ca(OH)2
Calcium carbide contains C₂²⁻, an acetylide ion with a carbon-carbon triple bond. It accepts two protons from water to form HC≡CH. The overall products are acetylene gas and calcium hydroxide.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Methane (CH4): Methane is produced when aluminum carbide (Al4C3) reacts with water: Al4C3 + 12H2O → 4Al(OH)3 + 3CH4. Al4C3 contains the methanide ion (C⁴⁻), not the acetylide ion. Different types of carbides produce different hydrocarbons.
B. Ethylene (C2H4): Ethylene contains a carbon-carbon double bond. In calcium carbide hydrolysis, protonation of C₂²⁻ retains its triple bond and gives acetylene. Converting acetylene to ethylene would require a separate reduction, not this hydrolysis.
D. Carbon dioxide (CO2): CO2 is produced when carbonates (e.g., CaCO3) react with acids, not when carbides react with water. The reaction CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2 involves a carbonate, which is chemically distinct from a carbide.
Key Educational Points:
  - Ionic carbides include acetylides such as CaC₂, which yield acetylene on hydrolysis, and methanides such as Al₄C₃, which yield methane. Carbides such as WC and TiC are hard, heat-resistant materials; chemical inertness is not a universal property of all carbides.
  - This reaction was historically important for producing acetylene for welding torches and acetylene lamps (used by miners and in early automobiles).
  - Acetylene is the simplest alkyne (C2H2) with a linear geometry and sp hybridization at each carbon.
Bottom Line: CaC2 + 2H2O → C2H2 + Ca(OH)2. Calcium carbide contains the acetylide ion (C₂²⁻) with a triple bond. Hydrolysis produces acetylene gas. Remember: calcium carbide = acetylene, aluminum carbide = methane.

STEM: When the complete combustion equation C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O is balanced using the smallest whole-number coefficients, what is the coefficient of O₂?

  A. 3
  B. 4
  C. 5   <= key
  D. 7

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 5
The balanced combustion equation for propane is:
C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O
Balancing step by step:
1. Carbon: 3 carbons on the left → need 3CO2 on the right
2. Hydrogen: 8 hydrogens on the left → need 4H2O on the right (providing 8 H atoms)
3. Oxygen: Right side has 3(2) + 4(1) = 6 + 4 = 10 oxygen atoms → need 10/2 = 5 O2 on the left
4. Verify: Left: 3C, 8H, 10O. Right: 3C (in 3CO2), 8H (in 4H2O), 10O (6 in CO2 + 4 in H2O). Balanced.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 3: With only 3O2 (6 oxygen atoms), you could form 3CO2 (using 6 O atoms), but then zero oxygen atoms remain for H2O. Hydrogen would be unbalanced. This coefficient is too small.
B. 4: Four O₂ molecules supply eight oxygen atoms. After three CO₂ use six oxygen atoms, only two oxygen atoms remain, enough for two H₂O rather than the four required to account for all eight hydrogen atoms. Five O₂ are needed.
D. 7: With 7O2 (14 oxygen atoms), the right side would need to contain 14 oxygen atoms total. But 3CO2 + 4H2O only requires 10 oxygen atoms. The excess 4 oxygen atoms would have no place, making the equation unbalanced.
Key Educational Points:
  - The general formula for complete combustion of an alkane: CnH(2n+2) + (3n+1)/2 O2 → nCO2 + (n+1)H2O. For propane (n=3): coefficient of O2 = (3x3+1)/2 = 10/2 = 5.
  - Always balance C first, then H, then O last (since O appears in both products).
  - Combustion reactions are exothermic and are the basis for energy production from fossil fuels.
Bottom Line: C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O. Balance carbon first (3 CO2), then hydrogen (4 H2O), then count oxygen atoms needed on the right (10 total) and divide by 2 to get the O2 coefficient (5).

STEM: Insulin and inulin are two distinct biological substances. Which statement correctly distinguishes them?

  A. Insulin is a polysaccharide stored in the liver; inulin is a peptide hormone from the pancreas
  B. Insulin is a peptide hormone regulating blood glucose; inulin is a polysaccharide used to measure glomerular filtration rate   <= key

  D. Both are polysaccharides with identical chemical formulas but different structures

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Insulin is a peptide hormone regulating blood glucose; inulin is a polysaccharide used to measure glomerular filtration rate
Insulin is a 51-amino acid peptide hormone produced by the β cells of the pancreatic islets of Langerhans. It promotes glucose uptake into cells, glycogen synthesis, and lipogenesis, thereby lowering blood glucose levels.
Inulin is a naturally occurring polysaccharide (a fructan, polymer of fructose) found in plants such as chicory, dahlia tubers, and Jerusalem artichokes. In medicine, it is used as the gold standard for measuring glomerular filtration rate (GFR) because it is freely filtered at the glomerulus and is neither reabsorbed, secreted, nor metabolized by the tubules.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Insulin is a polysaccharide stored in the liver; inulin is a peptide hormone from the pancreas: This reverses the identities completely. The polysaccharide stored in the liver is glycogen, not insulin. Insulin is a protein hormone, and inulin is a polysaccharide. This swap is a common exam trap.
C. Both are peptide hormones secreted by different endocrine glands: Only insulin is a peptide hormone. Inulin is a plant-derived carbohydrate with no hormonal activity. It is not produced by any endocrine gland.
D. Both are polysaccharides with identical chemical formulas but different structures: They are not both polysaccharides (insulin is a protein), and they do not share the same chemical formula. They are entirely different classes of biomolecules: one is a protein, the other a carbohydrate.

  - The similar names (insulin vs. inulin) are a notorious source of confusion in exams. Remember: insulin = hormone from the pancreas; inulin = plant polysaccharide for GFR measurement.

Bottom Line: Insulin = peptide hormone (pancreatic β cells, regulates glucose). Inulin = polysaccharide (plant fructan, gold standard for GFR measurement). Despite nearly identical names, they are completely different substances.

STEM: Which noble gas is the most abundant in Earth's dry atmosphere?

  A. Helium (He)
  B. Neon (Ne)
  C. Argon (Ar)   <= key
  D. Xenon (Xe)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Argon (Ar)
Argon constitutes approximately 0.93% of Earth's dry atmosphere by volume, making it the most abundant noble gas and the third most abundant gas overall (after nitrogen at 78% and oxygen at 21%). Atmospheric argon is predominantly the isotope Ar-40, produced by the radioactive decay of potassium-40 (K-40) in the Earth's crust over geological time.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Helium (He): Helium is the second most abundant element in the universe (after hydrogen), but it constitutes only about 0.000524% of Earth's atmosphere. Being very light (atomic mass 4), helium has a high thermal velocity that allows it to escape Earth's gravitational field over time. Commercial helium is extracted from natural gas deposits.
B. Neon (Ne): Neon is present in the atmosphere at about 0.0018% by volume, roughly 500 times less abundant than argon. Neon is obtained by fractional distillation of liquid air and is best known for its use in neon signs, where it produces a characteristic red-orange glow.
D. Xenon (Xe): Xenon is extremely rare in the atmosphere at about 0.0000087% (87 parts per billion). It is the heaviest stable noble gas and is notably expensive. Xenon is used in high-intensity lamps, anesthesia, and ion propulsion engines for spacecraft.
Key Educational Points:
  - Noble gas atmospheric abundance order: Ar >> Ne >> He >> Kr >> Xe >> Rn.
  - Argon's abundance is due to K-40 decay (half-life 1.25 billion years): ⁴⁰K → ⁴⁰Ar via electron capture. This decay is also used in potassium-argon radiometric dating of rocks.
  - Noble gases have completely filled outer electron shells, making them chemically inert under normal conditions. However, heavier noble gases (Xe, Kr) can form compounds with highly electronegative elements like fluorine and oxygen (e.g., XeF2, XeF4).
Bottom Line: Argon makes up about 0.93% of dry air and is its most abundant noble gas and third most abundant gas overall. Atmospheric argon is predominantly ⁴⁰Ar, much of it accumulated from radioactive decay of ⁴⁰K.

STEM: Sodium chloride (NaCl) is best classified as which type of crystalline solid?

  A. Ionic crystal   <= key
  B. Molecular crystal
  C. Metallic crystal
  D. Covalent network solid

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Ionic crystal
NaCl is an ionic crystal held together by electrostatic attraction between Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. In its rock-salt structure, each ion has six nearest neighbors of the opposite charge. The crystal is brittle and has a high melting point, about 801 °C. Solid NaCl does not conduct electricity well because its ions cannot move freely; molten NaCl and its aqueous solution conduct through mobile ions.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. Molecular crystal: A molecular crystal contains discrete molecules held together mainly by intermolecular forces. NaCl instead forms an extended lattice of ions, not separate NaCl molecules.
C. Metallic crystal: Metallic bonding involves delocalized electrons shared through a metal structure. NaCl has oppositely charged ions and does not show the electron-based conductivity of a metal in its solid state.
D. Covalent network solid: A covalent network solid, such as diamond, contains atoms linked by an extended network of covalent bonds. NaCl is classified as ionic because its lattice contains Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions.
