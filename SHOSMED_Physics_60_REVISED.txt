Q1  [phys-1]  Units and Measurements  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which of the following groups contains ONLY SI base units?

  A. kilogram, candela, volt
  B. kelvin, candela, second   <= key
  C. meter, joule, ampere
  D. second, newton, mole

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: kelvin, candela, second
The SI has seven base units. Other SI units are derived from them.
| Base Unit | Symbol | Measures |
| Meter | m | Length |
| Kilogram | kg | Mass |
| Second | s | Time |
| Ampere | A | Electric current |
| Kelvin | K | Thermodynamic temperature |
| Mole | mol | Amount of substance |
| Candela | cd | Luminous intensity |
The other options contain derived units: volt (V = kg.m^2.s^-3.A^-1), joule (J = kg.m^2.s^-2), newton (N = kg.m.s^-2).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. kilogram, candela, volt: The kilogram and candela are SI base units, but the volt is derived: V = kg·m^2·s^-3·A^-1. A group must contain only base units to satisfy the question.
C. meter, joule, ampere: The meter and ampere are base units. The joule is the derived SI unit of energy, equivalent to kg·m^2·s^-2.
D. second, newton, mole: The second and mole are base units. The newton is the derived SI unit of force, equivalent to kg·m·s^-2.
Key Concept: The seven SI base units are m, kg, s, A, K, mol and cd. Derived SI units are formed from products of powers of these units. Non-SI units, such as the minute, form a separate category.

Q2  [phys-2]  Mechanics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A ball is dropped from rest from a height of 20 meters. Ignoring air resistance, approximately how long does it take to reach the ground? (g = 10 m/s^2)

  A. 1 second
  B. 2 seconds   <= key
  C. 4 seconds
  D. 10 seconds

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2 seconds
For an object in free fall starting from rest (v_0 = 0):
h = 1/2 x g x t^2
20 = 1/2 x 10 x t^2
20 = 5t^2
t^2 = 4
t = 2 seconds
We can verify: in 2 seconds, the ball reaches velocity v = g x t = 10 x 2 = 20 m/s. Average velocity = (0 + 20)/2 = 10 m/s. Distance = 10 m/s x 2 s = 20 m.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 1 second: In 1 s, a ball dropped from rest falls 1/2 x 10 x 1^2 = 5 m. It has not yet covered the 20 m to the ground.
C. 4 seconds: In 4 s of uninterrupted free fall from rest, the distance would be 1/2 x 10 x 4^2 = 80 m. This ball reaches the ground sooner.
D. 10 seconds: Dividing height by acceleration does not give a time: its units are s^2. Use t = sqrt(2h/g), which gives 2 s.
Key Concept: For free fall from rest: h = 1/2gt^2, v = gt, and v^2 = 2gh. These three equations solve most free fall problems. Objects fall with the same acceleration regardless of mass (in vacuum).

Q3  [phys-3]  Electricity  correct answer: A
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Two resistors of 6 ohms and 3 ohms are connected in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance?

  A. 2 ohms   <= key
  B. 3 ohms
  C. 4.5 ohms
  D. 9 ohms

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2 ohms
For resistors in parallel, the reciprocal formula is used:
1/R_eq = 1/R_1 + 1/R_2
1/R_eq = 1/6 + 1/3
1/R_eq = 1/6 + 2/6
1/R_eq = 3/6 = 1/2
R_eq = 2 ohms

  | Connection | Formula | Result vs Individual |
| Series | R_eq = R_1 + R_2 | Always larger than largest |
| Parallel | 1/R_eq = 1/R_1 + 1/R_2 | Always smaller than smallest |
Shortcut for two resistors in parallel: R_eq = (R_1 x R_2) / (R_1 + R_2) = (6 x 3)/(6 + 3) = 18/9 = 2
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. 3 ohms: The 3 ohm branch already provides a path for current. Adding a second positive-resistance branch in parallel increases total current at the same voltage, so the equivalent resistance is below 3 ohms.
C. 4.5 ohms: 4.5 ohms is the arithmetic mean of 6 ohms and 3 ohms. Parallel resistance is found by adding conductances, 1/R, rather than averaging the resistances.
D. 9 ohms: 9 ohms is the series result, 6 + 3. In parallel, both resistors have the same voltage across them and their currents add.
Key Concept: Parallel resistance is always LESS than the smallest individual resistor. For two resistors, use the product-over-sum shortcut. For equal resistors in parallel: R_eq = R/n.

Q4  [phys-4]  Optics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: When light passes from air into water, which has a higher refractive index, what happens to its speed and wavelength?

  A. Speed increases, wavelength increases
  B. Speed decreases, wavelength decreases   <= key
  C. Speed decreases, wavelength increases
  D. Speed increases, wavelength decreases

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Speed decreases, wavelength decreases
At a stationary boundary, light keeps the same frequency. Water has a higher refractive index than air, so the speed falls. The relation v = fλ then shows that the wavelength also falls.
| Property | In air (n_air ≈ 1) | In water (n_water ≈ 1.33) | Change |
| Speed | v_air ≈ c = 3.0 × 10^8 m/s | v_water = c/n_water ≈ 2.26 × 10^8 m/s | Decreases |
| Frequency | f | f | Unchanged |
| Wavelength | λ_air | λ_air × n_air/n_water ≈ λ_air/1.33 | Decreases |
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Speed increases, wavelength increases: Water has a higher refractive index than air, so light travels more slowly in water. With frequency unchanged, its wavelength also becomes shorter.
C. Speed decreases, wavelength increases: Frequency remains unchanged at the stationary boundary. Because v = fλ, a lower speed at the same frequency requires a shorter wavelength, not a longer one.
D. Speed increases, wavelength decreases: At unchanged frequency, speed and wavelength change in the same proportion. Light entering water from air slows down and its wavelength decreases.
Key Concept: Refractive index is n = c/v = λ_vacuum/λ_medium. Entering a higher-index medium reduces speed and wavelength while frequency stays unchanged. At oblique incidence, the ray bends toward the normal according to Snell's law; at normal incidence, its direction does not change.

Q5  [phys-5]  Thermodynamics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: What is −73 °C in kelvins, rounded to the nearest kelvin?

  A. 100 K
  B. 200 K   <= key
  C. 346 K
  D. 373 K

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 200 K
Convert Celsius temperature t to absolute temperature T using T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15. Thus T = −73 + 273.15 = 200.15 K, which rounds to 200 K.
| Reference point | Celsius | Kelvin |
| Absolute zero | −273.15 °C | 0 K |
| Water freezes, approximately at 1 atm | 0 °C | 273.15 K |
| Example body temperature | 37 °C | 310.15 K |
| Water boils, approximately at 1 atm | 100 °C | 373.15 K |
One kelvin is the same temperature interval as one degree Celsius. The kelvin scale starts at absolute zero; this does not mean that all microscopic motion disappears.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 100 K: 100 K corresponds to −173.15 °C. Converting −73 °C requires adding 273.15, giving 200.15 K before rounding.
C. 346 K: 346 K would be about +72.85 °C. This answer can result from dropping the minus sign on the given Celsius temperature.
D. 373 K: 373 K is about 100 °C, close to the boiling point of water at 1 atm. It is not the conversion of −73 °C.
Key Concept: Use T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15, or +273 when that rounding is appropriate. Gas-law and thermodynamic temperature ratios require absolute temperatures in kelvins. Write the unit as K, without a degree sign.

Q6  [phys-6]  Dynamics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: According to Newton's third law, when you push against a wall with a force of 50 N, the wall pushes back on you with a force of:

  A. 0 N
  B. 25 N
  C. 50 N   <= key
  D. 100 N

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 50 N
Newton's Third Law states: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
When you push the wall with 50 N, the wall pushes back on you with exactly 50 N in the opposite direction. These are called action-reaction pairs.
| Newton's Law | Statement |
| First Law (Inertia) | When the net external force is zero, a body remains at rest or moves at constant velocity in a straight line. |
| Second Law | F_net = ma for a constant-mass body. |
| Third Law | F_AB = −F_BA: equal magnitude, opposite direction, different bodies. |
Your push acts on the wall; the wall's push acts on you. Only one member of the pair acts on each body, so the pair does not cancel in that body's force balance.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0 N: The wall can exert a force even though it does not move. Lack of acceleration means its forces balance; it does not mean that each individual force is zero.
B. 25 N: A third-law pair has equal magnitudes. The wall does not return only half of your 50 N push.
D. 100 N: The 50 N force on the wall and the 50 N force on you act on different bodies. Adding their magnitudes does not make the force on you 100 N.
Key Concept: Third-law forces have equal magnitudes and opposite directions and act on different bodies. They do not cancel in the force balance for one body. If both bodies are included in one system, the pair cancels as internal forces.

Q7  [phys-7]  Waves and Sound  correct answer: A
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A sound wave has a frequency of 440 Hz and travels at 340 m/s in air. What is its wavelength?

  A. 0.77 m   <= key
  B. 1.29 m
  C. 149,600 m
  D. 340 m

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 0.77 m
The wave equation relates velocity, frequency, and wavelength:
v = f x λ
λ = v / f
λ = 340 / 440
λ = 0.77 m (approximately)
This is the wavelength of the note A4 (the standard tuning pitch for musical instruments).
| Sound Property | Typical value or range |
| Frequency (hearing) | 20 Hz - 20,000 Hz |
| Speed in air (20 C) | ~340 m/s |
| Speed in water | ~1500 m/s |
| Speed in steel | ~5000 m/s |
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. 1.29 m: 440/340 gives frequency divided by speed, the reciprocal of wavelength. Wavelength is v/f = 340/440 ≈ 0.77 m.
C. 149,600 m: 149,600 is the product of speed and frequency. From v = fλ, wavelength is found by division; multiplying does not give units of length.
D. 340 m: 340 m/s is the wave speed, not its wavelength. In one second, 440 cycles pass, so each cycle occupies about 340/440 m.
Key Concept: For a wave, v = f x λ. Sound in air is a longitudinal pressure wave. Sound speed depends on the medium's elastic properties and density, and in gases on temperature; greater density alone does not imply greater speed.

Q8  [phys-8]  Fluid Mechanics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A solid object floats in water with 70% of its volume submerged. What is the density of the object? (Density of water = 1000 kg/m^3)

  A. 300 kg/m^3
  B. 700 kg/m^3   <= key
  C. 1000 kg/m^3
  D. 1400 kg/m^3

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 700 kg/m^3
For an unsupported object floating at rest, buoyant force balances weight. Neglecting air buoyancy and surface tension, the submerged volume fraction equals the object's average density divided by the water density.
Fraction submerged = ρ_object / ρ_fluid
0.70 = ρ_object / 1000
ρ_object = 0.70 x 1000 = 700 kg/m^3

  | Material | Density (kg/m^3) | Floats in Water? |
| Cork | ~120 | Yes (88% above) |
| Wood (oak) | ~700 | Yes (30% above) |
| Ice | ~917 | Yes (8.3% above) |
| Iron | ~7,870 | No (sinks) |
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 300 kg/m^3: 300 kg/m^3 uses the 30% above water. Buoyant force depends on the displaced water, so use the 70% submerged fraction.
C. 1000 kg/m^3: An object with the same average density as water can be neutrally buoyant when fully submerged. The stated object floats with part of its volume above the surface.
D. 1400 kg/m^3: An unsupported object with average density 1400 kg/m^3 is denser than water and would sink under this model, rather than float with 70% submerged.
Key Concept: For an unsupported object floating at rest, neglecting air buoyancy and surface tension, V_submerged/V_total = ρ_object,average/ρ_fluid. A lower average density permits floating; equal density gives neutral buoyancy when fully submerged.

Q9  [phys-9]  Energy  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A 2 kg ball is thrown vertically upward with an initial speed of 10 m/s. What is its kinetic energy at the moment of release? (Ignore air resistance.)

  A. 10 J
  B. 20 J
  C. 50 J
  D. 100 J   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 100 J
Kinetic energy is calculated using:
KE = 1/2 x m x v^2
KE = 1/2 x 2 x 10^2
KE = 1/2 x 2 x 100
KE = 100 J
For the subsequent vertical flight, take g = 10 m/s^2 and gravitational potential energy to be zero at release. At the highest point the speed is zero, so the 100 J has become gravitational potential energy. The rise above release is h = 100/(2 × 10) = 5 m.
| Position | KE | PE | Total Energy |
| Release (v=10 m/s) | 100 J | 0 J | 100 J |
| Halfway to the maximum height | 50 J | 50 J | 100 J |
| Top (v=0) | 0 J | 100 J | 100 J |
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 10 J: 10 is the speed in m/s, not the kinetic energy in joules. Energy requires both mass and the square of speed: 1/2mv^2.
B. 20 J: 20 is the numerical value of mv, which is momentum in kg·m/s. Kinetic energy uses 1/2mv^2 and is 100 J.
C. 50 J: 50 J would be the kinetic energy of a 1 kg object at 10 m/s. The given ball has mass 2 kg.
Key Concept: Kinetic energy is 1/2mv^2, so doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy. During this vertical flight under gravity alone, kinetic energy plus gravitational potential energy stays constant.

Q10  [phys-10]  Pressure  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An ideal hydraulic press has piston areas of 5 cm^2 and 100 cm^2. Neglect friction, piston weights and the height difference between the pistons. If 20 N is applied to the small piston, what output force does the large piston exert?

  A. 1 N
  B. 20 N
  C. 100 N
  D. 400 N   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 400 N
Pascal's principle states that an applied pressure increase is transmitted undiminished through a confined fluid. The pressure increase is ΔP = F_1/A_1, so F_2 = F_1(A_2/A_1) = 20 × (100/5) = 400 N.
Both areas are in the same units, so their ratio is dimensionless. If pressure itself is calculated in pascals, 5 cm^2 = 5 × 10^-4 m^2 and ΔP = 20/(5 × 10^-4) = 4.0 × 10^4 Pa.
For an incompressible fluid, A_1d_1 = A_2d_2. The larger piston therefore moves only one-twentieth as far. In this ideal, lossless press, F_1d_1 = F_2d_2.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 1 N: 1 N results from reversing the area ratio. The larger piston has 20 times the area, so the same pressure increase produces 20 times the force.
B. 20 N: 20 N would be the output if the piston areas were equal. The larger output area multiplies the applied force.
C. 100 N: The force ratio equals 100/5 = 20, not 5. Multiplying the 20 N input by 20 gives 400 N.
Key Concept: An ideal hydraulic press multiplies force by the piston-area ratio: F_2/F_1 = A_2/A_1. The output displacement decreases by the same factor, so the machine does not multiply energy.

Q11  [phys-11]  Electromagnetic Radiation  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In the usual ordering of the electromagnetic spectrum from long to short wavelengths, which of these categories is placed at the shortest-wavelength end?

  A. Radio waves
  B. Visible light
  C. Ultraviolet radiation
  D. Γ rays   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Γ rays
The conventional spectrum ordering places gamma rays at the shortest-wavelength end. In vacuum, shorter wavelength means higher frequency and greater energy per photon.
| EM Radiation Type | Approximate conventional wavelength range | Typical photon energy |
| Radio waves excluding the microwave band shown below | > 1 m | Lowest |
| Microwaves | 1 mm - 1 m | Low |
| Infrared | 700 nm - 1 mm | Medium-low |
| Visible light | 400-700 nm | Medium |
| Ultraviolet | 10-400 nm | Medium-high |
| X-rays | 0.01-10 nm | High |
| Γ rays |  | Highest |
These bands are a learning guide rather than strict boundaries. X-ray and gamma-ray energies can overlap; the names may also distinguish how the radiation is produced.
Key Relationships:
c = f x λ (c = 3 x 10^8 m/s for all EM waves in vacuum)
E = hf = hc/lambda (energy is inversely proportional to wavelength)
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Radio waves: Radio waves occupy the long-wavelength, low-frequency end of the electromagnetic spectrum. They do not have the shortest wavelengths among these choices.
B. Visible light: Visible wavelengths are roughly 400-700 nm. Ultraviolet and the conventional gamma-ray region lie at shorter wavelengths.
C. Ultraviolet radiation: Ultraviolet wavelengths are shorter than visible wavelengths, but the gamma-ray region is conventionally placed at still shorter wavelengths.
Key Concept: All electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light in vacuum. The energy of a photon is E = hf, where h is Planck's constant (6.626 x 10^-34 J.s). Higher frequency = higher energy = shorter wavelength.

Q12  [phys-12]  Nuclear Physics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: During β-minus decay, a neutron in the nucleus is converted into a proton and emits:

  A. An α particle and a γ ray
  B. An electron and an antineutrino   <= key
  C. A positron and a neutrino
  D. Two protons and a neutron

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: An electron and an antineutrino
In β-minus (β^-) decay, a neutron transforms into a proton, emitting a high-energy electron (β particle) and an electron antineutrino to conserve lepton number.
| Decay Type | Process | Emitted Particle(s) | Change in Nucleus |
| Α (α) | Nucleus ejects He-4 nucleus | Α particle (^4_2He) | Z-2, A-4 |
| Β-minus (β^-) | n -> p | Electron + electron antineutrino | Z+1, A unchanged |
| Β-plus (β^+) | p -> n | Positron + electron neutrino | Z-1, A unchanged |
| Γ (γ) | Excited nucleus relaxes | Γ photon | No change in Z or A |
Β-minus decay equation:
^A_ZX → ^A_Z+1Y + e^- + ν̄_e
Example: ^14_6C → ^14_7N + e^- + ν̄_e (carbon-14 dating)
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. An α particle and a γ ray: An alpha particle contains two protons and two neutrons. Alpha emission reduces the daughter's mass number by 4 and atomic number by 2; that is not beta-minus decay.
C. A positron and a neutrino: A positron and an electron neutrino are emitted in beta-plus decay, in which a proton converts to a neutron. Beta-minus decay has the opposite nucleon conversion.
D. Two protons and a neutron: Beta-minus decay converts one neutron into a proton within the nucleus. It does not eject two protons and a neutron as the emitted particles.
Key Concept: In beta-minus decay, a neutron becomes a proton, so the daughter has Z increased by 1 and the same A. Total electric charge is conserved when the emitted electron is included. Nuclear-decay equations conserve total energy, not the sum of rest masses.

Q13  [phys-13]  Work and Power  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A crane raises a 500 kg load by 10 m in 25 s. What is the average useful power associated with increasing the load's gravitational potential energy? (g = 10 m/s^2)

  A. 200 W
  B. 500 W
  C. 2000 W   <= key
  D. 5000 W

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2000 W
Power is the rate of doing work. First calculate the work done, then divide by time:
Work = Force x distance = mgh = 500 x 10 x 10 = 50,000 J
Power = Work / time = 50,000 / 25 = 2000 W = 2 kW

  | Quantity | Formula | SI Unit |
| Work | W = F x d x cos(θ) | Joule (J) |
| Power | P_average = W/Δt; instantaneous P = Fv cosθ | Watt (W = J/s) |
| Kinetic energy | KE = 1/2 mv^2 | Joule (J) |
| Potential energy | PE = mgh, gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface | Joule (J) |
The useful lifting force against gravity is mg = 5000 N. Multiplying it by the average upward speed, 10/25 = 0.4 m/s, also gives the average useful power of 2000 W.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 200 W: 200 W would supply only 5000 J in 25 s. Raising this load requires mgh = 50,000 J.
B. 500 W: The mass is 500 kg, but mass is not power. Divide the 50,000 J increase in gravitational potential energy by 25 s.
D. 5000 W: 5000 N is the load's weight. Power also depends on lifting speed; using the average speed 10/25 = 0.4 m/s gives 2000 W.
Key Concept: Average power is work divided by elapsed time; 1 W = 1 J/s. One mechanical horsepower is approximately 746 W. Holding a load stationary does no mechanical work on that load, although supporting muscles still consume metabolic energy.

Q14  [phys-14]  Momentum and Collisions  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A 1000 kg car traveling at 20 m/s collides with a stationary 2000 kg truck, and they stick together. Neglect external impulse during the collision. What is their common speed immediately afterward?

  A. 5.0 m/s
  B. 6.67 m/s   <= key
  C. 10 m/s
  D. 20 m/s

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 6.67 m/s
This is a perfectly inelastic collision (objects stick together). Conservation of momentum applies:
m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v_f
1000 x 20 + 2000 x 0 = (1000 + 2000) x v_f
20,000 = 3000 x v_f
v_f = 20,000 / 3000 = 6.67 m/s
They move in the car's original direction.
For a system with negligible external impulse:
| Collision Type | Momentum Conserved? | Kinetic Energy Conserved? |
| Elastic | Yes | Yes |

STEM: A charged particle moving through a uniform magnetic field experiences a force. In which direction is this magnetic force relative to the particle's velocity?

  A. Parallel to the velocity (same direction)
  B. Antiparallel to the velocity (opposite direction)
  C. Perpendicular to both the velocity and the magnetic field   <= key
  D. At a 45-degree angle to the velocity

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Perpendicular to both the velocity and the magnetic field
For a moving point charge, the magnetic force is F_B = q(v × B). When nonzero, it is perpendicular to both v and B. Its magnitude is |F_B| = |q|vB sinθ, where θ is the angle between v and B.
| Property | Electric force | Magnetic force |
| Direction | Along E for a positive charge; opposite E for a negative charge | Perpendicular to v and B when nonzero |
| Acts on a stationary charge? | Yes, if E is nonzero | No |
| Does work on the charge? | Can do work | Does no work |
| Changes speed? | Can change speed | Does not change speed by itself |

Why the other choices are wrong:

B. Antiparallel to the velocity (opposite direction): A force opposite to velocity would do negative work and reduce kinetic energy. The magnetic part of the Lorentz force does no work.
D. At a 45-degree angle to the velocity: A nonzero magnetic force is at 90° to velocity, not 45°. Its direction follows the cross product q(v × B).
Key Concept: Magnetic force does no work on a moving point charge and therefore does not change its speed by itself. Use the right-hand rule for v × B for a positive charge; reverse that direction for a negative charge.

Then 1/f = 1/u + 1/v gives 1/v = 1/20 − 1/30 = 1/60, so v = +60 cm on the opposite side of the lens. The lateral magnification is m = −v/u = −60/30 = −2. The image is inverted and twice the object's height.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. v = 40 cm, m = -1: At an object distance of 30 cm, the lens equation gives an image distance of 60 cm. Equal-size imaging, m = −1, would occur at object distance 2f = 40 cm.
C. v = 60 cm, m = +2: The image distance and magnification magnitude are correct, but the sign is not. A real image from this converging lens is inverted, so m is negative.
D. v = 40 cm, m = -2: The image distance is incorrect and the pair is inconsistent: with u = 30 cm and v = 40 cm, m would be −40/30, not −2.
Key Concept: When an object is between f and 2f of a converging lens, the image is real, inverted, and magnified.

STEM: A concave spherical mirror has a radius of curvature of 40 cm. An object is on its axis, 15 cm in front of the mirror. Using the paraxial approximation, where is the image formed?

  D. 30 cm in front of the mirror

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: C) 60 cm behind the mirror

The mirror equation gives 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/20 − 1/15 = −1/60, so v = −60 cm. A negative image distance in this convention means a virtual image behind the mirror. Its magnification is m = −v/u = +4, so it is upright and enlarged.

STEM: A compound microscope uses a 5 mm focal-length objective and a 25 mm focal-length eyepiece. It is adjusted for a final image at infinity, so the eye is relaxed. The optical tube length L, measured from the objective's rear focal plane to the eyepiece's front focal plane, is 160 mm. Using a reference near-point distance D = 250 mm, what is the magnitude of its total angular magnification?

  A. 100x
  B. 200x
  C. 320x   <= key
  D. 640x

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: C) 320x
For this thin-lens, relaxed-eye model, the magnitude of the objective's linear magnification is L/f_obj = 160/5 = 32. The eyepiece's angular magnification is D/f_eye = 250/25 = 10. Their product gives |M| = 32 × 10 = 320.
L here is the stated focal-plane separation, not the physical distance between the lenses. The 250 mm near point is the reference distance for comparison with unaided viewing; the final image in this question is at infinity.

D. 640x: 640× is twice the magnification. No additional factor of two appears when multiplying the objective magnification by the eyepiece's angular magnification.

Q58  [phys-58]  Geometric Optics Advanced  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A ray passes from air (n ≈ 1.0) into a glass slab (n = 1.5) at an incidence angle of 30° measured from the normal. What is the refraction angle inside the glass?

  A. 14.5 degrees
  B. 19.5 degrees   <= key
  C. 22.0 degrees
  D. 33.3 degrees

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: B) 19.5 degrees
By Snell's law: n_1 sin θ_1 = n_2 sin θ_2. 1.0 x sin 30 = 1.5 x sin θ_2. sin θ_2 = 0.5 / 1.5 = 0.333. θ_2 = arcsin(0.333) = 19.47 degrees, approximately 19.5 degrees.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 14.5 degrees: 14.5° is too small: 1.5 sin(14.5°) is about 0.376, not the required sin(30°) = 0.5.
C. 22.0 degrees: 22.0° gives 1.5 sin(22.0°) ≈ 0.562. Snell's law requires this product to equal 0.5.
D. 33.3 degrees: 33.3° would bend the ray away from the normal. Entering higher-index glass from air bends this obliquely incident ray toward the normal.
Key Concept: Snell's law is n_1sinθ_1 = n_2sinθ_2, with both angles measured from the normal. At oblique incidence, light bends toward the normal when it enters a higher-refractive-index medium.

Q59  [phys-59]  Geometric Optics Advanced  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: For light traveling from glass into air (n_air ≈ 1.0), the critical angle measured from the normal is 42°. What is the refractive index of the glass?

  A. 1.33
  B. 1.41
  C. 1.49   <= key
  D. 1.62

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: C) 1.49
At the critical angle: sin θ_c = n_air / n_glass. Therefore n_glass = n_air / sin θ_c = 1.0 / sin 42 = 1.0 / 0.6691 = 1.494, approximately 1.49.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 1.33: A refractive index of 1.33 would give a critical angle of about 48.8° for transmission into air, rather than 42°.
B. 1.41: A refractive index of 1.41 would give a critical angle of about 45.2°. The stated 42° requires a larger index.
D. 1.62: A refractive index of 1.62 would give a critical angle of about 38.1°. Use n_glass = 1/sin42° ≈ 1.49.
Key Concept: Total internal reflection requires light to travel from higher to lower refractive index with incidence angle greater than θ_c. The critical angle satisfies sinθ_c = n_lower/n_higher. This principle is used in optical fibers.

Q60  [phys-60]  SI System & Units  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which of the following is a base SI unit?

  A. Newton
  B. Joule
  C. Ampere   <= key
  D. Watt

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Ampere
The seven base SI units are: meter (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), ampere (A), kelvin (K), mole (mol), and candela (cd). Newton (force), Joule (energy), and Watt (power) are all derived units.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Newton: The newton is the derived SI unit of force: 1 N = 1 kg·m·s^-2. It is not one of the seven base units.
B. Joule: The joule is the derived SI unit of energy: 1 J = 1 kg·m^2·s^-2.
D. Watt: The watt is the derived SI unit of power: 1 W = 1 J/s = 1 kg·m^2·s^-3.
Key Concept: The SI classifies the ampere as a base unit for electric current. The seven base units are m, kg, s, A, K, mol and cd; derived SI units are formed from their products and powers.
