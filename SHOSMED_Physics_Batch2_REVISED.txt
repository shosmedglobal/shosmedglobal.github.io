Q1  [phys-61]  SI System & Units  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A nanoparticle has a diameter of 50 nm. What is this diameter in meters?

  A. 5 × 10^−7 m
  B. 5 × 10^−8 m   <= key
  C. 5 × 10^−9 m
  D. 5 × 10^−6 m

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 5 × 10^−8 m
The prefix 'nano' means 10^−9. Therefore: 50 nm = 50 × 10^−9 m = 5 × 10^−8 m.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 5 × 10^−7 m: This is ten times the correct diameter: 5 × 10^−7 m equals 500 nm. The prefix nano means 10^−9, so 50 nm must first be written as 50 × 10^−9 m.
C. 5 × 10^−9 m: This equals 5 nm, not 50 nm. When 50 × 10^−9 is written with a coefficient of 5, the exponent increases by one to −8.
D. 5 × 10^−6 m: This equals 5000 nm, or 5 μm. It is 100 times larger than the stated diameter.
Key Concept: nano = 10^−9, micro = 10^−6, milli = 10^−3, kilo = 10^3.

Q2  [phys-62]  SI System & Units  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which of the following quantities is a vector?

  A. Temperature
  B. Mass
  C. Displacement   <= key
  D. Speed

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Displacement
Displacement has both magnitude and direction, making it a vector quantity. Temperature, mass, and speed are all scalar quantities - they have magnitude only.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Temperature: Temperature is a scalar: it describes a thermal state without a spatial direction. A temperature gradient has a direction, but temperature itself does not.
B. Mass: Mass is a scalar amount of matter or measure of inertia. A value such as 2 kg does not need a direction.
D. Speed: Speed is the magnitude of velocity and has no direction. Velocity is a vector, but speed alone is a scalar.
Key Concept: Vectors have both magnitude and direction; scalars have magnitude only.

Q3  [phys-63]  SI System & Units  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Using dimensional analysis, determine which expression could represent pressure (SI unit: Pa = kg·m^−1·s^−2).

  A. Force × Area
  B. Force / Area   <= key
  C. Force × Velocity
  D. Energy × Volume

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Force / Area
Pressure = Force / Area. Dimensionally: [kg·m·s^−2] / [m^2] = kg·m^−1·s^−2, which matches the pascal (Pa).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Force × Area: Multiplying force by area gives kg·m^3·s^−2. Pressure instead divides the normal force by the area over which it acts.
C. Force × Velocity: Force multiplied by velocity has dimensions kg·m^2·s^−3, the dimensions of power. It does not have the dimensions of pressure.
D. Energy × Volume: Energy multiplied by volume has dimensions kg·m^5·s^−2. Energy divided by volume would have pressure dimensions, but that is not the operation given here.
Key Concept: A valid physical equation must have matching dimensions on both sides. Dimensional analysis can reject an inconsistent expression, but cannot by itself prove that a formula is correct.

Q4  [phys-64]  SI System & Units  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: How many microliters (μL) are in 0.25 mL?

  A. 25 μL
  B. 250 μL   <= key
  C. 2500 μL
  D. 0.025 μL

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 250 μL
1 mL = 1000 μL. Therefore 0.25 mL = 0.25 × 1000 = 250 μL.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 25 μL: This uses a conversion factor of 100 instead of 1000. Milli is 10^−3 and micro is 10^−6, so one milliliter contains 10^3 microliters.
C. 2500 μL: This is ten times too large. Multiplying 0.25 by 1000 gives 250, not 2500.
D. 0.025 μL: A microliter is smaller than a milliliter, so the numerical value increases when the same volume is expressed in microliters.
Key Concept: milli = 10^−3, micro = 10^−6; 1 mL = 10^3 μL.

Q5  [phys-65]  SI System & Units  correct answer: A
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: The SI unit of energy (Joule) can be expressed as:

  A. kg·m^2·s^−2   <= key
  B. kg·m·s^−2
  C. kg·m^2·s^−1
  D. kg·m^−1·s^−2

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: kg·m^2·s^−2
For a constant force parallel to a displacement, the work is W = Fd. Therefore 1 J = 1 N·m = (1 kg·m·s^−2)(1 m) = 1 kg·m^2·s^−2. Work transfers energy, so the joule is also the SI unit of energy.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. kg·m·s^−2: This is the newton, the unit of force. Multiplying a force by a distance adds another factor of meters and gives the joule.
C. kg·m^2·s^−1: This has one fewer factor of inverse seconds than energy. It is the unit of angular momentum or action, equivalent to J·s.
D. kg·m^−1·s^−2: This is the pascal, the unit of pressure: one newton per square meter. Energy and pressure therefore have different dimensions.
Key Concept: 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m^2·s^−2.

Q6  [phys-66]  Kinematics  correct answer: A
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A car travels 120 km in 1.5 hours, then 80 km in 1 hour. What is the average speed for the entire trip?

  A. 80 km/h   <= key
  B. 100 km/h
  C. 90 km/h
  D. 75 km/h

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 80 km/h
Average speed = total distance / total time = (120 + 80) / (1.5 + 1) = 200 / 2.5 = 80 km/h.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. 100 km/h: This would result from dividing 200 km by 2 hours, but the journey takes 2.5 hours. Include the full time for both legs.
C. 90 km/h: Both legs have an average speed of 80 km/h: 120/1.5 = 80 and 80/1 = 80. Combining them cannot produce 90 km/h without changing the stated distance or time.
D. 75 km/h: At 75 km/h, covering 200 km would take about 2.67 hours. The stated elapsed time is 2.5 hours, giving 80 km/h.
Key Concept: Average speed is total distance divided by total elapsed time. It is a time-weighted average of the speeds; a simple arithmetic mean is not valid in general.

Q7  [phys-67]  Kinematics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An object starts from rest and moves in a straight line with a constant acceleration of 4 m/s². What is its speed after 5 seconds?

  A. 10 m/s
  B. 15 m/s
  C. 20 m/s   <= key
  D. 25 m/s

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 20 m/s
Using v = v_0 + at = 0 + 4 × 5 = 20 m/s.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 10 m/s: This is the average speed during the five seconds, (0 + 20)/2, rather than the final speed. The final speed increases by 4 m/s each second.
B. 15 m/s: A final speed of 15 m/s after 5 s would require an acceleration of 3 m/s² from rest. The stated acceleration is 4 m/s².
D. 25 m/s: A final speed of 25 m/s after 5 s would require 5 m/s² from rest. Use v = at with the stated acceleration.
Key Concept: For uniform acceleration from rest, v = at.

Q8  [phys-68]  Kinematics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A ball is released from rest 45 m above the ground. Ignoring air resistance, how long does it take to reach the ground? (g = 10 m/s²)

  A. 2 s
  B. 3 s   <= key
  C. 4 s
  D. 4.5 s

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 3 s
Using h = ½gt²: 45 = ½ × 10 × t² → t² = 9 → t = 3 s.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 2 s: In 2 s, a ball released from rest falls only ½ × 10 × 2² = 20 m. It has not yet covered the stated 45 m.
C. 4 s: In 4 s, the free-fall distance would be 80 m. Solving 45 = 5t² gives t = 3 s.
D. 4.5 s: Dividing 45 by 10 does not give the fall time: acceleration is not a constant speed. Distance from rest grows with t², so a square root is needed.
Key Concept: For free fall from rest, h = ½gt², so t = √(2h/g).

Q9  [phys-69]  Kinematics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A stone is thrown vertically upward at 30 m/s. Ignoring air resistance, what is its maximum rise above the release point? (g = 10 m/s²)

  A. 30 m
  B. 45 m   <= key
  C. 60 m
  D. 90 m

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 45 m
At maximum height, v = 0. Using v² = v_0² − 2gh: 0 = 900 − 20h → h = 45 m.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 30 m: The rise is determined by v_0²/(2g), not by assigning the same numerical value to speed and height. The units and the factor of 2g both matter.
C. 60 m: Using an average upward speed of 15 m/s over the 3 s ascent gives 45 m, not 60 m. Gravity steadily reduces the upward speed.
D. 90 m: This comes from v_0²/g and misses the factor of 2. The correct relation is 0 = v_0² − 2gh.
Key Concept: For a vertical throw without air resistance, the speed is momentarily zero at the highest point. The rise is h = v_0²/(2g); gravity still acts at the top.

Q10  [phys-70]  Kinematics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A projectile is launched at 40 m/s, at 45° above the horizontal, and lands at the same height as its launch point. Ignoring air resistance, what is its horizontal range? (g = 10 m/s²)

  A. 80 m
  B. 120 m
  C. 160 m   <= key
  D. 200 m

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 160 m
Range R = v_0²sin(2θ)/g = (40)²×sin(90°)/10 = 1600×1/10 = 160 m.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 80 m: This is half the full range and corresponds to the horizontal distance reached at the highest point. The projectile then travels an equal horizontal distance while descending.
B. 120 m: The range depends on sin(2θ), which is sin 90° = 1 here. Substitution gives 1600/10 = 160 m.
D. 200 m: With the stated speed, 45° already gives the maximum range in this model, 160 m. A range of 200 m would require a larger launch speed.
Key Concept: For launch and landing at the same height without air resistance, R = v_0²sin(2θ)/g. At a fixed launch speed, this range is greatest at θ = 45°.

Q11  [phys-71]  Kinematics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A wheel rotates at 600 revolutions per minute. What is the period of rotation?

  A. 0.01 s
  B. 0.1 s   <= key
  C. 0.5 s
  D. 1 s

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 0.1 s
Frequency f = 600 rpm = 600/60 = 10 Hz. Period T = 1/f = 1/10 = 0.1 s.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0.01 s: A period of 0.01 s means 100 revolutions per second, or 6000 rpm. The stated wheel rotates at one tenth of that frequency.
C. 0.5 s: A period of 0.5 s means 2 revolutions per second, or 120 rpm. Convert 600 rpm to 10 Hz before taking its reciprocal.
D. 1 s: A period of 1 s means 1 revolution per second, or 60 rpm. At 600 rpm, each revolution lasts only 0.1 s.
Key Concept: T = 1/f; convert rpm to Hz by dividing by 60.

Q12  [phys-72]  Kinematics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An object moves in a circle of radius 2 m at a constant speed of 6 m/s. What is the centripetal acceleration?

  A. 3 m/s²
  B. 12 m/s²
  C. 18 m/s²   <= key
  D. 36 m/s²

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 18 m/s²
Centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r = 36/2 = 18 m/s².
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 3 m/s²: Dividing v by r gives 3 s^−1, the angular speed, not an acceleration. Centripetal acceleration requires v²/r.
B. 12 m/s²: Multiplying speed by radius gives 12 m²/s, which does not have acceleration units. Square the speed and divide by the radius instead.
D. 36 m/s²: This is the numerical value of v² before division by r. Dividing 36 m²/s² by 2 m gives 18 m/s².
Key Concept: Centripetal acceleration = v²/r, always directed toward the center.

Q13  [phys-73]  Kinematics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A projectile is launched horizontally at 15 m/s from a cliff 80 m above level ground. Ignoring air resistance, how far horizontally from the point directly below the launch does it land? (g = 10 m/s²)

  A. 30 m
  B. 45 m
  C. 60 m   <= key
  D. 75 m

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 60 m
Time to fall: h = ½gt² → 80 = 5t² → t = 4 s. Horizontal distance: x = v_0t = 15 × 4 = 60 m.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 30 m: This would require a flight time of 2 s. A fall from rest through 80 m takes 4 s, so the projectile travels twice as far horizontally.
B. 45 m: This would require a flight time of 3 s, during which the vertical drop would be only 45 m. Use the full 80 m drop to find the time.
D. 75 m: This corresponds to a 5 s flight, but in 5 s the vertical drop would be 125 m. The projectile reaches the ground after 4 s.
Key Concept: Without air resistance, horizontal velocity remains constant while vertical velocity changes under gravity. Find the fall time from the vertical motion, then use that same time in x = v_xt.

Q14  [phys-74]  Kinematics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A body undergoes uniform acceleration and covers 20 m in the 3rd second. If it started from rest, what is the acceleration?

  A. 6 m/s²
  B. 8 m/s²   <= key
  C. 10 m/s²
  D. 12 m/s²

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 8 m/s²
The third second is the interval from t = 2 s to t = 3 s. From rest with constant acceleration, x(t) = ½at². Therefore x(3 s) − x(2 s) = ½a[(3 s)² − (2 s)²] = (2.5 s²)a = 20 m, so a = 8 m/s². This is the distance during the third second, not the total distance during the first three seconds.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 6 m/s²: With a = 6 m/s², the distance during the third second would be (2.5 s²)(6 m/s²) = 15 m, rather than 20 m.
C. 10 m/s²: With a = 10 m/s², the third-second distance would be 25 m. Calculate the difference between the positions at 3 s and 2 s.
D. 12 m/s²: With a = 12 m/s², the third-second distance would be 30 m. The required acceleration is 20 m divided by 2.5 s².
Key Concept: Displacement during a time interval is final position minus initial position. If motion stays in one direction, the magnitude of that displacement is the distance traveled. The third second runs from 2 s to 3 s.

Q15  [phys-75]  Kinematics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: The angular velocity of a rotating disc is 4π rad/s. What is the frequency of rotation?

  A. 1 Hz
  B. 2 Hz   <= key
  C. 4 Hz
  D. 0.5 Hz

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2 Hz
ω = 2πf → f = ω/(2π) = 4π/(2π) = 2 Hz.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 1 Hz: One revolution per second corresponds to 2π rad/s. The disc turns through 4π radians each second, equivalent to two complete revolutions.
C. 4 Hz: This divides the angular speed by π instead of 2π. A full revolution contains 2π radians.
D. 0.5 Hz: The value 0.5 s is the period of this rotation, not its frequency. Frequency is the reciprocal of period, giving 2 Hz.
Key Concept: Angular velocity ω = 2πf, where f is frequency in Hz.

Q17  [phys-77]  Dynamics  correct answer: B

STEM: A 5 kg object is accelerated at 3 m/s². What net force acts on it?

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 15 N
By Newton's second law: F = ma = 5 × 3 = 15 N.
Why the other choices are wrong:

D. 20 N: A net force of 20 N on 5 kg would produce an acceleration of 4 m/s². The stated acceleration is 3 m/s², requiring 15 N.
Key Concept: F = ma; force is measured in Newtons (kg·m/s²).

Q18  [phys-78]  Dynamics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A 60 kg person stands in an elevator accelerating upward at 2 m/s². What is the apparent weight (normal force)? (g = 10 m/s²)

  A. 600 N
  B. 720 N   <= key
  C. 480 N
  D. 660 N

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 720 N
Take upward as positive. The forces on the person are the upward normal force N and the downward gravitational force mg. Newton's second law gives N − mg = ma, so N = 60(10 + 2) = 720 N. The gravitational weight remains 600 N; the larger normal force is the apparent weight measured by a scale.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 600 N: This is mg, the gravitational weight. It would also equal the apparent weight if the elevator had zero acceleration, but here an extra upward net force is required.
C. 480 N: This is m(g − 2), the apparent weight for a downward acceleration of 2 m/s². The question specifies upward acceleration.
D. 660 N: This would give a net upward force of only 60 N and an acceleration of 1 m/s². The stated 2 m/s² requires a net force of 120 N.
Key Concept: With upward defined as positive, N = m(g + a). Apparent weight depends on the direction of acceleration, not simply on whether the elevator is moving up or down.

Q19  [phys-79]  Dynamics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A 10 kg block slides on a level horizontal surface with coefficient of kinetic friction μ_k = 0.3. What horizontal pulling force maintains a constant velocity? Ignore other resistance. (g = 10 m/s²)

  A. 10 N
  B. 20 N
  C. 30 N   <= key
  D. 40 N

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 30 N
At constant velocity, applied force equals friction: F = μ_kmg = 0.3 × 10 × 10 = 30 N.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 10 N: The kinetic friction is 0.3 × 100 = 30 N. A 10 N pull would leave a net force opposite the motion and slow the block.
B. 20 N: A 20 N pull is smaller than the 30 N kinetic friction. The block would slow rather than maintain its velocity.
D. 40 N: A 40 N pull exceeds friction by 10 N, so the 10 kg block would accelerate at 1 m/s² in the pulling direction.
Key Concept: Constant velocity requires zero net force. Here the horizontal pull balances kinetic friction, and the normal force is mg because there is no vertical acceleration or vertical component of the pull.

  D. Must be contact forces

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Act on different objects
The two forces in a Newton's third-law pair act on different objects. The force of A on B acts on B, while the equal and opposite force of B on A acts on A. D. Must be contact forces: Third-law pairs also occur for noncontact interactions, such as the gravitational attraction between Earth and the Moon. Contact is not required.
Key Concept: Third-law pairs act on different objects. They do not cancel on either object separately, but they cancel as internal forces when both objects are included in one system.

Q51  [phys-111]  Heat & Thermodynamics  correct answer: C

STEM: According to the first law of thermodynamics, if 500 J of heat is added to a gas and the gas does 200 J of work, what is the change in internal energy?

  A. 700 J
  B. 500 J
  C. 300 J   <= key
  D. 200 J

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 300 J
Use ΔU = Q − W_by, where Q is positive for heat added to the gas and W_by is positive for work done by the gas. Here Q = 500 J and W_by = 200 J, so ΔU = 500 − 200 = 300 J. Part of the incoming energy leaves as work; the remainder increases internal energy.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 700 J: Adding 500 and 200 would be appropriate if 200 J of work were done on the gas. Here the gas does the work, transferring that energy out.
B. 500 J: This ignores the 200 J transferred out as work. Only 300 J of the supplied heat remains as an increase in internal energy.
D. 200 J: This is the work done by the gas, not its internal-energy change. Apply the complete energy balance, 500 − 200.
Key Concept: With Q positive into the system and W_by positive for work done by it, the first law is ΔU = Q − W_by. Always identify the work sign convention before substituting.

Q53  [phys-113]  Heat & Thermodynamics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In an isothermal process for a fixed amount of an ideal gas:

  A. Temperature changes while volume stays constant
  B. Pressure stays constant
  C. Internal energy remains constant   <= key
  D. No heat is exchanged

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Internal energy remains constant
Isothermal means constant temperature. For a fixed amount of an ideal gas, internal energy depends only on temperature, so ΔU = 0. This does not mean that no heat is transferred: when the gas does work, an equal amount of heat must enter to maintain constant temperature, using ΔU = Q − W_by.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Temperature changes while volume stays constant: Isothermal means that temperature stays constant. Constant volume is called isochoric and does not by itself prevent temperature from changing.
B. Pressure stays constant: Constant pressure is called isobaric. In an isothermal change for a fixed amount of ideal gas, pressure changes inversely with volume.
D. No heat is exchanged: No heat exchange defines an adiabatic process. An isothermal gas can exchange heat to balance the work it does or receives.
Key Concept: For a fixed amount of ideal gas, constant temperature means constant internal energy. An isothermal process is not necessarily adiabatic.

Q54  [phys-114]  Heat & Thermodynamics  correct answer: C   (option C restated as nondecrease)

STEM: Which statement most directly expresses the second law of thermodynamics for an isolated macroscopic system?

  A. Energy can be created in an isolated system
  B. Heat spontaneously flows from cold to hot objects
  C. The entropy of an isolated system cannot decrease   <= key
  D. Perpetual motion machines of the first kind are impossible

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The entropy of an isolated system cannot decrease
The second law states that the entropy of an isolated macroscopic system cannot decrease. It increases in irreversible processes and remains constant in the reversible idealization. Spontaneous heat transfer from a hotter object to a colder one is consistent with this law; transferring heat in the opposite direction requires an additional process, such as work supplied to a refrigerator.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Energy can be created in an isolated system: Creation of energy in an isolated system would violate the first law, which expresses energy conservation. The second law additionally restricts which energy transfers can occur spontaneously.
B. Heat spontaneously flows from cold to hot objects: Heat does not spontaneously flow from a colder object to a hotter one. Refrigerators move heat in this direction by using work and producing a compensating entropy increase elsewhere.
D. Perpetual motion machines of the first kind are impossible: This is a true consequence of the first law, because a perpetual-motion machine of the first kind would create energy. It is not the direct second-law statement requested here.
Key Concept: The entropy of an isolated system cannot decrease: it increases for irreversible processes and is unchanged for reversible ones. Heat does not flow spontaneously from cold to hot.

Q57  [phys-117]  Heat & Thermodynamics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In an adiabatic process:

  A. Heat is exchanged but no work is done
  B. No heat is exchanged with the surroundings   <= key
  C. Temperature remains constant
  D. Volume remains constant

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: No heat is exchanged with the surroundings
Adiabatic means that no heat crosses the system boundary, so Q = 0. For a closed gas exchanging energy only as heat and work, ΔU = −W_by. If an ideal gas expands and does work, its internal energy and temperature fall; if work is done on it in compression, they rise. However, an ideal gas expanding freely into a vacuum does no work, so in an adiabatic free expansion its temperature stays unchanged.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Heat is exchanged but no work is done: An adiabatic process has no heat exchange. Work can still cross the boundary and change the gas's internal energy.
C. Temperature remains constant: Constant temperature defines an isothermal process. Some adiabatic processes keep temperature constant, but many do not; constant temperature is not the defining condition.
D. Volume remains constant: Constant volume defines an isochoric process. Adiabatic expansion and compression can both occur, so volume need not remain fixed.
Key Concept: Adiabatic means no heat transfer, not constant temperature or no work. For an ideal gas, the temperature change depends on the work exchanged.

Key Concept: At height h above a spherical Earth, distance from the center is R + h and g(h) = g[R/(R + h)]². Reducing g to one-quarter requires doubling the center-to-object distance, so h = R.
