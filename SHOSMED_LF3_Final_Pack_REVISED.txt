STEM: Which of the following is a covalent network solid in which every atom is bonded to its neighbors by strong covalent bonds in a continuous 3D lattice?

  A. Iodine (I₂)
  B. Diamond (C)   <= key
  C. Iron (Fe)
  D. Solid carbon dioxide (CO₂)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Diamond (C)
Diamond is a covalent network solid. Each carbon atom is sp³-hybridized and covalently bonded to four neighboring carbon atoms in a tetrahedral arrangement. These strong bonds extend through a continuous three-dimensional network rather than forming separate molecules. This structure explains diamond's great hardness.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Iodine (I₂): Iodine consists of separate I₂ molecules. Each molecule contains a covalent I–I bond, but neighboring molecules are held together mainly by London dispersion forces. It is a molecular solid, not a continuous covalent network.
C. Iron (Fe): Iron is a metallic solid. Its bonding involves positive atomic cores and delocalized electrons rather than the tetrahedral carbon-carbon network found in diamond.
D. Solid carbon dioxide (CO₂): Solid carbon dioxide contains separate CO₂ molecules. The bonds within each molecule are covalent, but the attractions between molecules do not form a continuous covalent network.

A2  [chem-479]  chemistry  correct answer: A   (explanation rebuilt)

STEM: How many atoms are effectively contained within a simple cubic unit cell?

  A. 1   <= key
  B. 2
  C. 4
  D. 8

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 1
A simple cubic unit cell has 8 atoms positioned at its 8 corners. Each corner atom is shared among 8 adjacent unit cells, so it contributes only 1/8 of an atom to any given cell. Total = 8 corners × 1/8 = 1 atom per unit cell. Among the simple cubic, BCC and FCC models for identical spheres, simple cubic has the lowest packing fraction, about 52%.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. 2: Two atoms per conventional cell is the BCC count: one from the shared corners plus one at the body center. A simple cubic cell has no body-center atom.
C. 4: Four atoms per conventional cell is the FCC count. A simple cubic cell has only corner atoms, whose shared contributions total one atom.
D. 8: Eight counts the corner positions without accounting for sharing. Each corner contributes only one-eighth to this cell, so eight corners contribute one atom in total.

A3  [chem-480]  chemistry  correct answer: B   (explanation rebuilt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A body-centered cubic (BCC) unit cell contains how many atoms?

  A. 1
  B. 2   <= key
  C. 4
  D. 6

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2
A BCC unit cell has 8 atoms at the corners (each shared with 8 cells, contributing 8 × 1/8 = 1 atom) plus 1 atom at the body center entirely inside the cell (contributing 1 full atom). Total = 1 + 1 = 2 atoms per unit cell. Examples include alpha-iron, chromium, and tungsten. Packing fraction is ~68%.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 1: One counts the combined contribution of the corners but omits the atom entirely inside the body center.
C. 4: Four is the atom count for a conventional FCC cell. In BCC, the shared corners contribute one atom and the body center contributes one, giving two.
D. 6: Six is the coordination number of simple cubic packing, not the number of atoms in a BCC cell. BCC contains two atoms per conventional unit cell.

A4  [chem-481]  chemistry  correct answer: D   (explanation rebuilt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: What is the coordination number of an atom in a face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal structure?

  A. 4
  B. 6
  C. 8
  D. 12   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 12
In FCC packing each atom is in direct contact with 12 nearest neighbors: 4 in its own plane, 4 in the plane above, and 4 in the plane below. This is the maximum possible coordination number for identical spheres and is shared with hexagonal close-packed (HCP). FCC metals (e.g., Cu, Al, Au, Ag, Ni) achieve 74% packing efficiency, the highest possible for equal spheres.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 4: Four is the number of atoms effectively contained in a conventional FCC unit cell. Coordination number instead counts the nearest neighbors of one atom, which is twelve.
B. 6: Six is the coordination number of simple cubic packing. FCC packing places twelve nearest neighbors around each atom.
C. 8: Eight is the coordination number of BCC packing. FCC has twelve nearest neighbors, so its coordination number is larger.

A5  [chem-482]  chemistry  correct answer: B   (explanation rebuilt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Diamond and graphite are both made of pure carbon but have very different physical properties. They are best described as:

  A. Isomers
  B. Allotropes   <= key
  C. Isotopes
  D. Polymorphs of an alloy

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Allotropes
Allotropes are different structural forms of the same element. Diamond uses sp³ hybridization in a 3D tetrahedral network, making it hard, transparent, and an electrical insulator. Graphite uses sp² hybridization in stacked hexagonal layers held together by weak London forces, making it soft, opaque, and electrically conductive along the layers. Other carbon allotropes include fullerenes, carbon nanotubes, and graphene.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Isomers: Isomers have the same molecular formula but different structures. For the distinct structural forms of elemental carbon described here, the appropriate term is allotropes.
C. Isotopes: Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. Diamond and graphite differ in bonding and crystal structure, not in the defining neutron count of their carbon atoms.
D. Polymorphs of an alloy: An alloy is a metallic material containing two or more elements. Diamond and graphite are forms of pure carbon. They can also be described as different crystalline forms of carbon, but they are not polymorphs of an alloy.

A6  [chem-483]  chemistry  correct answer: B   (explanation rebuilt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which type of crystalline solid typically has the LOWEST melting point?

  A. Ionic crystal
  B. Molecular crystal   <= key
  C. Covalent network solid
  D. Metallic crystal

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Molecular crystal
Molecular crystals contain separate molecules held together by intermolecular attractions such as London dispersion forces, dipole-dipole interactions or hydrogen bonds. Melting disrupts their ordered arrangement without normally breaking the covalent bonds within the molecules. Molecular solids therefore often have relatively low melting points. This is a general trend, not an absolute ranking: metallic melting points vary widely, and some metals melt near or below room temperature.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Ionic crystal: Ionic crystals are held together by electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions. These attractions often give them relatively high melting points compared with molecular solids.
C. Covalent network solid: Covalent network solids have strong covalent bonds extending through their structures. Disrupting such a network generally requires substantial energy; they are not the class typically associated with low melting points.
D. Metallic crystal: Metallic solids have a wide range of melting points because metallic bonding varies between substances. Low-melting metals are exceptions to a simple ranking, but molecular solids are the intended general trend in this question.

A7  [chem-484]  chemistry  correct answer: C   (explanation rebuilt)

STEM: Which of the following substances has no stable liquid phase at 1 atm and therefore passes directly from solid to gas when heated at that pressure?

  A. Water (H₂O)
  B. Sodium chloride (NaCl)
  C. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — "dry ice"   <= key
  D. Iron (Fe)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — "dry ice"
Solid CO₂, or dry ice, sublimes at about −78.5 °C at 1 atm. Its triple-point pressure is about 5.1 atm, so liquid CO₂ is not a stable phase at 1 atm. Other solids, including ice, can also lose material by sublimation. The decisive distinction in this question is the absence of a stable liquid phase at the stated pressure.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Water (H₂O): Ice can sublime into sufficiently dry air, including at atmospheric pressure. However, water also has a stable liquid phase at 1 atm, so it does not satisfy the condition in the stem.
B. Sodium chloride (NaCl): Sodium chloride has a stable liquid phase at 1 atm and can melt when heated sufficiently. It does not share CO₂'s absence of a stable liquid phase at this pressure.
D. Iron (Fe): Iron can form a stable liquid at 1 atm when heated to a sufficiently high temperature. The question asks for a substance that cannot form a stable liquid at that pressure.

A8  [chem-485]  chemistry  correct answer: B   (explanation rebuilt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Why are most metallic solids good conductors of electricity in the solid state?

  A. Their lattice contains free-moving anions
  B. They have a "sea" of delocalized electrons that move freely throughout the lattice   <= key
  C. Their cations vibrate and physically carry charge
  D. They contain hydrogen bonds that conduct electrons

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: They have a "sea" of delocalized electrons that move freely throughout the lattice
In the metallic-bond model, positive atomic cores are surrounded by delocalized valence electrons. An applied electric field produces a net drift of these electrons through the solid, carrying current without requiring the atomic cores to move through the crystal. In an ordinary ionic crystal such as NaCl, the ions are not freely mobile; they carry current when the salt is molten or dissolved in water. Conductivity depends on available mobile charge carriers, so graphite can also conduct despite being a covalent network material.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Their lattice contains free-moving anions: The mobile charge carriers in an ordinary metal are delocalized electrons, not free-moving negative ions. A metal does not contain the alternating cation-anion lattice found in an ionic salt.
C. Their cations vibrate and physically carry charge: Positive atomic cores vibrate about their lattice positions. These vibrations are not the sustained movement of charge responsible for ordinary metallic electrical conduction.
D. They contain hydrogen bonds that conduct electrons: Hydrogen bonding does not explain metallic conductivity. The relevant feature is the presence of mobile, delocalized electrons within the metallic structure.

STEM: A sealed container holds 2.0 mol of an ideal gas at 300 K and 1.0 atm. If the temperature is increased to 600 K at constant volume, what is the new pressure?

  A. 0.5 atm
  B. 2.0 atm   <= key
  C. 1.0 atm
  D. 4.0 atm

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: B. 2.0 atm
At constant volume and constant moles, Gay-Lussac's law applies: P1/T1 = P2/T2. Substituting: 1.0/300 = P2/600, so P2 = 2.0 atm.
Doubling the absolute temperature at constant volume doubles the pressure because gas molecules move faster and collide with walls more frequently.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0.5 atm: Getting 0.5 atm would imply an inverse relationship between pressure and temperature, but Gay-Lussac's law states they are directly proportional at constant volume: P1/T1 = P2/T2. Doubling temperature (300K to 600K) doubles the pressure (1.0 to 2.0 atm).
C. 1.0 atm: The pressure cannot remain at 1.0 atm when temperature doubles in a sealed container. Higher temperature means gas molecules move faster and strike the container walls more forcefully and frequently, increasing the pressure proportionally.
D. 4.0 atm: Getting 4.0 atm would mean quadrupling the pressure, which would require quadrupling the absolute temperature (to 1200 K). Doubling temperature from 300K to 600K only doubles the pressure to 2.0 atm. Remember to always use Kelvin, not Celsius.
Key Educational Points:
  - At constant volume and amount of gas, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature (Gay-Lussac's law): P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂.
  - Temperature must be in kelvin, because the proportionality is to absolute temperature - using Celsius gives a wrong answer.
  - Here T doubles from 300 K to 600 K, so the pressure doubles from 1.0 to 2.0 atm.
  - The molecular reason is that hotter molecules move faster, so wall collisions become both more frequent and more forceful.
  - A sealed rigid container fixes both the volume and the number of moles, which is what allows the simple two-variable relationship.
Bottom Line: At constant volume, pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature (Gay-Lussac's Law).

B2  [chem-19]  chemistry  correct answer: C

STEM: Which type of isomerism is exhibited by a pair of molecules that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other?

  A. Structural (constitutional) isomerism
  B. Geometric (cis-trans) isomerism
  C. Optical isomerism (enantiomerism)   <= key
  D. Tautomerism

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Optical isomerism (enantiomerism)
Enantiomers are stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images, like left and right hands (hence the term "chirality" from Greek cheir = hand).
| Type of Isomerism | Definition | Example |
| Structural | Same formula, different connectivity | Butane vs. isobutane |
| Geometric (cis-trans) | Same connectivity, different arrangement around a double bond or within a ring | cis-2-butene vs. trans-2-butene |
| Optical (enantiomers) | Non-superimposable mirror images | D-alanine vs. L-alanine |
| Tautomerism | Interconversion of constitutional isomers, commonly involving proton transfer and a change in double-bond position | Keto-enol forms |
A chiral center (usually a carbon with 4 different substituents) is the most common source of optical isomerism. Pure enantiomers rotate plane-polarized light by equal amounts in opposite directions when measured under the same conditions. The signs (+) and (−) indicate the experimentally measured direction of rotation. This is a different labelling system from D/L, which describes configuration relative to glyceraldehyde. A D-sugar or an L-amino acid may be either (+) or (−), and neither label can be predicted from the other.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Structural (constitutional) isomerism: Structural (constitutional) isomers have the same molecular formula but different connectivity of atoms -- different bonding arrangements. For example, butane and isobutane both have the formula C4H10 but different carbon chain arrangements. They are not mirror images of each other.
B. Geometric (cis-trans) isomerism: Cis/trans isomerism describes relative arrangements around a double bond or within a ring. A pair such as cis-2-butene and trans-2-butene consists of diastereomers, not enantiomers. However, molecules with geometric isomerism can also be chiral, so geometric isomerism does not imply that a molecule is superimposable on its mirror image.
D. Tautomerism: Tautomers are constitutional isomers that interconvert, commonly by proton transfer and a change in double-bond position, as in keto-enol tautomerism. Their bonding patterns differ. Enantiomers retain the same connectivity and differ in three-dimensional arrangement.
Key Educational Points:
  - Enantiomers are stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images; the underlying property is called chirality.
  - The usual source of chirality is a carbon bearing four different groups - a stereocentre.
  - Under the same achiral conditions, pure enantiomers have matching melting points, boiling points and solubilities. They rotate plane-polarized light in opposite directions and can interact differently with chiral molecules.
  - That last difference matters biologically, because enzymes and receptors are themselves chiral - two enantiomers of a drug can differ completely in effect.
  - D/L and (+)/(−) are separate systems and must not be equated: D/L describes configuration by comparison with glyceraldehyde, while (+)/(−) is the experimentally measured direction of rotation. Neither can be predicted from the other.
Bottom Line: Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers. Their properties match under the same achiral conditions, but their interactions with chiral biological molecules can differ. D/L describes configuration; (+)/(−) describes measured optical rotation.

STEM: During establishment of lysogeny by bacteriophage λ (lambda) in E. coli, what typically happens to the phage DNA?

  A. It immediately destroys the host cell
  B. It integrates into the host chromosome as a prophage   <= key
  C. It remains in the cytoplasm as an independent plasmid
  D. It is degraded by host restriction enzymes

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: It integrates into the host chromosome as a prophage
Lambda is a temperate phage: it can undergo lytic growth or establish lysogeny. During lysogeny, lambda DNA integrates into the bacterial chromosome as a prophage and is copied with the host chromosome. Genes driving lytic growth are normally repressed, but the prophage is not necessarily completely transcriptionally silent.
| Feature | Lytic growth | Lysogeny in lambda |
| Viral DNA fate | Replicated to produce new phages | Integrated into the host chromosome |
| Host cell fate | Lysed after new phages are produced | Survives and can divide |
| Phage type | Virulent or temperate phages | Temperate phage |
| Production of new virions | Active | Normally repressed |
Lysogenic Cycle:
Lambda establishes lysogeny → prophage is copied during host division → DNA damage can trigger induction → phage replication and assembly → host-cell lysis
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. It immediately destroys the host cell: During lytic growth, phage genomes and particles are produced before the host is lysed; destruction is not instantaneous. Successful lambda lysogeny instead maintains the phage genome without immediate lysis.
C. It remains in the cytoplasm as an independent plasmid: Some temperate phages, such as P1, persist as plasmid prophages. Integration is therefore not required for all lysogeny. Lambda, the phage specified here, normally establishes lysogeny by integrating into the bacterial chromosome.
D. It is degraded by host restriction enzymes: Restriction enzymes can destroy incoming foreign DNA as part of bacterial defense. Destruction of the phage genome would prevent successful establishment of the prophage; it is not its normal fate during lambda lysogeny.
Key Educational Points:
  - Temperate phages can enter lytic growth or establish lysogeny; virulent phages do not establish lysogeny.
  - Lambda prophage DNA is integrated into the host chromosome and inherited during cell division.
  - Some other prophages, including P1, are maintained as plasmids rather than integrated into the chromosome.
Bottom Line: Lambda lysogeny maintains phage DNA as a chromosomal prophage. DNA damage can induce a switch to lytic growth, but lysogeny does not inevitably end in lysis.

STEM: A histological section of bone reveals tightly packed cylindrical units called osteons (Haversian systems), each with concentric lamellae surrounding a central canal. This tissue is MOST characteristic of:

  A. Spongy (cancellous) bone in the epiphysis
  B. Hyaline cartilage in the articular surface
  C. Fibrocartilage in the intervertebral disc
  D. Compact (cortical) bone in the diaphysis   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Compact (cortical) bone in the diaphysis
Compact bone is characterized by a highly organized structure of osteons (Haversian systems). Each osteon consists of concentric lamellae of bone matrix surrounding a central Haversian canal that contains blood vessels and nerves. This dense organization provides the mechanical strength needed in the shaft (diaphysis) of long bones.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Spongy (cancellous) bone in the epiphysis: Spongy bone consists of a network of bony spicules called trabeculae arranged along lines of stress. It lacks the organized osteon structure and is found in the interior of bones, especially at the ends (epiphyses).
B. Hyaline cartilage in the articular surface: Hyaline cartilage is an avascular connective tissue composed of chondrocytes within lacunae surrounded by a matrix rich in type II collagen and proteoglycans. It does not contain osteons.
C. Fibrocartilage in the intervertebral disc: Fibrocartilage contains dense type I collagen fibers and is found in areas requiring both tensile strength and compressive resistance, such as intervertebral discs. It has no osteon organization.
Key Educational Points:
  - Compact bone = osteons with concentric lamellae; spongy bone = trabeculae
  - The Haversian canal carries the blood supply within compact bone
Bottom Line: Osteons (Haversian systems) are the defining structural feature of compact bone, providing organized strength to the diaphysis of long bones.

B5  [bio-346]  biology  correct answer: C

STEM: Which type of epithelium lines the urinary bladder and allows it to stretch?

  A. Simple squamous epithelium
  B. Stratified squamous epithelium
  C. Transitional epithelium (urothelium)   <= key
  D. Pseudostratified columnar epithelium

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: C. Transitional epithelium (urothelium)
Transitional epithelium is a specialized stratified epithelium found exclusively in the urinary tract (renal pelvis, ureters, urinary bladder, and proximal urethra). Its unique feature is the ability to stretch and recoil. When relaxed, the surface cells appear dome-shaped (umbrella cells); when stretched, they flatten. This allows the bladder to accommodate varying volumes of urine.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Simple squamous epithelium: Simple squamous epithelium consists of one layer of flat cells. Examples include the alveolar lining, vascular endothelium and the parietal layer of Bowman's capsule. Unlike urothelium, it does not have multiple cell layers and a specialized umbrella-cell barrier.
B. Stratified squamous epithelium: Stratified squamous epithelium has several cell layers, with flattened cells at the surface and more cuboidal or columnar cells near the base. It protects sites such as the skin, mouth, esophagus and vagina against abrasion. The bladder instead uses urothelium, whose specialized umbrella cells accommodate filling while maintaining a urine barrier.
D. Pseudostratified columnar epithelium: Pseudostratified columnar epithelium appears to be multi-layered but is actually a single layer of cells of varying heights, all attached to the basement membrane. It is found primarily in the trachea and upper respiratory tract, where ciliated forms help move mucus and trapped particles upward (mucociliary escalator). It does not have the stretching capacity needed for the urinary bladder.
Key Educational Points:
  - Transitional epithelium (urothelium) is specialised for stretch: its surface cells change from dome-shaped to flat as the organ fills.
  - The superficial layer is made of large umbrella cells whose apical membrane carries rigid uroplakin plaques, with spare membrane held in cytoplasmic vesicles and inserted as the bladder distends.
  - Tight junctions and the specialized apical membrane of umbrella cells form a low-permeability barrier that limits movement of water and urinary solutes into the underlying tissue.
  - Urothelium lines the urinary tract continuously - renal calyces and pelvis, ureters, bladder, and proximal urethra.
  - During normal bladder filling, the epithelium becomes thinner as cells change shape and rearrange; this does not require cells to be added or lost.
