Q1  [chem-182]  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: The electron configuration of a Cl− ion is the same as that of which noble gas?

  A. Neon
  B. Argon   <= key
  C. Krypton
  D. Helium

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Argon
Chlorine (Z=17) has the electron configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵. When it gains one electron to form Cl−, it has 18 electrons: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶.
This is identical to the electron configuration of argon (Ar, Z=18). The Cl− ion is said to be isoelectronic with argon.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Neon: Neon has 10 electrons. A chloride ion has 17 + 1 = 18 electrons, so its configuration does not match neon.
C. Krypton: Krypton has 36 electrons. Chloride has only 18 electrons and therefore matches argon, not krypton.
D. Helium: Helium has only two electrons, with configuration 1s². Chloride has 18 electrons, so it cannot have helium's configuration.
Key Educational Points:
  - A negative ion has more electrons than its neutral atom; Cl− has 17 + 1 = 18.
  - Argon's configuration is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶, also the configuration of Cl−.
  - Species with the same number of electrons are isoelectronic, but their nuclear charges and sizes can differ.
Bottom Line: Cl− has 18 electrons and the same electron configuration as argon. Count electrons using the atomic number and ionic charge; sharing an electron configuration does not make two species the same element.

Q2  [chem-183]  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which subshell designation is NOT possible?

  A. 3d
  B. 2p
  C. 2d   <= key
  D. 4f

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2d
The angular momentum quantum number l determines the subshell type: l=0 (s), l=1 (p), l=2 (d), l=3 (f). The value of l must be less than n (it ranges from 0 to n−1).
For n=2, l can only be 0 or 1, corresponding to 2s and 2p subshells. A 2d subshell would require l=2, but l cannot equal n. The d subshell first appears at n=3.
All other options are valid: 3d (n=3, l=2), 2p (n=2, l=1), 4f (n=4, l=3).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 3d: For a 3d subshell, n = 3 and l = 2. This is allowed because l can take the values 0, 1 and 2 when n = 3.
B. 2p: For a 2p subshell, n = 2 and l = 1. This is allowed because l can be 0 or 1 when n = 2.
D. 4f: For a 4f subshell, n = 4 and l = 3. This is allowed because l can range from 0 to 3 when n = 4.
Key Educational Points:
  - For a given principal quantum number n, l can range from 0 to n − 1.
  - The labels s, p, d and f correspond to l = 0, 1, 2 and 3.
  - A d subshell requires n ≥ 3, so 2d is impossible.
Bottom Line: The quantum number l ranges from 0 to n−1. Therefore, a d subshell (l=2) cannot exist for n=1 or n=2.

Q3  [chem-184]  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: According to the Pauli exclusion principle, two electrons in the same orbital must differ in their:

  A. principal quantum number
  B. angular momentum quantum number
  C. magnetic quantum number
  D. spin quantum number   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: spin quantum number
The Pauli exclusion principle states that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ).
Two electrons in the same orbital share identical values of n, l, and mₗ. The only quantum number left to differ is the spin quantum number (mₛ), which can be +½ or −½. This is why each orbital can hold a maximum of 2 electrons with opposite spins.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. principal quantum number: Electrons in the same orbital have the same principal quantum number n, which identifies their shell. It is their spin quantum numbers that must differ.
B. angular momentum quantum number: Electrons in the same orbital also share the same angular momentum quantum number l, which identifies the subshell. Different l values would place them in different subshells.
C. magnetic quantum number: The magnetic quantum number (mₗ) specifies which orbital within a subshell an electron occupies. Two electrons in the same orbital have the same mₗ. The Pauli exclusion principle requires that they differ in spin (mₛ), limiting each orbital to a maximum of 2 electrons (one spin-up, one spin-down).
Key Educational Points:
  - An electron in an atom is described by four quantum numbers: n, l, mₗ and mₛ.
  - Two electrons in the same orbital share n, l and mₗ.
  - An orbital holds at most two electrons, with opposite spin quantum numbers, +½ and −½.
Bottom Line: The Pauli exclusion principle limits each orbital to two electrons, which must have opposite spins (+½ and −½).

Q4  [chem-185]  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which of the following has the most protons?

  A. ⁵⁶Fe
  B. ⁵⁸Ni   <= key
  C. ⁵⁵Mn
  D. ⁵⁹Co

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: ⁵⁸Ni
The number of protons equals the atomic number (Z), not the mass number. The mass number (superscript) is the sum of protons and neutrons.

Isotope | Element | Protons (Z)
    ⁵⁶Fe | Iron | 26
    ⁵⁸Ni | Nickel | 28
    ⁵⁵Mn | Manganese | 25
    ⁵⁹Co | Cobalt | 27

Nickel has the highest atomic number (28) and therefore the most protons.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. ⁵⁶Fe: Iron has atomic number 26, so ⁵⁶Fe contains 26 protons. Its mass number 56 counts protons plus neutrons, not protons alone.
C. ⁵⁵Mn: Manganese has atomic number 25, so ⁵⁵Mn contains 25 protons. Nickel has 28 protons and therefore has more than manganese.
D. ⁵⁹Co: Cobalt (Co) has atomic number 27, giving it 27 protons. Although ⁵⁹Co has the highest mass number of the four, mass number includes neutrons. Nickel (Z=28) still has more protons. This question tests whether you distinguish between mass number and atomic number.
Key Educational Points:
  - Atomic number Z equals the number of protons and determines the element.
  - Mass number A equals protons plus neutrons.
  - Nickel has 28 protons, more than the listed iron, manganese and cobalt isotopes, regardless of their mass numbers.
Bottom Line: The number of protons is determined by the element's atomic number, not its mass number. The mass number includes both protons and neutrons.

Q5  [chem-186]  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: What is the ground-state electron configuration of a neutral chromium atom (Z = 24)?

  A. [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²
  B. [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹   <= key
  C. [Ar] 3d⁶
  D. [Ar] 3d³ 4s² 4p¹

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹
Neutral chromium has 24 electrons. Its observed ground-state configuration is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹. The simple filling sequence would suggest [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s², but the 3d and 4s levels are close in energy, and interactions among the electrons favor the observed arrangement. Chromium is therefore a familiar exception to the simplest filling-rule prediction. A half-filled d subshell is a useful pattern to recognize here, not a rule that predicts every atom's configuration.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s²: [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² is the prediction from the simplest filling sequence, but it is not chromium's observed ground-state configuration. Chromium has one 4s electron and five 3d electrons.
C. [Ar] 3d⁶: [Ar] 3d⁶ contains 24 electrons, but the correct electron count alone does not establish the ground state. Neutral chromium's ground state also contains one 4s electron.
D. [Ar] 3d³ 4s² 4p¹: This configuration places an electron in 4p and does not represent chromium's ground state. The observed configuration is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹.
Key Educational Points:
  - Neutral chromium has 24 electrons: 18 in the argon core and six outside it.
  - Its observed ground-state configuration is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹.
  - Simple orbital-filling rules are useful guides, but some atoms have exceptions because orbital energies and electron interactions are close in balance.
Bottom Line: Neutral chromium's ground state is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹. Learn this observed exception to the simple filling sequence; do not apply a universal 'half-filled is always preferred' rule.

Q6  [chem-187]  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: How many orbitals are in the 4f subshell?

  A. 3
  B. 5
  C. 7   <= key
  D. 9

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 7
The number of orbitals in a subshell equals 2l + 1, where l is the angular momentum quantum number.

Subshell | l value | Number of orbitals (2l+1) | Max electrons
    s | 0 | 1 | 2
    p | 1 | 3 | 6
    d | 2 | 5 | 10
    f | 3 | 7 | 14

For the f subshell, l=3, so the number of orbitals = 2(3)+1 = 7. Each can hold 2 electrons, for a maximum of 14 electrons.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 3: Three orbitals corresponds to a p subshell (l=1, mₗ = −1, 0, +1). The f subshell has l=3, giving mₗ values from −3 to +3: that is 7 distinct values, meaning 7 orbitals. The number of orbitals in any subshell is always 2l + 1.
B. 5: Five orbitals corresponds to a d subshell (l=2, mₗ = −2 to +2). The f subshell has l=3, so mₗ ranges from −3 to +3, giving 2(3)+1 = 7 orbitals. Each orbital can hold 2 electrons, so the f subshell can accommodate a maximum of 14 electrons.
D. 9: Nine orbitals would correspond to l=4 (a g subshell, which has mₗ from −4 to +4). The f subshell has l=3, giving 2(3)+1 = 7 orbitals. The pattern is: s=1, p=3, d=5, f=7, g=9 orbitals.
Key Educational Points:
  - An f subshell has l = 3.
  - A subshell contains 2l + 1 orbitals, so an f subshell contains seven.
  - Each orbital holds at most two electrons, giving an f subshell a maximum capacity of 14 electrons.
Bottom Line: The number of orbitals in a subshell is 2l+1. The s, p, d, and f subshells contain 1, 3, 5, and 7 orbitals respectively.

Q7  [chem-188]  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which ion has the smallest ionic radius?

  A. Na⁺
  B. Mg²⁺
  C. Al³⁺   <= key
  D. F−

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Al³⁺
Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, and F− are all isoelectronic species with 10 electrons (same as neon). For isoelectronic species, the one with the most protons has the smallest radius because the greater nuclear charge pulls the electrons closer.

Ion | Protons | Electrons | Relative size
    F− | 9 | 10 | Largest
    Na⁺ | 11 | 10 | ↓
    Mg²⁺ | 12 | 10 | ↓
    Al³⁺ | 13 | 10 | Smallest

Al³⁺ has 13 protons attracting only 10 electrons, resulting in the smallest ionic radius.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Na⁺: Na+ has 10 electrons attracted by 11 protons. Al³⁺ also has 10 electrons but has 13 protons, so its stronger nuclear attraction produces a smaller ion.
B. Mg²⁺: Mg²⁺ has the same 10-electron configuration as Al³⁺, but only 12 protons rather than 13. It is therefore larger than Al³⁺.
D. F−: F⁻ has 10 electrons but only 9 protons - the fewest protons among the isoelectronic set. This makes F⁻ the largest of these four ions because fewer protons exert less pull on the electron cloud. Anions are generally larger than cations with the same electron count.
Key Educational Points:
  - F−, Na+, Mg²⁺ and Al³⁺ each have 10 electrons.
  - Within this isoelectronic series, increasing nuclear charge pulls the electron cloud more tightly.
  - Their size order is F− > Na+ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺.
Bottom Line: Among isoelectronic species, higher nuclear charge (more protons) results in a smaller ionic radius.

Q8  [chem-189]  correct answer: B

STEM: Which element has the electron configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p³?

  A. Silicon
  B. Phosphorus   <= key
  C. Sulfur
  D. Chlorine

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Phosphorus
[Ne] represents the first 10 electrons (1s² 2s² 2p⁶). Adding 3s² 3p³ gives 10 + 2 + 3 = 15 electrons.
The element with atomic number 15 is phosphorus (P). Phosphorus is in Period 3, Group VA, with 5 valence electrons (3s² 3p³). Silicon (Z=14) would be [Ne] 3s² 3p², sulfur (Z=16) would be [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴, and chlorine (Z=17) would be [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Silicon: Silicon has 14 electrons and ground-state configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p². The additional 3p electron in the question identifies phosphorus instead.
C. Sulfur: Sulfur has 16 electrons and configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴. The given configuration contains one fewer electron.
D. Chlorine: Chlorine has 17 electrons and configuration [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵. The given configuration contains only 15 electrons.
Key Educational Points:
  - [Ne] represents 10 electrons; adding 3s² 3p³ gives 15 in total.
  - Atomic number 15 identifies phosphorus.
  - Phosphorus has five valence electrons and belongs to Group 15, also called Group VA in older notation.
Bottom Line: Electron configuration reveals element identity. Group VA elements have 5 valence electrons in the ns²np³ configuration.

STEM: A neutral atom has 20 neutrons and a mass number of 39. What is the element?

  A. Calcium
  B. Potassium   <= key
  C. Argon
  D. Scandium

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Potassium
The mass number (A) equals the number of protons (Z) plus neutrons (N): A = Z + N.
Given: A = 39 and N = 20, so Z = 39 − 20 = 19 protons.
The element with atomic number 19 is potassium (K). Calcium has Z=20, argon has Z=18, and scandium has Z=21.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Calcium: Calcium has 20 protons. An atom with mass number 39 and 20 protons would have 19 neutrons, not the 20 stated in the question.
C. Argon: Argon has 18 protons. An argon atom with mass number 39 would have 21 neutrons, which does not match the question.
D. Scandium: Scandium has 21 protons. With mass number 39, it would have 18 neutrons rather than 20.
Key Educational Points:
  - Mass number equals the number of protons plus the number of neutrons.
  - Here, Z = 39 − 20 = 19, identifying potassium.
  - Because the atom is neutral, it also has 19 electrons.
Bottom Line: Mass number = protons + neutrons. The number of protons (atomic number) uniquely identifies the element.

Q10  [chem-191]  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In the usual introductory Aufbau (diagonal-rule) filling sequence for neutral atoms, how are the 4s and 3d subshells ordered?

  A. 3d before 4s
  B. 4s before 3d   <= key
  C. 3d and 4s simultaneously
  D. 4p before 3d

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 4s before 3d
The usual diagonal-rule sequence places 4s before 3d: 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p. In this rule, subshells are ordered by n + l; 4s has n + l = 4, whereas 3d has n + l = 5. This is a useful introductory filling guide. It does not mean that 4s is always lower in energy than 3d in every atom or ion: the relative energies depend on the atom and its electron configuration.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 3d before 4s: The usual diagonal-rule sequence places 4s before 3d, not the reverse. This question asks about that introductory sequence, rather than the orbital-energy ordering in every individual atom or ion.
C. 3d and 4s simultaneously: The introductory filling sequence lists 4s and then 3d as separate steps. It does not describe them as filling simultaneously.
D. 4p before 3d: In the usual sequence, 3d comes before 4p. Thus the relevant order is 4s → 3d → 4p.
Key Educational Points:
  - The diagonal rule orders subshells using n + l, with lower n first when those sums are equal.
  - In that sequence, 4s comes before 3d, and 3d before 4p.
  - Real orbital energies depend on the atom and its electron configuration, so the filling mnemonic has limits.
Bottom Line: The usual introductory filling sequence is 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p. Do not turn that mnemonic into a claim that 4s is always lower in energy than 3d.

Q11  [chem-192]  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In the hydrogen-like atomic-orbital model, what is the total number of radial and angular nodes in a 3p orbital?

  A. 0
  B. 1
  C. 2   <= key
  D. 3

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2
The total number of nodes in an orbital equals n − 1, where n is the principal quantum number.
For a 3p orbital: total nodes = 3 − 1 = 2.
For a 3p orbital, n = 3 and l = 1. Radial nodes = n − l − 1 = 1, angular nodes = l = 1, and the total is n − 1 = 2. The angular node of a usual real p orbital is a plane, but that does not make every angular node in every subshell planar.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0: Only the 1s orbital has zero nodes. The total number of nodes for any orbital is n − 1. For a 3p orbital: 3 − 1 = 2 nodes (1 radial node + 1 angular node). Nodes are regions where the probability of finding an electron is zero, and they increase with the principal quantum number.
B. 1: One is the number of radial nodes, and also the number of angular nodes, in a 3p orbital. The question asks for their total, which is 1 + 1 = 2.
D. 3: Three total nodes would apply to an orbital with n = 4 (since total nodes = n − 1). For the 3p orbital, total nodes = 3 − 1 = 2. These are distributed as 1 angular node (the nodal plane characteristic of all p orbitals) and 1 radial node (a spherical shell where electron density is zero).
Key Educational Points:
  - For a 3p orbital, n = 3 and l = 1.
  - Radial nodes = n − l − 1 = 1.
  - Angular nodes = l = 1, so the total is two.
Bottom Line: In the hydrogen-like orbital model, total nodes = n − 1. A 3p orbital has one radial node and one angular node, giving two in total.

STEM: Which pair of atoms are isotopes of the same element?

  A. ¹⁴C and ¹⁴N
  B. ¹⁶O and ¹⁸O   <= key
  C. ²³Na and ²⁴Mg
  D. ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: ¹⁶O and ¹⁸O
Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons, same Z) with different mass numbers (different numbers of neutrons).
¹⁶O and ¹⁸O are both oxygen atoms (Z=8). ¹⁶O has 8 neutrons and ¹⁸O has 10 neutrons - they are isotopes.
¹⁴C (Z=6) and ¹⁴N (Z=7) are isobars (same mass number, different elements). ⁴⁰Ar (Z=18) and ⁴⁰Ca (Z=20) are also isobars.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. ¹⁴C and ¹⁴N: ¹⁴C and ¹⁴N have the same mass number but different atomic numbers: carbon has six protons and nitrogen has seven. They are isobars, not isotopes of one element.
C. ²³Na and ²⁴Mg: ²³Na has 11 protons and ²⁴Mg has 12. Although each has 12 neutrons, their different proton numbers make them different elements, not isotopes of one element.
D. ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca: ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca share mass number 40 but have different proton numbers, 18 and 20. They are isobars, not isotopes of one element.
Key Educational Points:
  - Isotopes of one element share a proton number but differ in neutron number.
  - ¹⁶O and ¹⁸O each have eight protons, with eight and ten neutrons respectively.
  - Equal mass numbers alone do not establish isotopes; different elements can have the same mass number.
Bottom Line: Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers. Isobars have the same mass number but are different elements.

Q13  [chem-194]  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which listed element has the highest electronegativity on the Pauling scale?

  A. Oxygen
  B. Nitrogen
  C. Fluorine   <= key
  D. Chlorine

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Fluorine
Electronegativity describes an atom's attraction for the electrons it shares in a bond. On the Pauling scale it generally increases across a period and generally decreases down a group, and fluorine is the highest of all the elements at about 4.0.

Element | Electronegativity (Pauling)
    Nitrogen | 3.0
    Oxygen | 3.4
    Fluorine | 4.0
    Chlorine | 3.2

Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Oxygen: Oxygen is highly electronegative, about 3.4 on the Pauling scale, but fluorine is higher at about 4.0.
B. Nitrogen: Nitrogen has a Pauling electronegativity of about 3.0. It attracts shared electrons strongly, but less strongly than fluorine.
D. Chlorine: Chlorine has an electronegativity of about 3.16 - high, but less than fluorine (3.98). Chlorine is directly below fluorine in Group 17. Moving down a group decreases electronegativity because the valence electrons are farther from the nucleus and more shielded by inner electron shells.
Key Educational Points:
  - Electronegativity describes an atom's attraction for shared bonding electrons.
  - Fluorine is highest on the Pauling scale, at about 4.0.
  - Electronegativity and electron affinity are different properties and need not have the same highest-ranked element.
Bottom Line: Fluorine has the highest Pauling electronegativity, about 4.0. Electronegativity concerns shared electrons in a bond, not the energy change when an isolated atom gains an electron.

STEM: For main-group elements, which property generally increases down a group?

  A. Ionization energy
  B. Electronegativity
  C. Metallic character   <= key
  D. Energy released on electron attachment

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Metallic character
For main-group elements, the following tendencies are generally observed down a group:

Property | Trend down a group
    Atomic radius | Generally increases
    Metallic character | Generally increases
    Ionization energy | Generally decreases
    Electronegativity | Generally decreases
    Energy released on electron attachment | Irregular; not a reliable simple down-group trend

Metallic character generally increases down a main group because valence electrons lie farther from the nucleus and are more shielded, so an outer electron is easier to lose. Trends of this kind are useful generalizations rather than exceptionless rules.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Ionization energy: First ionization energy generally decreases down a main group because the outer electron is farther from the nucleus and more shielded. This is the opposite of the increase asked about.
B. Electronegativity: Electronegativity generally decreases down a main group. An atom's attraction for shared bonding electrons becomes weaker as its valence shell lies farther from the nucleus.
D. Energy released on electron attachment: The energy released when an isolated atom gains an electron has an irregular pattern, with important exceptions. A general increase down a main group is not the reliable trend tested here.
Key Educational Points:
  - Down a main group, valence electrons generally lie farther from the nucleus and experience more shielding.
  - Easier loss of outer electrons is associated with greater metallic character.
  - Periodic trends are useful generalizations, not rules without exceptions.
Bottom Line: Metallic character generally increases down a main group as outer electrons become easier to remove. Electron attachment has more exceptions and should not be treated as a simple universal trend.

Q15  [chem-196]  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Which element has the highest first ionization energy?

  A. Li
  B. Be
  C. N
  D. Ne   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Ne
First ionization energy is the energy required to remove one electron from a gaseous atom. It generally increases across a period and up a group.
Among the listed Period 2 elements, neon has the highest first ionization energy. Its filled 2s² 2p⁶ valence shell and large effective nuclear charge make an electron hard to remove.
Note: Within Period 2, there are minor exceptions (Be > B and N > O), but the overall trend places Ne highest.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Li: Lithium's outer electron occupies 2s and is relatively easy to remove. Removing it leaves the helium configuration, so lithium's first ionization energy is much lower than neon's.
B. Be: Beryllium has a higher ionization energy than lithium due to its greater nuclear charge, but it is still far below neon. Be has a filled 2s subshell, which provides some extra stability, but neon's completely filled 2s²2p⁶ configuration is far more stable. Ionization energy generally increases across a period.
C. N: Nitrogen has a relatively high ionization energy (enhanced by its half-filled 2p³ configuration), but neon still has a significantly higher value. Among the listed Period 2 elements, neon's filled outer shell gives it the highest first ionization energy, so Ne requires the most energy to remove an electron.
Key Educational Points:
  - First ionization energy is the energy needed to remove one electron from a gaseous neutral atom.
  - Neon has the highest first ionization energy among Li, Be, N and Ne.
  - The general across-period increase has exceptions, including Be/B and N/O.
Bottom Line: Neon has the highest first ionization energy among the listed Period 2 elements. Its filled valence shell and strong attraction for its outer electrons make electron removal difficult.

Q16  [chem-197]  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: The elements Na, Mg, and Al are in the same period. Which has the smallest atomic radius?

  A. Na
  B. Mg
  C. Al   <= key
  D. They are all the same size

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Al
Atomic radius generally decreases across a period from left to right. Each successive element has one more proton, and the added electrons enter the same main shell, so the increase in nuclear charge is not fully offset by shielding. The effective nuclear attraction therefore grows and pulls the electron cloud closer.

  B. 14   <= key
  C. 15
  D. 16

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 14
A linear chain of n amino-acid residues has n − 1 peptide links. In the overall condensation equation starting from free amino acids, each link corresponds to loss of one water molecule. Thus 15 amino acids give 15 − 1 = 14 links and 14 water molecules in that equation. This is net stoichiometry; ribosomal synthesis uses activated amino acids attached to tRNA rather than direct dehydration of free amino acids.
Why the other choices are wrong:

Key Educational Points:

  - In the overall condensation equation from free amino acids, one water is produced per link.

STEM: The isoelectric point (pI) of an amino acid is the pH at which:

  A. The amino acid carries a net positive charge
  B. The amino acid carries a net negative charge
  C. The amino acid has no net electrical charge   <= key
  D. All functional groups are uncharged

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The amino acid has no net electrical charge
The isoelectric point, pI, is the pH at which an amino acid or protein has zero average net electrical charge. Positive and negative charged groups can still be present: zero net charge does not mean that every group is uncharged. For a simple amino acid without an ionizable side chain, the zwitterion predominates near the pI, and pI = (pKa₁ + pKa₂)/2. Zero average charge also explains the absence of net charge-driven migration in an ideal electrophoresis experiment at the pI.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. The amino acid carries a net positive charge: Below its pI, an amino acid has a positive average net charge because more of its ionizable groups are protonated. At the pI itself, the average positive and negative charges balance.
B. The amino acid carries a net negative charge: Above its pI, an amino acid has a negative average net charge because more groups are deprotonated. This differs from the zero average net charge at the pI.
D. All functional groups are uncharged: Charged groups remain present at the pI. For example, the predominant glycine zwitterion has –NH₃+ and –COO− groups whose charges cancel; neither group is uncharged.
Key Educational Points:
  - At the pI, positive and negative charges balance on average.
  - A zwitterion can contain charged groups while having zero net formal charge.
  - For an amino acid with no ionizable side chain, pI is the average of the carboxyl and ammonium pKa values.
Bottom Line: pI is the pH of zero average net charge, not the pH at which every functional group is uncharged.

STEM: Which type of bond links successive amino acid residues along a protein's polypeptide backbone?

  A. Hydrogen bond
  B. Ionic bond
  C. Peptide bond   <= key
  D. Disulfide bond

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Peptide bond
Successive residues along a polypeptide backbone are joined by peptide bonds. A peptide bond is a covalent amide linkage running from the carbonyl carbon of one residue to the nitrogen of the next, formed between the α-carboxyl group of one amino acid and the α-amino group of the following one. Most peptide links are written –CO–NH–, but an internal proline residue has no N–H at that nitrogen.
Hydrogen bonds stabilize secondary structure. Ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions, and van der Waals forces contribute to tertiary structure. Disulfide bonds are covalent but link cysteine side chains rather than joining successive backbone residues.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Hydrogen bond: Hydrogen bonds help stabilize structures such as alpha helices and beta sheets. They are not the covalent C–N links that connect successive residues along the backbone.
B. Ionic bond: Attractions between charged side chains can stabilize protein structure, but they are not the amide bonds joining successive backbone residues.
D. Disulfide bond: Disulfide bonds connect the sulfur atoms of cysteine side chains. They can cross-link parts of a protein, but they are not the regular links between successive residues in the polypeptide backbone.
Key Educational Points:
  - A peptide bond joins a carbonyl carbon to the nitrogen of the next residue.
  - These bonds form the continuous polypeptide backbone.
  - Disulfide bonds link cysteine side chains rather than supplying the usual residue-to-residue backbone connection.
Bottom Line: Successive amino-acid residues are joined along the backbone by peptide bonds, which are covalent amide C–N links.

STEM: Among the 20 standard amino acids in their free forms, which has a secondary α-amino group?

  A. Glycine
  B. Proline   <= key
  C. Tryptophan
  D. Histidine

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Proline
In the unprotonated structure of free proline, the alpha-amino nitrogen is bonded to two carbon atoms and one hydrogen, making it a secondary amine. Its saturated ring joins the side chain back to that nitrogen. A secondary amine is not an imine, so 'imino group' is misleading here. When proline is an internal residue of a peptide, its nitrogen is part of a tertiary amide and has no N–H hydrogen-bond donor.
The ring restricts backbone rotation. Peptide bonds preceding proline have a higher cis proportion than most other peptide bonds, but most are still trans. Proline can introduce bends or kinks rather than necessarily destroying every regular secondary structure.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Glycine: Glycine is unique for having the simplest side chain (just a hydrogen atom), making it the smallest amino acid with no chirality at the α-carbon. However, free glycine has a standard primary alpha-amino group (-NH2), not a secondary one.
C. Tryptophan: Tryptophan contains a large aromatic indole ring system. Free tryptophan has a primary alpha-amino group like most standard amino acids, and its indole nitrogen belongs to that aromatic ring rather than to the alpha-amino group.
D. Histidine: Histidine contains an imidazole ring in its side chain, which has important acid-base properties (pKa ~6.0). Its imidazole nitrogens are part of that ring; free histidine's alpha-amino group is a standard primary amine. Only proline has a side chain that connects back to the alpha-amino nitrogen.
Key Educational Points:
  - Free proline's unprotonated alpha-amino nitrogen is attached to two carbons, so it is a secondary amine.
  - The ring connecting its side chain to that nitrogen restricts backbone rotation.
  - Once proline is an internal peptide residue, its nitrogen has no N–H bond for the usual backbone hydrogen-bond donation.
Bottom Line: Free proline has a secondary alpha-amino group, not an imino group. Internal peptide-bonded proline has a tertiary amide nitrogen with no N–H donor.
