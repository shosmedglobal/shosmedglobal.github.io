The endosymbiotic theory proposes that mitochondria originated from an ancient symbiotic relationship. Which evidence BEST supports this theory?

  A) Mitochondria contain actin filaments identical to those of bacteria
  B) Mitochondria have a single membrane like gram-positive bacteria
  C) Mitochondria are found in all prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
  D) Mitochondrial genes and translation machinery show clear relationships to those of bacteria

### Correct Answer: Mitochondrial genes and translation machinery show clear relationships to those of bacteria

Mitochondria evolved from a bacterial ancestor that formed a lasting association with an ancestral host cell. Comparisons of mitochondrial genes and the machinery used to make proteins provide strong evidence of this bacterial ancestry. Mitochondria also retain a small genome and arise by division of existing mitochondria. Over evolution, many genes were lost or transferred to the nucleus, so modern mitochondria depend on numerous proteins made from nuclear genes. They are integrated parts of the cell, not independently living bacteria.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Mitochondria contain actin filaments identical to those of bacteria:** The claim about identical actin filaments is not the evidence for mitochondrial ancestry. Bacteria and eukaryotes have related cytoskeletal proteins, but comparisons of mitochondrial genes and translation machinery provide the relevant evidence here.

**B. Mitochondria have a single membrane like gram-positive bacteria:** Mitochondria have an outer and an inner membrane. A single membrane therefore does not describe them. Their double-membrane structure is consistent with bacterial ancestry, but it should not be used to assert a particular origin for each membrane as an established fact.

**C. Mitochondria are found in all prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells:** Mitochondria are organelles of eukaryotic cells; bacteria and archaea do not contain them. They are also not present in every eukaryotic cell. Their distribution does not support the claim that they occur in all cells.

### Key Educational Points:

  | Evidence | What it teaches |
  | Related genes and translation machinery | Mitochondria have bacterial ancestry |
  | Small retained genome | Some genetic functions remain within mitochondria |
  | Dependence on nuclear genes | Modern mitochondria are integrated into the host cell |
  | Division of existing mitochondria | New mitochondria arise from pre-existing organelles |

>> Bottom Line: Mitochondrial genes and translation machinery provide strong evidence of bacterial ancestry; modern mitochondria also depend on the host cell's nuclear genes.

Some of the earliest widely accepted stromatolite evidence of microbial life is approximately how old?

  A) 3.5 billion years ago
  B) 4.6 billion years ago
  C) 2.1 billion years ago
  D) 540 million years ago

### Correct Answer: 3.5 billion years ago

Stromatolites are layered structures that can form through the activity of microbial communities. Examples from rocks about 3.5 billion years old provide widely accepted evidence that microbial life was already present at that time. These fossils provide a minimum age for life; they do not identify the exact time or place where life first originated.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. 4.6 billion years ago:** About 4.5-4.6 billion years is the age of the Earth itself, which is not in itself evidence of life. The question asks how old the preserved evidence of microbial life is, not how old the planet is.

**C. 2.1 billion years ago:** Life was already present long before 2.1 billion years ago. This date is too recent for the evidence asked about.

**D. 540 million years ago:** This is the approximate date of the Cambrian Explosion, when most major animal phyla appeared in the fossil record within a relatively short period. Life existed for roughly 3 billion years before this event.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Stromatolites can preserve evidence of ancient microbial communities.
  - Widely accepted evidence places microbial life on Earth by about 3.5 billion years ago.
  - The oldest preserved evidence does not necessarily date the origin of life.

>> Bottom Line: Microbial life was present by about 3.5 billion years ago; its exact origin remains uncertain.

Which sequence is consistent with a proposed origin-of-life model in which self-replicating molecules arise before membrane-enclosed protocells?

  A) Formation of polymers → abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of protobionts → origin of self-replicating molecules
  B) Formation of protobionts → abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of polymers → origin of self-replicating molecules
  C) Origin of self-replicating molecules → abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of protobionts → formation of polymers
  D) Abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of polymers → origin of self-replicating molecules → formation of protobionts

### Correct Answer: Abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of polymers → origin of self-replicating molecules → formation of protobionts

In this proposed model, small organic building blocks form without living organisms, join into larger molecules, and eventually give rise to systems capable of replication. Enclosure within compartments then helps keep components together. The sequence in D fits that model. The actual route to the first cells is not known, and other models allow compartments, metabolism and replication to develop together or in a different order.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Formation of polymers → abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of protobionts → origin of self-replicating molecules:** Polymers are placed before their monomers. A polymer cannot be assembled before its constituent building blocks are available.

**B. Formation of protobionts → abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of polymers → origin of self-replicating molecules:** Protocells are placed before the organic building blocks used to construct them in the stated model.

**C. Origin of self-replicating molecules → abiotic synthesis of monomers → formation of protobionts → formation of polymers:** The proposed self-replicating polymers are placed before the monomers from which they are built.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Monomers are building blocks; polymers are larger molecules assembled from them.
  - Replication with heritable variation allows selection among replicating systems.
  - Proposed origin-of-life sequences are models, and the historical order remains uncertain.

>> Bottom Line: D follows the sequence specified in this model; it is not a proven timeline of the origin of life.

Why are alkaline hydrothermal environments considered a possible setting for the origin of life?

  A) Hydrothermal vents provide oxygen-rich conditions ideal for aerobic metabolism
  B) Hydrothermal vents contain large amounts of pre-formed DNA
  C) Hydrothermal vents have lower temperatures that preserve fragile organic molecules
  D) Hydrothermal vents supply continuous chemical energy and mineral catalysts in a protected environment

### Correct Answer: Hydrothermal vents supply continuous chemical energy and mineral catalysts in a protected environment

Alkaline hydrothermal systems can supply chemical energy through differences in chemical composition and pH between vent fluids and surrounding water. Mineral surfaces may catalyze reactions, and small mineral pores may help concentrate reactants. Submerged settings are also shielded from surface ultraviolet radiation. These features make such environments useful models for studying prebiotic chemistry, but they do not establish that life actually began there.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Hydrothermal vents provide oxygen-rich conditions ideal for aerobic metabolism:** These models do not depend on an oxygen-rich environment or on aerobic metabolism. The early deep ocean near such vents was essentially free of dissolved oxygen, and the proposed prebiotic chemistry is anaerobic.

**B. Hydrothermal vents contain large amounts of pre-formed DNA:** The hypothesis concerns energy sources and conditions for prebiotic chemistry, not a ready-made supply of DNA. Finding DNA in a modern inhabited vent would not demonstrate that DNA formed there before life.

**C. Hydrothermal vents have lower temperatures that preserve fragile organic molecules:** Vents are not generally cooler than the surface. Conditions vary between and within vent systems, and excessive heat can break down some organic compounds, so a single temperature range does not define these environments.

### Key Educational Points:

  - In the alkaline-vent model, natural proton gradients across mineral barriers resemble the gradients used in modern chemiosmosis
  - Iron-sulfur minerals at vents may catalyze carbon-fixation reactions
  - Modern enzymes with iron-sulfur clusters are often cited as consistent with this proposal, but they do not show where life originated

>> Bottom Line: Alkaline hydrothermal systems are a possible setting for the origin of life: they may supply chemical energy and mineral catalysts, and they are shielded from surface ultraviolet radiation.

A ribozyme is a type of RNA molecule that can catalyze specific biochemical reactions. The discovery of ribozymes is significant because it:

  A) proved that proteins were the first biological catalysts
  B) demonstrated that DNA can function without RNA
  C) supported the idea that RNA could have served as both genetic material and enzyme before DNA and proteins evolved
  D) showed that all modern enzymes are made of RNA

### Correct Answer: supported the idea that RNA could have served as both genetic material and enzyme before DNA and proteins evolved

The discovery of ribozymes by Thomas Cech (self-splicing introns in Tetrahymena, 1982) and Sidney Altman (RNase P, 1983) - for which they shared the 1989 Nobel Prize - provided crucial evidence for the RNA world hypothesis. If RNA can both store information and catalyze reactions, then a primitive RNA-based life form could have existed before the evolution of DNA (for information storage) and proteins (for catalysis).

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Proved that proteins were the first biological catalysts:** The discovery of ribozymes actually challenged the assumption that only proteins can be catalysts. It showed that RNA can also function as an enzyme, suggesting that proteins may NOT have been the first catalysts - RNA may have preceded them.

**B. Demonstrated that DNA can function without RNA:** Ribozyme discovery has nothing to do with DNA functioning without RNA. In fact, it suggests the opposite - that RNA was more functionally central in early life, and DNA came later as a more stable information storage molecule.

**D. Showed that all modern enzymes are made of RNA:** The vast majority of modern enzymes are proteins. Ribozymes are relatively rare in modern organisms, though they perform some critical functions (e.g., the ribosome's peptidyl transferase center is a ribozyme). The discovery showed RNA CAN be catalytic, not that all enzymes are RNA.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Key ribozymes: self-splicing introns (Group I and II), RNase P, ribosomal RNA (peptidyl transferase)
  - Thomas Cech and Sidney Altman won the 1989 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for ribozyme discovery
  - The ribosome is fundamentally a ribozyme - the catalytic core is rRNA, not protein

>> Bottom Line: Ribozymes demonstrate that RNA can catalyze reactions, supporting the RNA world hypothesis that RNA preceded both DNA and proteins in early life.

In the lac operon of E. coli, what happens when both glucose and lactose are ABSENT from the growth medium?

  A) The operon is maximally transcribed due to CAP activation
  B) Loss of glucose inactivates the lac repressor
  C) RNA polymerase constitutively transcribes the structural genes
  D) The lac repressor remains active and keeps transcription strongly repressed

### Correct Answer: The lac repressor remains active and keeps transcription strongly repressed

When lactose is absent, its inducer derivative allolactose is unavailable to inactivate the lac repressor. The repressor therefore strongly reduces transcription of lacZ, lacY and lacA. Low glucose favors CAP-cAMP activation, but this does not overcome strong repression in the absence of inducer. A very small amount of basal expression can still occur; repression does not mean that absolutely no transcript is ever made.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. The operon is maximally transcribed due to CAP activation:** Low glucose favors CAP-cAMP activation, but maximal expression also requires relief of lac repression. With lactose absent, the repressor remains active, so CAP activation alone does not produce maximal transcription.

**B. Loss of glucose inactivates the lac repressor:** The lac repressor responds to the lactose-derived inducer allolactose. Glucose mainly affects the CAP-cAMP control system; removing glucose does not directly inactivate the repressor.

**C. RNA polymerase constitutively transcribes the structural genes:** Constitutive expression means expression that no longer responds normally to the regulatory signal. The normal lac operon remains regulated. Certain mutations that disable repression can produce constitutive expression, but that is not the situation in the stem.

### Key Educational Points:

  | Condition | Glucose | Lactose | cAMP | CAP active? | Repressor predominantly bound? | Transcription |
  | Both absent | − | − | High | Yes | Yes | Strongly repressed; very low basal expression |
  | Glucose only | + | − | Low | No | Yes | Strongly repressed; very low basal expression |
  | Lactose only | − | + | High | Yes | No | MAXIMAL |
  | Both present | + | + | Low | No | No | LOW |

>> Bottom Line: Without lactose, the lac repressor keeps expression very low even when low glucose favors CAP-cAMP activation.

The trp operon in E. coli is regulated by a mechanism called attenuation. What does attenuation involve?

  A) A repressor protein that binds the operator only when tryptophan is present
  B) Activation of the operon by a tryptophan-CAP complex
  C) Degradation of trp mRNA by RNase when tryptophan concentrations are low
  D) Premature termination of mRNA transcription in the leader sequence when tryptophan levels are high

### Correct Answer: Premature termination of mRNA transcription in the leader sequence when tryptophan levels are high

This E. coli attenuation mechanism depends on transcription and translation occurring together. Ribosome movement on the leader RNA influences whether a terminator or an antiterminator forms. Eukaryotic nuclear transcription is separated from cytoplasmic translation, so it does not use this same coupled mechanism. The trp operon mRNA has a leader sequence (trpL) containing two tandem tryptophan codons. When tryptophan is abundant, charged tRNAᵀʳᵖ is plentiful, the ribosome translates through these codons quickly, and the mRNA folds into a terminator hairpin structure (3:4 hairpin), causing RNA polymerase to dissociate before transcribing the structural genes.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. A repressor protein that binds the operator only when tryptophan is present:** This describes corepression, which is a separate regulatory mechanism of the trp operon (the trp repressor requires tryptophan as a corepressor to bind the operator). Attenuation is an additional, distinct layer of control that operates at the level of mRNA secondary structure, not repressor-operator binding.

**B. Activation of the operon by a tryptophan-CAP complex:** CAP (catabolite activator protein) is involved in regulating the lac operon and other sugar metabolism operons in response to glucose levels. CAP does not regulate the trp operon. The trp operon is regulated by the trp repressor and attenuation.

**C. Degradation of trp mRNA by RNase when tryptophan concentrations are low:** mRNA degradation by RNases is a general mechanism of gene regulation, but it is not what attenuation refers to. Attenuation specifically involves early termination of transcription, not post-transcriptional degradation of completed mRNA.

### Key Educational Points:

  - This E. coli mechanism works because transcription and translation occur together, with no nuclear envelope separating them
  - The trp operon has TWO regulatory mechanisms: corepression (operator level) and attenuation (leader sequence level)
  - When tryptophan is LOW, the ribosome stalls at trp codons → anti-terminator hairpin (2:3) forms → transcription continues
  - Eukaryotic nuclear transcription is separated from cytoplasmic translation, so eukaryotes do not use this coupled mechanism; premature transcription termination does regulate some eukaryotic genes by other routes

>> Bottom Line: Attenuation regulates the trp operon by causing premature transcription termination when tryptophan levels are high, using mRNA secondary structure changes.

In the classic E. coli F-plasmid system, what is the role of the F plasmid?

  A) It encodes the genes for pilus formation and DNA transfer from donor (F+) to recipient (F−)
  B) It encodes antibiotic resistance genes that are transferred to the recipient
  C) It integrates into the bacterial chromosome and prevents further conjugation
  D) It carries the genes for restriction enzymes that protect against phage DNA

### Correct Answer: It encodes the genes for pilus formation and DNA transfer from donor (F+) to recipient (F−)

The F (fertility) plasmid carries the tra genes that encode the sex pilus (F pilus) and the DNA transfer machinery. During conjugation, the F+ (donor) cell extends its pilus to contact an F− (recipient) cell, establishing a connection through the conjugation machinery for DNA transfer. A single strand of the F plasmid is transferred to the recipient, which then synthesizes the complementary strand and becomes F+.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. It encodes antibiotic resistance genes that are transferred to the recipient:** Resistance genes define the function of an R plasmid. The classic F plasmid is identified here by its pilus and DNA-transfer genes. These categories are not mutually exclusive: some conjugative plasmids, including F-like plasmids, also carry antibiotic-resistance genes.

**C. It integrates into the bacterial chromosome and prevents further conjugation:** The F plasmid CAN integrate into the chromosome (forming an Hfr strain), but this does NOT prevent conjugation. Hfr cells actually transfer chromosomal DNA to F− recipients very efficiently, though the entire F plasmid is rarely transferred completely (so recipients usually remain F−).

**D. It carries the genes for restriction enzymes that protect against phage DNA:** Restriction-modification systems help protect bacteria from foreign DNA. Their genes can occur on chromosomes or plasmids. They do not describe the defining fertility and DNA-transfer function of the F plasmid in this question.

### Key Educational Points:

  - F+ × F− → both become F+ (F plasmid transferred)
  - Hfr × F− → recipient gets chromosomal genes but usually remains F− (incomplete F transfer)
  - F' (F prime) plasmid = excised F plasmid carrying some chromosomal genes → can transfer specific chromosomal genes to F− (sexduction)

>> Bottom Line: The F plasmid encodes the sex pilus and transfer machinery needed for bacterial conjugation from F+ donor to F− recipient.

During generalized transduction, how does bacterial DNA get transferred from one bacterium to another?

  A) A bacteriophage accidentally packages random fragments of host bacterial DNA instead of its own genome
  B) Two bacterial cells exchange DNA through direct cell-to-cell contact via a pilus
  C) A bacterium takes up free DNA from the environment released by a lysed cell
  D) A specialized phage excises a specific bacterial gene adjacent to its integration site

### Correct Answer: A bacteriophage accidentally packages random fragments of host bacterial DNA instead of its own genome

During phage-particle production, some particles mistakenly package bacterial DNA rather than the usual phage genome. A particle can then deliver that bacterial DNA to another bacterium, where it can be incorporated into the chromosome by homologous recombination. In generalized transduction, DNA from many different chromosomal regions can be transferred.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. Two bacterial cells exchange DNA through direct cell-to-cell contact via a pilus:** This describes conjugation, not transduction. Conjugation transfers DNA between cells that come into contact; in E. coli the F pilus is the classic example, although not every conjugation system uses the same appendage. Transduction instead uses bacteriophage particles as vectors.

**C. A bacterium takes up free DNA from the environment released by a lysed cell:** This describes transformation, the process discovered by Frederick Griffith (1928) and confirmed by Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty (1944). In transformation, competent bacteria take up naked DNA from the environment. No phage is involved.

**D. A specialized phage excises a specific bacterial gene adjacent to its integration site:** This describes specialized transduction, not generalized transduction. In specialized transduction, a lysogenic phage (like lambda) imprecisely excises from the chromosome and takes nearby bacterial genes (e.g., gal or bio genes flanking the lambda attachment site). Only specific genes can be transferred, not random fragments.

### Key Educational Points:

  | Feature | Generalized transduction | Specialized transduction |
  | Typical origin of transferred DNA | Accidental packaging of bacterial DNA during phage-particle production | Faulty excision of an integrated prophage that carries adjacent bacterial DNA |
  | Genes transferred | Many different chromosomal regions | Only genes near the phage integration site |
  | Example phage | P1, P22 | Lambda (λ) |

>> Bottom Line: Generalized transduction occurs when a phage accidentally packages random bacterial DNA during the lytic cycle and transfers it to a new host.

Q10  [bio-482]  Answer: A
==============================================================================
An R plasmid is clinically significant because it:

  A) carries genes for antibiotic resistance that can be transferred between bacteria
  B) enables bacteria to undergo meiosis
  C) encodes the enzymes for lactose metabolism
  D) is required for bacterial binary fission

### Correct Answer: carries genes for antibiotic resistance that can be transferred between bacteria

R plasmids (resistance plasmids) carry genes encoding enzymes that inactivate antibiotics (e.g., beta-lactamases that break down penicillin), efflux pumps, or modified target sites. Many R plasmids can spread between bacteria by conjugation, including between different species, contributing to the rapid spread of antibiotic resistance. Some carry their own transfer machinery, some depend on a helper system, and some are not transferred this way at all.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. Enables bacteria to undergo meiosis:** Bacteria are prokaryotes and NEVER undergo meiosis. Meiosis is exclusive to sexually reproducing eukaryotes. Bacteria reproduce by binary fission and exchange genetic material through conjugation, transformation, and transduction - none of which involves meiosis.

**C. Encodes the enzymes for lactose metabolism:** The usual E. coli lacZ, lacY and lacA genes are chromosomal. Some plasmids can acquire metabolic genes, including lac genes, but the designation R plasmid refers to antibiotic resistance, not lactose metabolism.

**D. Is required for bacterial binary fission:** Binary fission is the standard mode of bacterial reproduction and requires only the chromosomal DNA and cell division machinery. Typical accessory resistance plasmids are not the core machinery required for bacterial cell division. Their significance here is the resistance they can confer.

### Key Educational Points:

  - R plasmids are a major driver of the antibiotic resistance crisis
  - A single R plasmid can carry resistance genes for multiple antibiotics simultaneously (multi-drug resistance)
  - R plasmids can transfer between species (e.g., from E. coli to Salmonella), making resistance spread unpredictable
  - Overuse of antibiotics selects for bacteria carrying R plasmids

>> Bottom Line: R plasmids carry transferable antibiotic resistance genes that spread between bacteria via conjugation, posing a major clinical threat.

Q11  [bio-483]  Answer: A
==============================================================================
Transposons ("jumping genes") in bacteria are significant because they:

  A) can move from one location to another in the genome, sometimes carrying antibiotic resistance genes
  B) are required for normal DNA replication in all bacteria
  C) replace the function of RNA polymerase during transcription
  D) are only found in eukaryotic genomes and not in prokaryotes

### Correct Answer: can move from one location to another in the genome, sometimes carrying antibiotic resistance genes

Transposons are mobile genetic elements (discovered by Barbara McClintock in maize) that can "jump" from one position to another within the genome or between the chromosome and plasmids. Composite transposons can carry genes, such as antibiotic-resistance genes, between two insertion sequences. Other transposons can also carry accessory genes without having this two-IS arrangement. Transposition can disrupt genes, activate nearby genes, or spread resistance genes between the chromosome and plasmids.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. Are required for normal DNA replication in all bacteria:** Transposons are not part of the normal DNA replication machinery. Ordinary chromosome replication depends on the cell's own replication machinery and does not require transposition. Transposons are accessory elements, and many bacteria function perfectly well without them.

**C. Replace the function of RNA polymerase during transcription:** Transposons do not replace RNA polymerase. Their movement uses transposition machinery, and insertion can alter nearby gene expression. Transcription of cellular and transposon genes still uses RNA polymerase.

**D. Are only found in eukaryotic genomes and not in prokaryotes:** Transposons are found in BOTH prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Barbara McClintock discovered them in eukaryotes (maize), but they are abundant in bacteria as well. Bacterial insertion sequences (IS elements) are the simplest transposons.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Simple transposons (IS elements) = transposase gene flanked by inverted repeats
  - Composite transposons carry additional genes, often antibiotic resistance, between two insertion sequences; other transposons can carry accessory genes without that arrangement
  - Many transposons generate short target-site duplications when they insert
  - Barbara McClintock received the 1983 Nobel Prize for discovering transposons

>> Bottom Line: Transposons are mobile genetic elements that can move within genomes and often carry antibiotic resistance genes in bacteria.

In bacterial transformation, competence refers to:

  A) the ability of a bacteriophage to integrate its DNA into the host chromosome
  B) the ability of a transposon to move between chromosomal locations
  C) the ability of a plasmid to replicate independently of the chromosome
  D) the ability of a bacterium to take up exogenous DNA from its environment

### Correct Answer: the ability of a bacterium to take up exogenous DNA from its environment

Competence is the physiological state in which a bacterial cell can take up naked (free) DNA from the surrounding environment. Some bacteria are naturally competent (e.g., Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Bacillus subtilis), possessing specialized surface proteins that bind and import DNA. Other bacteria can be made artificially competent in the laboratory using CaCl₂ treatment, heat shock, or electroporation.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. The ability of a bacteriophage to integrate its DNA into the host chromosome:** This describes integration of phage DNA into a bacterial chromosome, as occurs when lambda establishes a prophage. Competence instead concerns the bacterium's ability to take up free DNA from its surroundings.

**B. The ability of a transposon to move between chromosomal locations:** Transposition is mediated by the transposase enzyme encoded by the transposon itself. This is a property of the mobile genetic element, not a state of the bacterial cell, and it has nothing to do with competence for transformation.

**C. The ability of a plasmid to replicate independently of the chromosome:** A plasmid's replication origin allows its DNA to be replicated as a separate replicon. Competence refers to DNA uptake, not to how DNA subsequently replicates. A competent bacterium may take up plasmid DNA during transformation.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Griffith's experiment (1928): material from heat-killed smooth bacteria transformed living rough Streptococcus pneumoniae into virulent smooth bacteria
  - Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty (1944): identified the transforming principle as DNA
  - Laboratory methods to induce competence: CaCl₂ + heat shock, electroporation
  - Only a small fraction of cells in a population may be naturally competent at any given time

>> Bottom Line: Competence is the ability of a bacterial cell to take up free DNA from its environment, enabling the process of transformation.

The lac operon contains three structural genes. Which of the following correctly identifies these genes and their products?

  A) lacI (repressor), lacO (operator), lacP (promoter)
  B) lacR (repressor), lacT (transacetylase), lacB (β-galactosidase)
  C) lacZ (permease), lacY (β-galactosidase), lacA (repressor)
  D) lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease), lacA (transacetylase)

### Correct Answer: lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease), lacA (transacetylase)

The three structural genes of the lac operon are: (1) lacZ, encoding β-galactosidase - the enzyme that cleaves lactose into glucose and galactose; (2) lacY, encoding lactose permease - a membrane transport protein that brings lactose into the cell; and (3) lacA, encoding thiogalactoside transacetylase - which transfers an acetyl group to galactosides (its exact physiological role is less clear but may be involved in detoxification).

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. lacI (repressor), lacO (operator), lacP (promoter):** lacI, lacO, and lacP are indeed part of the lac operon system, but they are NOT structural genes. lacI is the regulatory gene (encodes the repressor protein), lacO is the operator (a DNA sequence, not a gene that encodes a protein), and lacP is the promoter (where RNA polymerase binds). Structural genes encode proteins with enzymatic or structural functions.

**B. lacR (repressor), lacT (transacetylase), lacB (β-galactosidase):** These gene names are incorrect. The lac operon genes are designated with specific letters: Z (β-galactosidase), Y (permease), A (transacetylase), and I (repressor). There are no genes named lacR, lacT, or lacB in the standard lac operon nomenclature.

**C. lacZ (permease), lacY (β-galactosidase), lacA (repressor):** This switches the products of lacZ and lacY. lacZ encodes β-galactosidase (not permease), and lacY encodes permease (not β-galactosidase). Additionally, the repressor is encoded by lacI, not lacA.

### Key Educational Points:

  - All three structural genes are transcribed as a single polycistronic mRNA
  - β-galactosidase also converts lactose to allolactose - the actual inducer of the operon
  - The lacI gene is transcribed constitutively from its own promoter, separate from the lac operon structural genes

>> Bottom Line: The lac operon's three structural genes are lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease), and lacA (transacetylase), transcribed as one polycistronic mRNA.

In negative transcriptional regulation of a bacterial gene, what is the role of a repressor?

  A) An activator protein binds the promoter to stimulate RNA polymerase binding
  B) Transcription is always on and cannot be turned off
  C) A repressor binds regulatory DNA and reduces transcription
  D) Small RNA molecules degrade mRNA before it can be translated

### Correct Answer: A repressor binds regulatory DNA and reduces transcription

Negative transcriptional regulation uses a repressor to reduce gene transcription. Binding of the repressor to regulatory DNA can interfere with productive transcription by RNA polymerase. Whether the repressor is active depends on the regulatory system and its signals. For example, allolactose reduces lac-repressor activity, whereas tryptophan helps activate the trp repressor.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. An activator protein binds the promoter to stimulate RNA polymerase binding:** This describes positive gene regulation. An activator enhances transcription, as the CAP-cAMP complex does when it binds upstream of the lac operon promoter while glucose is low. Positive regulation does not mean a gene has no expression at all without its activator.

**B. Transcription is always on and cannot be turned off:** A gene that cannot respond to regulatory signals does not illustrate repression. Negative regulation allows a repressor to reduce transcription under particular conditions.

**D. Small RNA molecules degrade mRNA before it can be translated:** Small RNAs can reduce protein production by interfering with translation or promoting mRNA degradation. That is regulation after an RNA has been made, rather than the repressor-mediated control of transcription asked about here.

### Key Educational Points:

  | Type | Mechanism | Example |
  | Negative inducible | Repressor reduces transcription; inducer lowers repressor activity | lac operon (allolactose = inducer) |
  | Negative repressible | Repressor alone is inactive; corepressor activates repressor | trp operon (tryptophan = corepressor) |
  | Positive regulation | Activator enhances transcription | CAP-cAMP at lac operon |

>> Bottom Line: Negative transcriptional regulation uses a repressor to reduce transcription; the signal controlling the repressor differs between systems.

Q15  [bio-487]  Answer: D
==============================================================================
An Hfr (High frequency recombination) strain of E. coli is formed when:

  A) a bacterium acquires multiple copies of the same plasmid
  B) the bacterial cell loses its cell wall and becomes a protoplast
  C) a mutation inactivates the DNA mismatch repair system
  D) the F plasmid integrates into the bacterial chromosome

### Correct Answer: the F plasmid integrates into the bacterial chromosome

An Hfr strain is created when the F plasmid recombines into the bacterial chromosome via homologous recombination at IS (insertion sequence) elements shared between the F plasmid and chromosome. When an Hfr cell conjugates with an F− cell, transfer begins at an origin within the integrated F sequence. Chromosomal DNA is transferred before the remaining F sequence can arrive, and the connection usually breaks before the whole chromosome and complete F sequence are transferred. The recipient therefore usually remains F−.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. A bacterium acquires multiple copies of the same plasmid:** Having multiple copies of a plasmid is called high copy number and is determined by the plasmid's origin of replication, not by chromosomal integration. Nondisjunction during meiosis I versus meiosis II produces different patterns of aneuploidy. If nondisjunction occurs during meiosis I, what is the result?

  A) Two normal gametes and two aneuploid gametes
  B) Only one gamete is aneuploid; the other three are normal
  C) All four gametes are normal
  D) All four gametes are aneuploid - two with an extra chromosome and two missing a chromosome

### Correct Answer: All four gametes are aneuploid - two with an extra chromosome and two missing a chromosome

When nondisjunction occurs in meiosis I, homologous chromosomes fail to separate during anaphase I. This means one secondary cell receives both homologs and the other receives none. After meiosis II proceeds normally, the result is four aneuploid gametes: two gametes with n+1 chromosomes and two gametes with n−1 chromosomes. None of the four gametes has a normal chromosome number.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Two normal gametes and two aneuploid gametes:** This pattern occurs when nondisjunction happens in meiosis II, not meiosis I. In meiosis II nondisjunction, only one of the two secondary cells is affected. The unaffected cell produces two normal gametes, while the affected cell produces one n+1 and one n−1 gamete.

**B. Only one gamete is aneuploid; the other three are normal:** Nondisjunction always affects at least two gametes. The "lost" chromosome material from one cell must end up in another, so there are always complementary aneuploid products (n+1 paired with n−1).

**C. All four gametes are normal:** If nondisjunction occurred, at least some gametes must be aneuploid. Normal gamete production requires proper chromosome segregation at both meiotic divisions.

### Key Educational Points:

  | Feature | Nondisjunction in Meiosis I | Nondisjunction in Meiosis II |
  | What fails to separate | Homologous chromosomes | Sister chromatids |
  | Aneuploid gametes | 4 out of 4 (all) | 2 out of 4 |
  | Normal gametes | 0 | 2 |

>> Bottom Line: Nondisjunction in meiosis I produces four aneuploid gametes (all abnormal), while nondisjunction in meiosis II produces two normal and two aneuploid gametes.

Two healthy parents each carry one copy of a recessive allele for cystic fibrosis (both are heterozygous carriers, Cc). What is the probability that their next child will be affected?

  A) 0% - both parents are healthy
  B) 25%
  C) 50%
  D) 75%

### Correct Answer: 25%

When both parents are heterozygous carriers (Cc × Cc), the Punnett square gives the following offspring ratios: 1/4 CC (homozygous normal), 2/4 Cc (carrier), and 1/4 cc (affected). Therefore, there is a 25% (1 in 4) chance that any given child will be affected with cystic fibrosis.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. 0% - both parents are healthy:** Being healthy does not mean both parents cannot produce affected children. Carriers of autosomal recessive diseases are phenotypically normal because one functioning copy of the gene generally supplies enough function to prevent the disease. However, if both parents pass their recessive allele to a child, that child will be homozygous recessive (cc) and affected.

**C. 50%:** 50% is the probability that a child will be a CARRIER (Cc), not affected. A 50% risk of being affected would apply if one parent were affected (cc) and the other a carrier (Cc) - in that cross, 50% would be cc (affected) and 50% Cc (carrier).

**D. 75%:** 75% represents the fraction of offspring that are phenotypically normal (CC + Cc) in a Cc × Cc cross. This is the chance of being UNAFFECTED, not affected. It is a common mistake to confuse the affected and unaffected proportions.

### Key Educational Points:

  - An autosomal recessive disorder generally requires disease-causing variants in both copies of the relevant gene. The variants may be the same or different. The Cc notation is a simplified model for this cross
  - Carrier × Carrier → 25% affected, 50% carriers, 25% homozygous normal
  - Carrier frequency varies between populations; the parents' stated carrier status, not a population estimate, determines the answer here
  - Each pregnancy is an independent event - prior children's status does not change the 25% probability

>> Bottom Line: When both parents are carriers of an autosomal recessive disease, each child has a 25% chance of being affected.

A pedigree shows an affected father who has ALL affected daughters and NO affected sons. The mother is unaffected. What is the most likely inheritance pattern?

  A) Autosomal recessive
  B) Autosomal dominant
  C) X-linked dominant
  D) X-linked recessive

### Correct Answer: X-linked dominant

Under the usual fully penetrant Mendelian model, an affected father with an X-linked dominant trait passes his single X chromosome to every daughter, so all of his daughters are affected (X^DY father × X^dX^d mother → daughters are all X^DX^d and affected). None of his sons is affected, because sons receive his Y chromosome rather than his X (sons are all X^dY).

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Autosomal recessive:** If the father is affected (aa) and the mother is unaffected, the children would depend on the mother's genotype. If the mother is AA, no children would be affected. If she is Aa, 50% would be carriers and 50% affected. Autosomal inheritance does not predict this systematic difference between daughters and sons, although a small family could show such a pattern by chance.

**B. Autosomal dominant:** An affected autosomal dominant father (Aa) crossed with an unaffected mother (aa) is expected to produce 50% affected and 50% unaffected children, regardless of sex. That does not predict the systematic daughter-versus-son difference described, although a small family could show it by chance.

**D. X-linked recessive:** For an X-linked recessive condition, an affected father (X^aY) and a noncarrier mother (X^AX^A) have carrier daughters and unaffected sons in the standard model. If the mother is a carrier (X^AX^a), each daughter has a 50% chance of being affected and a 50% chance of being a carrier; each son has a 50% chance of being affected. That does not predict all daughters affected and all sons unaffected.

### Key Educational Points:

  - X-linked dominant: affected father → ALL daughters affected, NO sons affected
  - A heterozygous mother with an X-linked dominant variant has a 50% chance of transmitting it to each child; under the fully penetrant model, either sex can be affected
  - X-linked hypophosphatemia is an example of an X-linked dominant disorder

>> Bottom Line: An affected father with ALL affected daughters and NO affected sons indicates X-linked dominant inheritance - every daughter gets his single X chromosome.

An earthquake reduces an island lizard population to 10% of its original size. Survival is unrelated to inherited differences among the lizards, and the survivors have different allele frequencies from the original population. This is an example of:

  A) Gene flow
  B) Directional selection
  C) The bottleneck effect
  D) Assortative mating

### Correct Answer: The bottleneck effect

The bottleneck effect is a type of genetic drift that occurs when a population is dramatically reduced in size by a catastrophic event (earthquake, flood, disease epidemic, fire). The surviving population is a small, random sample of the original and may have significantly different allele frequencies - not because of any selective advantage, but due to chance (random sampling error). Genetic diversity is typically reduced after a bottleneck.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Gene flow:** Gene flow is the transfer of alleles between populations through migration. In this scenario, no migration is occurring - the population is simply being reduced in size. Gene flow requires movement of individuals (or their gametes) between distinct populations.

**B. Directional selection:** Directional selection favors individuals at one extreme of a phenotypic range, shifting the population mean over time. The stem specifies that survival was unrelated to inherited differences among the lizards, so the change in allele frequencies is due to chance (drift) rather than selection.

**D. Assortative mating:** Assortative mating is a form of nonrandom mating where individuals with similar phenotypes mate preferentially (positive assortative mating) or dissimilar individuals mate (negative assortative mating). The earthquake scenario does not involve mating patterns.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Bottleneck effect = drastic population reduction, which often reduces genetic diversity
  - Founder effect = small group colonizes new area → limited genetic diversity
  - Both are types of genetic drift - random changes in allele frequency due to small population size
  - Real examples: Northern elephant seal bottleneck, cheetah genetic uniformity

>> Bottom Line: A catastrophic reduction in population size causing random changes in allele frequencies is the bottleneck effect - a form of genetic drift.

Inbreeding depression in a population occurs because inbreeding:

  A) Introduces new mutations at a higher rate than outcrossing
  B) Increases the frequency of heterozygotes, masking beneficial recessive alleles
  C) Increases homozygosity, exposing harmful recessive alleles that reduce fitness
  D) Causes chromosomal inversions and translocations

### Correct Answer: Increases homozygosity, exposing harmful recessive alleles that reduce fitness

Inbreeding (mating between close relatives) increases the proportion of homozygous genotypes in a population because related individuals are more likely to share alleles inherited from a common ancestor. As homozygosity increases, deleterious recessive alleles that were hidden in the heterozygous state become expressed. This leads to inbreeding depression - reduced survival, fertility, and overall fitness in the population.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Introduces new mutations at a higher rate than outcrossing:** Inbreeding does NOT increase the mutation rate. It changes genotype frequencies (more homozygotes, fewer heterozygotes) without introducing new alleles. The mutation rate is determined by DNA replication fidelity and repair mechanisms, not by mating patterns.

**B. Increases the frequency of heterozygotes, masking beneficial recessive alleles:** This is exactly backwards. Inbreeding DECREASES heterozygosity and INCREASES homozygosity. Outcrossing (mating between unrelated individuals) maintains or increases heterozygosity. The loss of heterozygosity is the fundamental genetic consequence of inbreeding.

**D. Causes chromosomal inversions and translocations:** Chromosomal rearrangements are caused by DNA breakage and repair errors, exposure to mutagens, or errors during recombination. They are structural mutations unrelated to mating patterns. Inbreeding does not cause structural chromosomal changes.

### Key Educational Points:

  - The inbreeding coefficient F describes the probability that an individual's two alleles at a locus are identical by descent
  - Under the simple assumption of otherwise unrelated, non-inbred ancestors, offspring of first cousins have an expected F of 1/16, and offspring of a full-sibling mating an expected F of 1/4. These are values for the offspring, not the proportion of alleles shared between the parents
  - Inbreeding depression is a major concern in conservation biology for endangered species with small populations
  - Some crosses between genetically different lines show hybrid vigor (heterosis), partly because heterozygosity can mask harmful recessive variants. Such a fitness improvement is not guaranteed for every cross

>> Bottom Line: Inbreeding depression results from increased homozygosity that unmasks deleterious recessive alleles, reducing population fitness.

Assortative mating refers to:

  A) Individuals choosing mates at random from the population
  B) Individuals preferentially mating with others who have similar (or dissimilar) phenotypes
  C) Males competing physically for access to females
  D) Organisms reproducing asexually to maintain their genotype

### Correct Answer: Individuals preferentially mating with others who have similar (or dissimilar) phenotypes

Assortative mating is a form of nonrandom mating. Positive assortative mating favors partners with similar phenotypes, such as taller people pairing with taller partners; negative assortative mating favors dissimilar phenotypes. For genetically influenced traits, these patterns can change genotype frequencies at relevant loci. In a simple model with equal reproductive contributions and no selection, rearranging mating pairs can alter genotype frequencies without changing allele frequencies. In real populations, differences in mating success can also produce selection.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Individuals choosing mates at random from the population:** Random mating (panmixia) is one of the assumptions of Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. Assortative mating is specifically NON-random mating - it involves a preference for certain phenotypes. Random mating and assortative mating are opposite concepts.

**C. Males competing physically for access to females:** This describes intrasexual selection (male-male competition), a form of sexual selection. While sexual selection and assortative mating both represent nonrandom mating, they involve different mechanisms. Intrasexual selection is about competition, while assortative mating is about mate preference based on phenotypic similarity or dissimilarity.

**D. Organisms reproducing asexually to maintain their genotype:** Asexual reproduction (e.g., binary fission, budding, parthenogenesis) does not involve mate choice at all. Assortative mating is a concept that applies exclusively to sexually reproducing organisms making mate choices based on phenotype.

### Key Educational Points:

  - In the simplified model, positive assortative mating can increase homozygosity at loci related to the mating trait; it works through phenotype rather than relatedness
  - Negative assortative mating can correspondingly increase heterozygosity at those same loci
  - Assortative mating violates the random mating assumption of Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium
  - Assortative mating concerns similarity in a trait; inbreeding concerns relatedness. They can overlap but are not the same process

>> Bottom Line: Assortative mating is nonrandom pairing by phenotype; its genetic effects depend on the trait and reproductive assumptions.
