Aldosterone acts on the principal cells of the collecting duct to regulate electrolyte balance. Which of the following BEST describes its primary effect?

  A) It promotes sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion
  B) It promotes potassium reabsorption and sodium secretion
  C) It inhibits ADH release from the posterior pituitary
  D) It directly increases glomerular filtration rate

### Correct Answer: It promotes sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion

Aldosterone is a mineralocorticoid produced by the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal cortex. It binds to intracellular receptors in principal cells of the collecting duct, upregulating the expression of epithelial sodium channels (ENaC) on the apical membrane and Na+/K+-ATPase on the basolateral membrane. This increases sodium reabsorption from the urine back into the blood and promotes potassium secretion into the tubular lumen.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. Promotes potassium reabsorption and sodium secretion:** This is the exact opposite of aldosterone's action. Aldosterone retains sodium and promotes potassium secretion, which is why hyperaldosteronism is often associated with potassium loss.

**C. Inhibits ADH release:** Aldosterone does not directly regulate ADH secretion. ADH release is controlled by plasma osmolarity (via hypothalamic osmoreceptors) and blood volume (via baroreceptors).

**D. Directly increases GFR:** Aldosterone does not directly affect GFR. GFR is primarily determined by the balance of hydrostatic and oncotic pressures across the glomerular capillaries and by afferent/efferent arteriole tone.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Aldosterone: Na+ in, K+ out (at the collecting duct)
  - Excess aldosterone can cause hypertension, potassium loss and metabolic alkalosis; serum sodium does not necessarily rise

>> Bottom Line: Aldosterone promotes sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion in the collecting duct, regulating blood pressure and electrolyte balance.

Q2  [bio-538]  Answer: C
==============================================================================
Blood plasma constitutes approximately 55% of total blood volume. Which of the following is the MOST abundant protein in plasma?

  A) Fibrinogen
  B) Immunoglobulins (antibodies)
  C) Albumin
  D) Transferrin

### Correct Answer: Albumin

Albumin makes up approximately 60% of total plasma protein (normal concentration ~3.5-5.0 g/dL). It is synthesized by the liver and serves several vital functions: maintaining colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure, helping retain water within the circulation by opposing net fluid movement into the tissues, transporting hydrophobic molecules (fatty acids, bilirubin, hormones, drugs), and buffering blood pH.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Fibrinogen:** Fibrinogen is a clotting factor that constitutes about 4% of plasma proteins. It is converted to fibrin by thrombin during the clotting cascade. Serum is the liquid remaining after blood clots; fibrinogen is largely removed into the clot, but not every clotting factor disappears.

**B. Immunoglobulins (antibodies):** Immunoglobulins (gamma globulins) make up about 20% of plasma proteins. They are produced by plasma cells (differentiated B lymphocytes) and function in adaptive immunity.

**D. Transferrin:** Transferrin is a transport protein that carries iron (Fe3+) in the blood. It is present in much smaller quantities than albumin and belongs to the beta globulin fraction.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Plasma proteins (by abundance): albumin > globulins > fibrinogen
  - Low albumin (hypoalbuminemia) can contribute to edema through reduced oncotic pressure

>> Bottom Line: Albumin is the most abundant plasma protein, responsible for maintaining oncotic pressure and transporting various substances.

Which statement about mature human red blood cells (erythrocytes) is correct?

  A) They lack a nucleus and mitochondria, relying entirely on anaerobic glycolysis for ATP
  B) They contain a nucleus and mitochondria for aerobic respiration
  C) They are produced in the yellow bone marrow of long bones
  D) They have a lifespan of approximately 30 days

### Correct Answer: They lack a nucleus and mitochondria, relying entirely on anaerobic glycolysis for ATP

During maturation, the developing red cell expels its nucleus and removes mitochondria and other organelles. This also leaves more space for hemoglobin. Mature human red cells therefore make ATP through glycolysis and do not use mitochondria to consume the oxygen they carry. Their flexible, biconcave shape provides a large surface area for gas exchange and helps them pass through narrow capillaries.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. They contain a nucleus and mitochondria:** While most vertebrate RBCs (e.g., in birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish) retain their nucleus, mammalian RBCs are enucleated. This is a distinctive mammalian adaptation.

**C. Produced in yellow bone marrow:** RBCs are produced in red bone marrow (found in flat bones like the sternum, pelvis, and vertebrae in adults). Yellow bone marrow consists primarily of fat and is not normally involved in hematopoiesis unless demand increases.

**D. Lifespan of approximately 30 days:** The average lifespan of a human RBC is approximately 120 days. After this, they are removed from circulation by macrophages in the spleen and liver (reticuloendothelial system). Platelets have a lifespan of about 8-10 days.

### Key Educational Points:

  - RBCs lack nucleus, mitochondria, and other organelles to maximize hemoglobin content
  - Biconcave shape increases surface area-to-volume ratio for efficient gas exchange

>> Bottom Line: Mature mammalian RBCs are enucleated and lack mitochondria, generating energy solely through anaerobic glycolysis and maximizing space for hemoglobin.

A patient has a complete blood count (CBC) showing elevated eosinophil levels. This finding is MOST commonly associated with which condition?

  A) Bacterial pneumonia
  B) Viral hepatitis
  C) Iron deficiency anemia
  D) Parasitic helminth infection or allergic reaction

### Correct Answer: Parasitic helminth infection or allergic reaction

Eosinophils are granulocytic white blood cells that typically constitute 1-4% of circulating WBCs. Eosinophilia is associated particularly with tissue-invasive helminths and with allergic conditions, including allergic asthma, hay fever and eczema. Helminths are too large to be phagocytosed, so eosinophils release cytotoxic granule contents (major basic protein, eosinophilic cationic protein) onto the parasite surface instead.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Bacterial pneumonia:** Bacterial infections typically cause neutrophilia (elevated neutrophils), not eosinophilia. Neutrophils are the first responders to bacterial infection and are the most abundant WBC type.

**B. Viral hepatitis:** Some viral infections are associated with increased lymphocytes, although blood-count changes vary. Viral hepatitis is not the classic association with eosinophilia tested here; helminth infection and allergy are better matches.

**C. Iron deficiency anemia:** Iron deficiency anemia is characterized by microcytic, hypochromic RBCs and low hemoglobin. It does not specifically cause eosinophilia, though it may be accompanied by mild thrombocytosis (elevated platelets).

### Key Educational Points:

  - Typical patterns: neutrophils with bacteria, lymphocytes with viruses, eosinophils with parasites and allergy, basophils in allergic responses and monocytes in chronic inflammation. These are associations, not diagnostic rules
  - Eosinophilia has several possible causes, including allergy, some helminth infections and medication reactions; the clinical context matters

>> Bottom Line: Eosinophilia suggests an allergic disorder or certain helminth infections, especially those involving tissue migration, but is not diagnostic on its own.

Platelets (thrombocytes) play a critical role in hemostasis. Which statement about platelets is CORRECT?

  A) Platelets are cell fragments derived from megakaryocytes in the bone marrow
  B) Platelets are nucleated cells produced by neutrophils in the lymph nodes
  C) Platelets function primarily in adaptive immunity by producing antibodies
  D) Platelets have a lifespan of approximately 120 days, similar to red blood cells

### Correct Answer: Platelets are cell fragments derived from megakaryocytes in the bone marrow

Platelets are small, anucleate cell fragments (2-3 micrometers) that bud off from the cytoplasm of large precursor cells called megakaryocytes in the bone marrow. A single megakaryocyte can produce thousands of platelets. Platelets contain granules (dense granules with ADP and serotonin; alpha granules with clotting factors and growth factors) essential for hemostasis.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. Nucleated cells produced by neutrophils:** Platelets are not nucleated cells and are not produced by neutrophils. They are cytoplasmic fragments of megakaryocytes. Thrombopoietin (TPO), produced primarily by the liver, regulates megakaryocyte development.

**C. Function in adaptive immunity by producing antibodies:** Antibody production is the function of plasma cells (derived from B lymphocytes). Platelets function in hemostasis (clot formation), not adaptive immunity, though they do release some inflammatory mediators.

**D. Lifespan of approximately 120 days:** Platelet lifespan is approximately 8-10 days, much shorter than the 120-day lifespan of RBCs. Old platelets are removed primarily by the spleen.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Platelets: anucleate fragments from megakaryocytes; lifespan ~8-10 days
  - Normal count: 150,000-400,000 per microliter; regulated by thrombopoietin

>> Bottom Line: Platelets are anucleate cell fragments derived from megakaryocytes in the bone marrow, with a lifespan of 8-10 days.

In a healthy adult, where is erythropoietin (EPO) predominantly produced, and what stimulates its production?

  A) Produced in the liver; stimulated by high blood oxygen levels
  B) Produced in the bone marrow; stimulated by elevated iron levels
  C) Produced in the spleen; stimulated by elevated bilirubin levels
  D) Produced in the kidneys; stimulated by low blood oxygen levels (hypoxia)

### Correct Answer: Produced in the kidneys; stimulated by low blood oxygen levels (hypoxia)

EPO is produced primarily by peritubular interstitial fibroblasts in the renal cortex (about 90% of production; the liver contributes about 10%). When the kidneys detect reduced oxygen delivery (hypoxia), hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) transcription factors accumulate and upregulate EPO gene expression. EPO then travels to the bone marrow where it stimulates the proliferation and differentiation of erythroid progenitor cells into mature red blood cells.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Produced in the liver; stimulated by high oxygen:** The liver is the major EPO-producing organ during fetal life, whereas the kidneys are the main source in adults. Reduced oxygen availability, not high oxygen levels, stimulates EPO production.

**B. Produced in the bone marrow; stimulated by iron:** Bone marrow is the main site where EPO stimulates red-cell production, rather than its main source. Iron is required to make hemoglobin, but the principal stimulus for renal EPO production is reduced oxygen availability.

**C. Produced in the spleen; stimulated by bilirubin:** The spleen destroys old RBCs but does not produce EPO. Bilirubin is a breakdown product of heme and does not regulate erythropoiesis.

### Key Educational Points:

  - EPO production: kidneys (90%) > liver (10%)
  - Chronic kidney disease leads to anemia due to decreased EPO production

>> Bottom Line: EPO is produced by the kidneys in response to hypoxia and stimulates RBC production in the bone marrow.

The extrinsic pathway of the coagulation cascade is initiated by tissue damage. Which factor is the key trigger of the extrinsic pathway?

  A) Factor XII (Hageman factor)
  B) Thrombin (Factor IIa)
  C) Tissue factor (Factor III / thromboplastin)
  D) Fibrinogen (Factor I)

### Correct Answer: Tissue factor (Factor III / thromboplastin)

Vessel injury exposes tissue factor (TF, also called Factor III or thromboplastin) to blood. TF is a transmembrane protein expressed on subendothelial cells that is normally not in contact with blood. Tissue factor binds factor VII/VIIa; the active tissue factor-VIIa complex initiates coagulation by activating downstream clotting factors, beginning with factor X. The traditional intrinsic/extrinsic diagram is useful for understanding the laboratory tests, whereas tissue factor initiates most normal clotting in the body. PT assesses the extrinsic and common pathways; aPTT assesses the intrinsic and common pathways.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Factor XII (Hageman factor):** Factor XII initiates the intrinsic pathway (not the extrinsic pathway). It is activated when blood contacts negatively charged surfaces, and the aPTT assesses the intrinsic and common pathways. Contact activation through factor XII is the traditional intrinsic-pathway trigger used in the laboratory, but it is not required to initiate normal hemostasis in the body.

**B. Thrombin (Factor IIa):** Thrombin is the product of the common pathway, where Factor Xa converts prothrombin (Factor II) to thrombin. Thrombin then converts fibrinogen to fibrin. It is not the initiator of the extrinsic pathway.

**D. Fibrinogen (Factor I):** Fibrinogen is the substrate of thrombin and is converted into fibrin monomers that polymerize to form the clot meshwork. It is the final substrate in the common pathway, not the trigger of the extrinsic pathway.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Extrinsic pathway: tissue factor + factor VII; the PT/INR assesses the extrinsic and common pathways
  - Intrinsic pathway: factors XII, XI, IX and VIII; the aPTT assesses the intrinsic and common pathways

>> Bottom Line: Exposure of tissue factor allows formation of the tissue factor-VIIa complex, initiating the extrinsic pathway and downstream fibrin formation.

A patient has a prolonged aPTT but a normal PT. Factor testing shows reduced factor VIII activity, and genetic testing identifies a disease-causing variant in F8, the gene encoding factor VIII. Which condition does the patient have?

  A) Hemophilia A
  B) Hemophilia B (Christmas disease)
  C) Von Willebrand disease
  D) Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)

### Correct Answer: Hemophilia A

Hemophilia A is caused by a deficiency or dysfunction of clotting Factor VIII. It is the most common severe hereditary bleeding disorder, inherited in an X-linked recessive pattern. Factor VIII participates in the intrinsic coagulation pathway. aPTT assesses the intrinsic and common pathways; PT assesses the extrinsic and common pathways. Hemophilia A typically causes a prolonged aPTT with normal PT, but mild disease can have a normal aPTT.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. Hemophilia B (Christmas disease):** Hemophilia B is caused by deficiency of Factor IX (not Factor VIII). It is also X-linked recessive and clinically similar to Hemophilia A, but it is less common. It also typically causes a prolonged aPTT with normal PT.

**C. Von Willebrand disease:** Von Willebrand factor helps platelets adhere to an injured vessel and carries factor VIII in the blood. Its deficiency or dysfunction can therefore cause bleeding and sometimes reduced factor VIII activity. Here, the disease-causing variant is in F8 itself, identifying hemophilia A.

**D. Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC):** DIC involves widespread clotting and consumption of platelets and clotting factors; PT and aPTT are often prolonged. It does not explain the inherited F8 defect in this stem.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Hemophilia A = Factor VIII deficiency (X-linked); Hemophilia B = Factor IX deficiency (X-linked)
  - Hemophilia A and B typically produce a prolonged aPTT with a normal PT and are distinguished by factor assays; mild disease can have a normal aPTT

>> Bottom Line: Hemophilia A is an X-linked disorder caused by reduced factor VIII activity. It typically prolongs aPTT with normal PT, although mild disease may have a normal aPTT.

A mother with blood type O (genotype ii) and a father with blood type AB (genotype IA IB) have a child. What are the possible blood types of their offspring?

  A) Only type O
  B) Type A, type B, or type AB
  C) Type A or type B only
  D) Type A, type B, type AB, or type O

### Correct Answer: Type A or type B only

The mother (ii) can only contribute the i allele. The father (I^A I^B) can contribute either I^A or I^B. Therefore, the possible offspring genotypes are I^A i (blood type A) or I^B i (blood type B). There is a 50% chance of each. No offspring can be type O (would need ii) or type AB (would need I^A I^B, but the mother has no I^A or I^B to contribute).

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Only type O:** Type O requires the homozygous genotype ii. The father has no i allele to contribute (he is I^A I^B), so type O is impossible in this cross.

**B. Type A, type B, or type AB:** Type AB requires the genotype I^A I^B. Since the mother can only contribute i alleles, no child can receive both I^A and I^B. Type AB is not possible.

**D. Type A, type B, type AB, or type O:** Neither type O nor type AB is possible from this cross, as explained above.

### Key Educational Points:

  - ABO blood type demonstrates codominance (IA and IB are codominant) and complete dominance (both IA and IB are dominant over i)
  - Cross: ii x IA IB produces 50% IA i (type A) and 50% IB i (type B)

>> Bottom Line: A type O mother (ii) and type AB father (IA IB) can only produce children with type A or type B blood.

What is the key difference between blood serum and blood plasma?

  A) Serum contains clotting factors while plasma does not
  B) Plasma retains fibrinogen, whereas serum is collected after clotting and is depleted of fibrinogen
  C) Serum contains red blood cells while plasma does not
  D) Plasma is obtained after blood clotting while serum is obtained before

### Correct Answer: Plasma retains fibrinogen, whereas serum is collected after clotting and is depleted of fibrinogen

Plasma is the liquid portion obtained when blood is prevented from clotting, usually by an anticoagulant in the collection tube. It retains fibrinogen. Serum is the liquid remaining after blood has clotted and the clot has been removed. Fibrinogen is largely converted into fibrin in the clot, and some other clotting components are consumed or altered. Serum is not devoid of every clotting factor.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Serum contains clotting factors while plasma does not:** This reverses the distinction. Anticoagulated plasma retains fibrinogen; serum is collected after clotting and is depleted of it. The difference should not be described as all clotting factors being present in one and absent from the other.

**C. Serum contains red blood cells:** Neither serum nor plasma contains red blood cells. Both are obtained after separating the cellular components by centrifugation.

**D. Plasma is obtained after blood clotting:** This reverses the definitions. Plasma is obtained from unclotted blood (anticoagulant added), while serum is obtained after the blood has been allowed to clot.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Plasma = liquid from anticoagulated blood (contains fibrinogen)
  - Serum = liquid from clotted blood, depleted of fibrinogen, with some other clotting components consumed or altered

>> Bottom Line: Plasma is obtained from anticoagulated blood and retains fibrinogen; serum is obtained after clotting and is depleted of fibrinogen.

Q11  [bio-548]  Answer: C
==============================================================================
The fossil known as 'Lucy,' discovered in Ethiopia in 1974, is classified as a member of which hominin species?

  A) Homo habilis
  B) Homo erectus
  C) Australopithecus afarensis
  D) Homo neanderthalensis

### Correct Answer: Australopithecus afarensis

Lucy is a 3.2-million-year-old partial skeleton discovered by Donald Johanson and Tom Gray in Hadar, Ethiopia. She belongs to the species Australopithecus afarensis and represents one of the most complete early hominin fossils ever found (approximately 40% complete). Lucy's skeleton shows clear evidence of bipedalism (upright walking) based on her knee joint angle, pelvic structure, and spinal curvature, while her brain size remained relatively small (~400-500 cm3), similar to modern chimpanzees.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Homo habilis:** Homo habilis ("handy man") appeared approximately 2.4-1.5 million years ago, well after Lucy, and had a larger brain. It is traditionally associated with Oldowan stone tools, but it is not established as the first stone-tool maker. Lucy belongs to an earlier australopith species.

**B. Homo erectus:** Homo erectus lived later and is associated with dispersal beyond Africa and more modern human-like body proportions. It is not Lucy's species.

**D. Homo neanderthalensis:** Neanderthals lived approximately 400,000-40,000 years ago in Europe and western Asia. They had brain sizes comparable to or larger than modern humans and are far too recent to be Lucy's species.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Australopithecus afarensis combined habitual bipedalism with a relatively small brain and adaptations for climbing
  - Lucy demonstrates that bipedalism evolved before significant brain enlargement

>> Bottom Line: Lucy belongs to Australopithecus afarensis, demonstrating that bipedalism preceded the evolution of larger brains in hominins.

The name Homo habilis, meaning "handy man," reflects its traditional association with which activity?

  A) The first evidence of controlled fire use
  B) The first migration out of Africa
  C) The first evidence of symbolic art and burial rituals
  D) Manufacture of Oldowan stone tools

### Correct Answer: Manufacture of Oldowan stone tools

Homo habilis was named "handy man" because of its traditional association with Oldowan tools. These include cores and sharp flakes made by striking stone. This association does not establish H. habilis as the first or only toolmaker: stone-tool evidence predates its known fossil record, and the makers of the earliest tools remain uncertain.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. The first evidence of controlled fire use:** Controlled fire use is not the association behind the name Homo habilis. Evidence for early fire use and the identity of the hominins responsible must be evaluated separately from stone-tool manufacture.

**B. The first migration out of Africa:** Early Homo fossils document dispersal beyond Africa, but that is not the basis of the name Homo habilis. The name refers to the species' traditional association with making tools.

**C. The first symbolic art and burial rituals:** Symbolic art and deliberate burial are different archaeological behaviors. They are not the discoveries that led to the name Homo habilis, and they should not be combined into one confidently dated "first" event.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Homo habilis means "handy man" and is traditionally associated with Oldowan tools
  - An association with tools does not prove that a species invented them or was their only maker

>> Bottom Line: Homo habilis is traditionally associated with Oldowan toolmaking, not securely identified as the first stone-tool maker.

Homo neanderthalensis (Neanderthals) coexisted with early Homo sapiens in Europe. Genetic evidence has revealed that:

  A) Many people with ancestry outside Africa carry roughly 1-2% Neanderthal-derived DNA
  B) Neanderthals and modern humans never interbred
  C) Neanderthals had significantly smaller brains than Homo sapiens
  D) Neanderthals were the direct ancestors of all modern humans

### Correct Answer: Many people with ancestry outside Africa carry roughly 1-2% Neanderthal-derived DNA

Genome sequencing of Neanderthal fossils revealed that interbreeding occurred between Neanderthals and anatomically modern humans. That interbreeding contributed ancestry to present-day populations: many people with ancestry outside Africa carry roughly 1-2% Neanderthal-derived DNA, and the proportion varies among individuals and populations. Migration back into Africa and subsequent gene flow contributed Neanderthal-derived segments there as well, so continental labels should not be treated as genetically sealed categories.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**B. Neanderthals and modern humans never interbred:** Genomic evidence clearly demonstrates hybridization between Neanderthals and H. sapiens. Functional Neanderthal gene variants persist in modern humans, affecting immune function, skin pigmentation, and other traits.

**C. Neanderthals had significantly smaller brains:** Neanderthal brains were actually comparable in size to or slightly larger than modern human brains (~1200-1750 cm3). However, brain shape differed, with Neanderthals having more elongated crania and modern humans having more globular brains. A larger brain volume alone does not establish greater intelligence.

**D. Neanderthals were the direct ancestors of all modern humans:** Neanderthals were not the sole direct ancestors of all modern humans. They were a related hominin lineage that contributed some ancestry through interbreeding, sharing a common ancestor with H. sapiens roughly 500,000-700,000 years ago.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Neanderthals: ~400,000-40,000 years ago, Europe and western Asia
  - Interbreeding contributed roughly 1-2% Neanderthal-derived DNA in many people with ancestry outside Africa, with the proportion varying between individuals and populations

>> Bottom Line: Genetic evidence shows Neanderthals interbred with Homo sapiens, contributing roughly 1-2% Neanderthal-derived DNA to many present-day people with ancestry outside Africa.

Fossils assigned to early Homo sapiens date to approximately:

  C) 300,000 years ago in Africa

Fossils from Jebel Irhoud in Morocco, dated to about 300,000 years ago, are assigned to early Homo sapiens. They combine relatively modern facial features with a more elongated braincase. The Omo I remains from Ethiopia are older than an overlying volcanic layer dated to approximately 233,000 years ago, which is a minimum age rather than an exact age for the individual. ### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. 5-7 million years ago:** This timeframe corresponds to the estimated divergence of the human and chimpanzee lineages, not the emergence of Homo sapiens. The earliest hominins (e.g., Sahelanthropus tchadensis, Ardipithecus) date to this period.

**D. 50,000 years ago in Europe:** Approximately 50,000-70,000 years ago marks an important later expansion that contributed much of the ancestry of people outside Africa. It is neither the first appearance of our species nor the first possible dispersal beyond Africa.

### Key Educational Points:

  - H. sapiens originated in Africa approximately 300,000 years ago
  - An important later expansion around 50,000-70,000 years ago contributed much of the ancestry of people outside Africa

>> Bottom Line: Early Homo sapiens fossils from Africa are about 300,000 years old; modern human traits developed over time.

Which of the following is considered a key advantage of bipedalism (upright walking) in early hominins?

  A) It increased speed compared to quadrupedal locomotion in all terrains
  B) It freed the hands for tool use and carrying objects while moving
  C) It eliminated the need for a large brain
  D) It provided better camouflage in forest environments

### Correct Answer: It freed the hands for tool use and carrying objects while moving

One of the most significant advantages of bipedalism is that it frees the upper limbs from locomotion, allowing early hominins to carry food, tools, and offspring while walking. Other proposed advantages include reduced surface area exposed to the tropical sun (thermoregulation), the ability to see over tall grasses (predator detection), and more energy-efficient long-distance travel compared to quadrupedal ape locomotion. An advantage of a trait does not by itself establish why the trait first evolved.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Increased speed compared to quadrupedal locomotion:** Walking upright does not provide superior speed on every terrain, and bipedal humans are slower than many quadrupedal mammals of similar size in a sprint. The proposed advantages centre on efficient long-distance walking rather than top speed.

**C. Eliminated the need for a large brain:** Bipedalism did not eliminate a need for brain function, nor did it require a modern human-sized brain. Early bipedal hominins already walked upright while retaining relatively small brains.

**D. Better camouflage in forest environments:** Standing upright does not provide a camouflage advantage and may make an organism more visible. Early bipeds occupied a range of settings, including wooded ones, so a single open-savanna origin story is not established.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Bipedalism preceded brain enlargement by millions of years
  - Proposed additional advantages include energy-efficient travel, thermoregulation and improved sight lines

>> Bottom Line: Bipedalism freed the hands for tool use and carrying, and offered energy-efficient locomotion, representing a key early hominin adaptation.

Over the course of hominin evolution, cranial capacity has generally increased. Which of the following correctly lists hominin species in order of INCREASING average brain size?

  A) Homo sapiens, Homo erectus, Australopithecus afarensis, Homo habilis
  B) Homo habilis, Australopithecus afarensis, Homo sapiens, Homo erectus
  C) Homo erectus, Homo habilis, Australopithecus afarensis, Homo sapiens
  D) Australopithecus afarensis, Homo habilis, Homo erectus, Homo sapiens

### Correct Answer: Australopithecus afarensis, Homo habilis, Homo erectus, Homo sapiens

Among these four taxa, the usual ordering of approximate average cranial capacity is Australopithecus afarensis, Homo habilis, Homo erectus, then Homo sapiens. There was substantial variation within taxa and overlap between some of their ranges. This comparison is not a claim that each species directly evolved into the next.

During accommodation for near vision, the ciliary muscle contracts (via parasympathetic stimulation), which reduces the diameter of the ciliary ring. ### Key Educational Points:

  - S. haematobium (bladder) vs. S. mansoni (liver/intestine) vs. S. japonicum (liver/intestine, Asia)
  - Chronic S. haematobium infection is a risk factor for squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder
  - Cercaria = penetrates human skin; Miracidium = infects snails; both are larval stages of schistosomes

>> Bottom Line: Freshwater exposure with hematuria suggests S. haematobium. Cercariae infect humans through the skin; eggs drive chronic inflammation, and longstanding infection increases the risk of bladder squamous cell carcinoma.

A 6-year-old girl presents with intense perianal itching that is worse at night. Her mother is instructed to apply adhesive tape to the child's perianal region first thing in the morning for diagnosis. Which parasite is being investigated with this technique?

  A) Ascaris lumbricoides
  B) Trichuris trichiura
  C) Enterobius vermicularis
  D) Ancylostoma duodenale

### Correct Answer: Enterobius vermicularis

**Enterobius vermicularis** (pinworm) is the most common helminth in developed countries, particularly affecting school-age children. The characteristic symptom is **intense perianal pruritus**, worse at night, caused by female worms migrating to the perianal area to deposit eggs. It strongly suggests pinworm but is not exclusive to it. The **Scotch tape (cellophane tape) test** is the classic diagnostic method: transparent adhesive tape is pressed against the perianal skin first thing in the morning, before bathing or using the toilet, to collect eggs, which are then examined microscopically.

The eggs are characteristically **D-shaped (plano-convex)**. Routine stool examination often misses pinworm because the eggs are mainly deposited around the anus rather than passed in stool.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Ascaris lumbricoides:** Ascaris eggs are passed in the stool, not deposited perianally. Diagnosis is by identification of characteristic fertilized eggs (round with mammillated outer coat) in stool examination. Ascaris does not cause perianal itching - it causes abdominal discomfort or, in heavy infections, intestinal obstruction.

**B. Trichuris trichiura:** Trichuris (whipworm) resides in the cecum and large intestine, with its thin anterior end threaded into the mucosa. Diagnosis is by finding characteristic barrel-shaped (lemon-shaped) eggs with bipolar plugs in stool. It causes bloody diarrhea and rectal prolapse in heavy infections, not perianal pruritus.

**D. Ancylostoma duodenale:** Ancylostoma duodenale is a hookworm that usually enters through skin penetration by infective larvae; oral acquisition of larvae can also occur. Adults attach to small-intestinal mucosa and can cause iron-deficiency anemia. Its eggs are sought in stool, whereas the pinworm tape test collects eggs deposited on perianal skin.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Infection usually follows swallowing eggs transferred from contaminated hands or objects. Rarely, airborne eggs are inhaled and then swallowed
  - Entire families should be treated simultaneously because reinfection within households is common
  - Treatment: mebendazole or albendazole (single dose, repeated in 2 weeks); treat the whole household

>> Bottom Line: Nocturnal perianal itching suggests pinworm. Morning perianal tape sampling is more useful than routine stool examination because eggs are mainly laid on the surrounding skin.
