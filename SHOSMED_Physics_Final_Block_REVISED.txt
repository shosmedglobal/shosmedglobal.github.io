Q1  [phys-214]  Lorentz Force  correct answer: B   (KEEP: a content-exact repeat of approved batch 4 Q34, frozen, adds no coverage)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An electron enters a region with an electric field E pointing downward and a magnetic field B pointing into the page. The electron moves to the right. For it to travel in a straight line without deflection, the field magnitudes must satisfy:

  A. E = B
  B. E = vB   <= key
  C. E = v/B
  D. E = qvB

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: E = vB
The electron is negatively charged, so the downward electric field exerts an upward electric force. For motion to the right with B into the page, v × B points upward; the electron's magnetic force therefore points downward. The forces cancel when their magnitudes are equal: eE = evB, giving E = vB or v = E/B. Equal magnitudes alone would not be enough if the forces pointed in the same direction.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. E = B: This ignores the velocity and has mismatched units (V/m vs T).
  - C. E = v/B: This inverts the relationship. Rearranging gives v = EB, which is wrong.
  - D. E = qvB: This keeps the charge in the equation, but it cancels when equating forces (qE = qvB).
Key Concept: A velocity selector requires electric and magnetic forces that are opposite in direction as well as equal in magnitude. With the fields arranged correctly, particles traveling in the selected direction pass undeflected at speed v = E/B.

Q2  [phys-241]  Semiconductors - Half-Wave Rectifier  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In a circuit with one ideal diode, a resistive load and no smoothing capacitor, a half-wave rectifier converts a sinusoidal AC input to a pulsating DC output by:

  A. Allowing both positive and negative half-cycles to pass through
  B. Allowing only one half-cycle (positive or negative) to pass while blocking the other   <= key
  C. Doubling the frequency of the input signal
  D. Inverting the negative half-cycle to produce a smooth constant voltage

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Allowing only one half-cycle (positive or negative) to pass while blocking the other
A half-wave rectifier uses a single diode to convert AC to pulsating DC. During the forward-biased half-cycle, the diode conducts and current flows through the load. During the reverse-biased half-cycle, the diode blocks current and the output is zero. The result is a pulsating waveform that contains only positive (or only negative) half-cycles.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Allowing both positive and negative half-cycles to pass through: If both half-cycles passed, the output would still be AC, not DC. Allowing both half-cycles (by inverting one) describes a full-wave rectifier, which uses either a center-tapped transformer with two diodes or a bridge of four diodes to utilize both halves of the AC cycle.
C. Doubling the frequency of the input signal: A half-wave rectifier does not change the frequency. The output pulses have the same frequency as the input AC. It is the full-wave rectifier whose output pulses at twice the input frequency (because it converts both half-cycles into positive pulses).
D. Inverting the negative half-cycle to produce a smooth constant voltage: A half-wave rectifier does not invert the blocked half-cycle; it simply eliminates it (output = 0 during that time). Producing a smooth constant voltage requires additional filtering (typically a capacitor) after rectification. Even then, some ripple remains.
Key Educational Points:
  - A half-wave rectifier uses one half-cycle. For the same sinusoidal input peak, its average output magnitude is lower and its ripple is greater than for an ideal full-wave rectifier.
  - Without smoothing, the magnitude of the average output is V_DC = V_peak/π ≈ 0.318 × V_peak. The diode orientation determines whether the pulses are positive or negative.
  - A smoothing capacitor placed in parallel with the load reduces ripple by charging during the pulse and discharging during the blocked half-cycle.
Bottom Line: A half-wave rectifier passes one half-cycle and blocks the other. Its unfiltered output is unidirectional but pulsating; a smoothing capacitor can reduce the ripple.

Q3  [phys-242]  Semiconductors - Depletion Region  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: The depletion region at a PN junction forms because:

  A. External voltage is applied to the junction
  B. Majority carriers diffuse across the junction, leaving behind immobile ionized dopant atoms   <= key
  C. Photons are absorbed at the junction, creating electron-hole pairs
  D. The temperature gradient across the junction drives carriers apart

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Majority carriers diffuse across the junction, leaving behind immobile ionized dopant atoms
When P-type and N-type semiconductors are joined, a concentration gradient drives diffusion of majority carriers across the junction: electrons diffuse from N to P, and holes diffuse from P to N. As these mobile carriers leave their respective regions, they leave behind fixed, immobile ionized dopant atoms: positive donor ions (like P+) on the N-side and negative acceptor ions (like B-) on the P-side. This region of exposed fixed charges, depleted of mobile carriers, is the depletion region.
The fixed charges create a built-in electric field that opposes further diffusion, establishing equilibrium.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. External voltage is applied to the junction: The depletion region forms spontaneously when the PN junction is created, without any external voltage. In fact, external voltage modifies the already-existing depletion region (forward bias narrows it, reverse bias widens it), but it does not create it.
C. Photons are absorbed at the junction, creating electron-hole pairs: Photon absorption creates electron-hole pairs in solar cells and photodiodes, but this is not the mechanism that forms the depletion region. The depletion region exists in the dark, formed purely by carrier diffusion and the resulting charge imbalance.
D. The temperature gradient across the junction drives carriers apart: A temperature gradient is not needed: a junction at uniform temperature still develops a depletion region. The initial carrier concentration difference drives diffusion, and the resulting fixed charges create the built-in electric field.
Key Educational Points:
  - The built-in potential depends on the semiconductor, doping levels and temperature; it is not a universal 0.7 V or 0.3 V value.
  - The depletion region has very few mobile majority carriers, rather than being completely empty. Its width depends on doping and applied bias: forward bias narrows it and reverse bias widens it.
  - Equilibrium is reached when the drift current (due to the electric field) exactly balances the diffusion current (due to the concentration gradient).
Bottom Line: The depletion region forms spontaneously by diffusion of majority carriers across the PN junction, exposing fixed ionized dopant atoms. This creates a built-in electric field that establishes equilibrium. No external voltage is needed.

Q4  [phys-243]  Fluid Dynamics - Bernoulli Equation  correct answer: B   (SUBSTANTIAL: Bernoulli terms and conditions; keyed option B renamed)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: For steady flow of an incompressible, nonviscous fluid in a horizontal pipe, with no pump or turbine adding or removing energy, which sum is constant along a streamline?

  A. Pressure energy, kinetic energy, and thermal energy
  B. Static pressure, kinetic energy per unit volume (½ρv^2), and gravitational potential energy per unit volume (ρgh)   <= key
  C. Pressure, velocity, and viscous shear stress
  D. Gravitational potential energy, surface tension energy, and kinetic energy

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Static pressure, kinetic energy per unit volume (½ρv^2), and gravitational potential energy per unit volume (ρgh)
Bernoulli's equation is P + ½ρv^2 + ρgh = constant along a streamline under the stated conditions. Each term is energy per unit volume and has units of pressure: P is the pressure term, ½ρv^2 is the kinetic term (often called dynamic pressure), and ρgh is the gravitational potential-energy term. In a horizontal pipe, h is constant, so P + ½ρv^2 is constant. Where the speed is greater, the static pressure is lower in this ideal model.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Pressure energy, kinetic energy, and thermal energy: The standard Bernoulli equation here describes mechanical energy per unit volume, including gravitational potential energy. It does not include a separate thermal-energy term; conversion of mechanical energy into heat through friction is excluded by the ideal model.
C. Pressure, velocity, and viscous shear stress: Pressure, velocity and shear stress cannot simply be added: velocity has different units from pressure and stress. The kinetic term is ½ρv^2. For real viscous flow, an extended energy equation can include losses.
D. Gravitational potential energy, surface tension energy, and kinetic energy: The equation includes pressure, kinetic and gravitational terms. Surface tension can affect pressure differences across curved interfaces, but it is not a separate term in this standard pipe-flow equation.
Key Educational Points:
  - For steady incompressible flow, continuity gives Av = constant. A narrower section therefore has higher speed; Bernoulli then predicts lower static pressure at the same height in the ideal model.
  - Use Bernoulli along a streamline for steady, incompressible, nonviscous flow with no added or removed mechanical energy.
  - Venturi devices and Pitot tubes use pressure–speed relationships. Real blood flow and other viscous flows can also involve energy losses, so the ideal equation has limits.
Bottom Line: Bernoulli: P + ½ρv^2 + ρgh = constant along a streamline under the stated ideal conditions. At equal height, increased speed corresponds to decreased static pressure.

Q5  [phys-244]  Particle Accelerators - Cyclotron  correct answer: B   (SUBSTANTIAL: neutrons have magnetic moments; keyed option B renamed)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Why can a conventional cyclotron not directly accelerate neutrons using its electric-field gaps and magnetic bending field?

  A. Neutrons are too heavy to reach relativistic speeds
  B. Neutrons have no net electric charge, so the accelerating electric field does not exert the qE force on them   <= key
  C. Neutrons decay too quickly to complete a full revolution
  D. The magnetic field of a cyclotron repels neutral particles

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Neutrons have no net electric charge, so the accelerating electric field does not exert the qE force on them
A conventional cyclotron accelerates charged particles with an electric field across the gap between its electrodes and bends their paths with a magnetic field. These charge-dependent forces are F_E = qE and F_B = qv × B. A neutron has q = 0, so it cannot be accelerated and bent by this mechanism. Neutrons do have magnetic moments and can interact with nonuniform magnetic fields; that is a different mechanism.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Neutrons are too heavy to reach relativistic speeds: The neutron mass (1.675 x 10^-27 kg) is very similar to the proton mass (1.673 x 10^-27 kg), and protons are routinely accelerated in cyclotrons. Mass is not the limiting factor; the absence of charge is.
C. Neutrons decay too quickly to complete a full revolution: Free neutrons have a half-life of about 10 minutes, far longer than the timescale of motion in a cyclotron. Their decay is not the obstacle; the conventional cyclotron mechanism requires electric charge.
D. The magnetic field of a cyclotron repels neutral particles: Neutral particles are not automatically repelled by a magnetic field. A neutron has a magnetic moment and can be manipulated in a suitable nonuniform magnetic field, but it does not undergo the charge-dependent cyclotron motion.
Key Educational Points:
  - Cyclotrons can accelerate protons, deuterons, α particles, and other charged ions.
  - For nonrelativistic motion, the cyclotron frequency is f = |q|B/(2πm). At fixed B and particle mass, it is independent of speed and orbit radius.
  - Accelerators can produce neutron beams by sending accelerated charged particles into a target, where nuclear reactions release neutrons.
Bottom Line: A conventional cyclotron directly accelerates charged particles. Neutrons have no net electric charge, so this acceleration mechanism does not work for them.

Q6  [phys-245]  Electromagnetism - Lorentz Force  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A positive charge moves to the right in a magnetic field directed into the page. The magnetic force on the charge is directed:

  A. Into the page
  B. Out of the page
  C. Upward (toward the top of the page)   <= key
  D. Downward (toward the bottom of the page)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Upward (toward the top of the page)
The magnetic Lorentz force on a moving charge is given by F = qv x B. Using the right-hand rule for a positive charge:
  - Point your fingers in the direction of velocity v (to the right)
  - Curl your fingers in the direction of B (into the page)
  - Your thumb points in the direction of F (upward)
Alternatively: v (right) x B (into page) → using cross product rules, right x (into page) = up.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Into the page: The force must be perpendicular to both v and B. Since v is to the right (in the plane of the page) and B is into the page, the force must be in the plane of the page (either up or down), not into or out of the page.
B. Out of the page: Out of the page is opposite to the magnetic-field direction, so it is still along the field axis. The magnetic force is perpendicular to both the velocity and the field; here it points upward in the plane of the page.
D. Downward (toward the bottom of the page): This would be the direction if the charge were negative. For a negative charge, the force is opposite to v x B. This is a critical distinction: the sign of the charge reverses the direction of the magnetic force. Electrons moving to the right in this field would be deflected downward.
Key Educational Points:
  - The magnetic part of the Lorentz force is perpendicular to velocity. It can change the direction of motion but does no work and does not change the particle's kinetic energy.
  - For a negative charge, the force direction is reversed: F = qv x B with q < 0 gives the opposite direction to the right-hand rule result.
  - This principle underlies many technologies: mass spectrometers (separating ions by mass), cyclotrons, cathode ray tubes, and the Hall effect.
Bottom Line: Use the right-hand rule for positive charges: fingers point along v, curl toward B, thumb gives F direction. For negative charges, reverse the result. The magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity and does no work.

Q7  [phys-246]  Thermal Radiation - Black Body  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A perfect black body is an object that:

  A. Absorbs all incident radiation but emits none
  B. Absorbs all incident radiation and is also the most efficient emitter of thermal radiation   <= key
  C. Reflects all incident radiation perfectly
  D. Emits radiation only at a single specific wavelength

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Absorbs all incident radiation and is also the most efficient emitter of thermal radiation
A perfect black body has two equally important properties: it absorbs all electromagnetic radiation that falls on it (no reflection, no transmission) AND it is the most efficient possible emitter of thermal radiation at every wavelength for its given temperature. These properties are linked by Kirchhoff's law of thermal radiation, which states that at thermal equilibrium, the emissivity of a body equals its absorptivity.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Absorbs all incident radiation but emits none: A black body at a temperature above absolute zero emits thermal radiation as well as absorbing it. Its emitted power is P = σAT^4, so a perfect absorber is not an object that never emits.
C. Reflects all incident radiation perfectly: This describes a perfect mirror (or perfect reflector), which is the exact opposite of a black body. A perfect reflector has zero absorptivity and zero emissivity, while a black body has maximum absorptivity and maximum emissivity.
D. Emits radiation only at a single specific wavelength: A black body emits a continuous thermal spectrum with a temperature-dependent peak. A narrow spectral line or laser emission is different from that broad black-body spectrum.
Key Educational Points:
  - The black body spectrum (Planck curve) resolved the "ultraviolet catastrophe" of classical physics and was one of the foundations of quantum mechanics.
  - Total radiated power: P = σ A T^4 (Stefan-Boltzmann law), where σ = 5.67 x 10^-8 W/(m^2 K^4).
  - Radiation escaping through a small opening in a heated cavity is a good laboratory approximation to black-body radiation. Stellar spectra are approximate examples; the cosmic microwave background is a close example.
Bottom Line: A black body absorbs all incident radiation and has the maximum thermal emission per unit area at a given temperature in this model. Good absorbers are good emitters, as expressed by Kirchhoff's law.

Q8  [phys-247]  Thermal Radiation - Wien's Law  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: For a black body's emission spectrum plotted per unit wavelength, how does the peak wavelength change as its absolute temperature increases?

  A. Increases (shifts toward longer wavelengths / red shift)
  B. Remains constant regardless of temperature
  C. Decreases (shifts toward shorter wavelengths / blue shift)   <= key
  D. First increases then decreases as temperature rises

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Decreases (shifts toward shorter wavelengths / blue shift)
Wien's displacement law states that the peak emission wavelength of a black body is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature:
λ_max = b / T
where b = 2.898 x 10^-3 m K (Wien's displacement constant) and T is the absolute temperature in kelvin. As temperature increases, λ_max decreases (shifts to shorter wavelengths).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Increases (shifts toward longer wavelengths / red shift): Wien's law gives λ_max = b/T, so higher absolute temperature gives a shorter peak wavelength. A red-hot bar has a shorter peak wavelength than a warm radiator, although both peaks can still lie in the infrared.
B. Remains constant regardless of temperature: The peak wavelength is strongly temperature-dependent. This would violate Wien's law and our everyday observation that heated objects change color (dark red → orange → yellow → white → blue-white) as they get hotter.
D. First increases then decreases as temperature rises: The relationship is strictly monotonic (always decreasing with increasing temperature). There is no turning point or non-monotonic behavior in Wien's law.
Key Educational Points:
  - Approximate wavelength-spectrum peaks: 5800 K gives 500 nm, in the green region of visible light; 310 K gives 9.35 μm, in the infrared; 30000 K gives 97 nm, in the ultraviolet. The Sun's broad spectrum does not make it look green.
  - This law explains why hotter stars appear blue-white and cooler stars appear red-orange.
  - Infrared thermometers estimate temperature from detected infrared radiation in their measurement band, with calibration and emissivity assumptions. They do not generally locate the emission peak directly.
Bottom Line: Wien's law: λ_max = b/T. Hotter objects emit at shorter (bluer) wavelengths; cooler objects emit at longer (redder) wavelengths. This is why heating metal makes it glow from red to orange to yellow to white.

Q9  [phys-248]  Nuclear Physics - Radioactive Decay  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: For a sample containing a single radioactive parent isotope with constant decay constant λ and no production of new parent nuclei, the expected activity of that isotope follows which relationship?

  A. Linear decrease: A(t) = A0 - kt
  B. Quadratic decrease: A(t) = A0 - kt^2
  C. Exponential decrease: A(t) = A0 e^(-λ t)   <= key
  D. Logarithmic decrease: A(t) = A0 ln(t)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Exponential decrease: A(t) = A0 e^(-λ t)
Radioactive decay is a first-order process where the rate of decay is proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei present. This gives rise to an exponential decay function:
A(t) = A0 e^(-λ t)
where A0 is the initial activity, λ is the decay constant, and t is time. Equivalently: N(t) = N0 e^(-λ t) for the number of remaining nuclei.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Linear decrease: A(t) = A0 - kt: A linear law removes a fixed amount of activity per unit time. The decay law instead removes a fixed fraction over equal time intervals, giving an exponential curve. A straight line can approximate a very short part of that curve, but is not the general law.
B. Quadratic decrease: A(t) = A0 - kt^2: A quadratic decrease would initially be slow, then accelerate -- the opposite of what actually occurs. In radioactive decay, the rate of decrease is fastest at the beginning (when there are the most undecayed nuclei) and progressively slows.
D. Logarithmic decrease: A(t) = A0 ln(t): As written, ln(t) is undefined at t = 0 and increases with t, so this expression is not the stated decay law. A logarithm should also have a dimensionless argument. It is ln[A(t)/A_0], rather than A(t), that varies linearly with time.
Key Educational Points:
  - The half-life (t1/2) is related to the decay constant: t1/2 = ln(2)/lambda ≈ 0.693/lambda.
  - After n half-lives, the remaining activity is: A = A0 x (1/2)^n.
  - The exponential curve describes expected activity for a population of radioactive nuclei. Individual decays are random, and a finite sample can eventually have no parent nuclei left.
  - Common medical isotopes: Tc-99m (t1/2 = 6 hours), I-131 (t1/2 = 8 days), Co-60 (t1/2 = 5.27 years).
Bottom Line: Radioactive decay is exponential, not linear. A(t) = A0 e^(-λ t). Each half-life halves the remaining activity. The exponential nature is a direct consequence of the decay rate being proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei present.

STEM: A 60 kg adult sits 1.5 m from the fulcrum of a horizontal seesaw. The seesaw's own weight produces no net torque about the fulcrum. How far from the fulcrum should a 30 kg child sit on the opposite side to balance it? Use g = 10 m/s^2.

  A. 0.75 m
  B. 1.5 m
  C. 3.0 m   <= key
  D. 4.5 m

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 3.0 m
For a balanced seesaw, the torques on each side must be equal:
F_1 × d_1 = F_2 × d_2
m_1g × d_1 = m_2g × d_2
60 × 1.5 = 30 × d_2
90 = 30 × d_2
d_2 = 3.0 m
Since g appears on both sides, it cancels out. The child (half the mass) must sit twice as far from the fulcrum. This is the principle of the lever: a smaller force can balance a larger force by acting at a greater distance.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0.75 m: At 0.75 m, the child provides only 30 × 10 × 0.75 = 225 N·m, compared with the adult's 900 N·m. The lighter person needs a longer lever arm.
B. 1.5 m: Equal distances would balance equal masses. At 1.5 m, the child produces only half the adult's torque.
D. 4.5 m: At 4.5 m, the child produces 1350 N·m, greater than the adult's 900 N·m. The required distance is 900/(30 × 10) = 3.0 m.
Key Concept: Balanced seesaw: m_1 × d_1 = m_2 × d_2 (g cancels). Half the weight needs double the distance. This is Archimedes' lever principle.

  B. 483 s   <= key
  C. 965 s
  D. 4825 s

Correct Answer: 483 s

m = (M × I × t) / (z × F)
t = (m × z × F) / (M × I)
t = (5.4 × 1 × 96500) / (108 × 10)
t = 521100 / 1080
t ≈ 483 s (about 8 minutes)

Q28  [phys-267]  Faraday's Law of Electrolysis  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Two electrolytic cells containing CuSO_4 and AgNO_3 solutions are connected in series, and both deposit their metals with 100% current efficiency. If 2.16 g of silver is deposited, how much copper is deposited? Use molar masses M_Ag = 108 g/mol and M_Cu = 64 g/mol; Ag^+ requires one electron and Cu^2+ requires two.

  A. 0.32 g
  B. 0.64 g   <= key
  C. 1.28 g
  D. 2.16 g

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 0.64 g
By Faraday's second law, when the same charge passes through different electrolytes in series:
m_Cu/m_Ag = (M_Cu/z_Cu) / (M_Ag/z_Ag)
m_Cu/2.16 = (64/2) / (108/1)
m_Cu/2.16 = 32/108
m_Cu = 2.16 × 32/108
m_Cu = 2.16 × 0.2963
m_Cu = 0.64 g
For the same charge and 100% current efficiency, deposited mass is proportional to M/z. Copper has M/z = 32 g per mole of electrons, while silver has 108 g per mole of electrons, so less copper is deposited. The mass deposited per coulomb, called the electrochemical equivalent, is M/(zF).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0.32 g: 0.32 g is half the required copper mass. The factor of two for Cu^2+ is already included in M_Cu/z_Cu = 64/2; do not divide by two again.
C. 1.28 g: 1.28 g results from comparing molar masses while ignoring that each Cu^2+ needs two electrons. Including z_Cu = 2 halves that result to 0.64 g.
D. 2.16 g: Equal charge does not mean equal deposited masses. Silver and copper have different masses deposited per mole of electrons.
Key Concept: Faraday's law gives m = MQ/(zF) at 100% current efficiency. For the same charge, the deposited-mass ratio is (M_1/z_1)/(M_2/z_2). Compare both molar mass and electron requirement.

STEM: An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 400 V. What is its de Broglie wavelength? (Use the shortcut: λ(nm) = 1.226/√V for electrons)

  A. 0.0307 nm
  B. 0.0613 nm   <= key
  C. 0.1226 nm
  D. 0.245 nm

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 0.0613 nm
Using the electron wavelength shortcut:
λ(nm) = 1.226 / √V
λ = 1.226 / √400
λ = 1.226 / 20
λ = 0.0613 nm
The wavelength is about 0.6 angstroms, shorter than typical interatomic spacings. Such short wavelengths make electron diffraction and high-resolution imaging possible, although an electron microscope's resolution also depends on its lenses and other design limits.
Full derivation check: KE = eV = 1.602 × 10^−19 × 400 = 6.408 × 10^−17 J. v = √(2KE/m) = √(2 × 6.408 × 10^−17/9.109 × 10^−31) = 1.186 × 10^7 m/s. λ = h/(mv) = 6.626 × 10^−34/(9.109 × 10^−31 × 1.186 × 10^7) = 6.13 × 10^−11 m = 0.0613 nm. Matches.

A. 0.0307 nm: 0.0307 nm is about half the correct wavelength. Since wavelength varies as 1/√V, halving it would require four times the voltage, about 1600 V.
C. 0.1226 nm: 0.1226 nm would correspond to 100 V using the supplied shortcut. At 400 V, the square root in the denominator is 20, not 10.
D. 0.245 nm: 0.245 nm would correspond to approximately 25 V. For the stated 400 V, λ = 1.226/20 = 0.0613 nm.
Key Concept: For electrons accelerated from rest at nonrelativistic speeds, λ(nm) ≈ 1.226/√V when V is in volts. Larger accelerating voltage gives shorter wavelength. At high voltages, such as 100–300 kV, use a relativistic correction; wavelength alone does not determine microscope resolution.

STEM: An ideal 10 μF capacitor is connected directly to a 50 Hz sinusoidal AC source with peak voltage V_0 = 100 V. What are its capacitive reactance and peak current?

  A. X_C = 318 Ω, I_0 = 0.314 A   <= key
  B. X_C = 159 Ω, I_0 = 0.628 A
  C. X_C = 318 Ω, I_0 = 0.628 A
  D. X_C = 159 Ω, I_0 = 0.314 A

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: X_C = 318 Ω, I_0 = 0.314 A
The capacitive reactance (opposition to AC current by a capacitor) is:
X_C = 1 / (2πfC)
X_C = 1 / (2 × 3.14 × 50 × 10 × 10^−6)
X_C = 1 / (2 × 3.14 × 50 × 10^−5)
X_C = 1 / (3.14 × 10^−3)
X_C = 318 Ω
The peak current:
I_0 = V_0 / X_C = 100 / 318 = 0.314 A
In a purely capacitive AC circuit, the current LEADS the voltage by 90° (a quarter cycle). Higher frequency or larger capacitance means lower reactance and more current.
Why the other choices are wrong:

Q32  [phys-271]  Thermodynamics  correct answer: C

STEM: At 25 degrees C, the saturation vapor pressure of water is 3.17 kPa. If the actual water vapor pressure in the air is 1.90 kPa, what is the relative humidity?

  A. 40%
  B. 50%
  C. 60%   <= key
  D. 80%

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 60%
Relative humidity (RH) is the ratio of the actual vapor pressure to the saturation vapor pressure at the same temperature:
RH = (p_vapor / p_saturated) x 100%
RH = (1.90 / 3.17) x 100% = 59.9%, rounding to 60%.
At 100% RH, the water-vapor pressure equals the saturation vapor pressure at that temperature. Further cooling can cause condensation under ordinary conditions.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 40%: The given vapor pressure is about 60% of 3.17 kPa, not 40%. Subtracting 60% from 100% gives the percentage shortfall from saturation, not relative humidity.

D. 80%: 80% would require a vapor pressure of 0.80 × 3.17 ≈ 2.54 kPa. The stated 1.90 kPa gives about 60%.
Key Concept: Relative humidity is 100 × actual water-vapor pressure / saturation vapor pressure at the same temperature. Saturation vapor pressure increases with temperature; cooling moist air generally raises its relative humidity until condensation occurs.
