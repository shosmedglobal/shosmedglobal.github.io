How to Apply to Charles University's Third Faculty of Medicine (LF3) for 2027 Entry

11 min read

The short answer

You apply online through the Charles University portal, pay a CZK 2,000 admission fee, sit a Biology and Chemistry entrance exam, attend a short interview, and send your school documents once you pass. Applications for 2027 entry open on 1 November 2026 and the application and the fee must both be in by 30 April 2027. There is no MCAT, no personal statement and no letters of recommendation. The exam decides almost everything.

Goes to is.cuni.cz, the university's own portal. You will need an email address and about twenty minutes.

Where the exam date comes from depends on your route. The 28 May 2027 date above is the faculty's own sitting in Prague. If you apply through the faculty's listed representative for your country, that representative runs a sitting closer to home and tells you the date and their registration deadline. The faculty does not publish those dates. The two routes are explained below.

Step 1: choose your route, because the first step differs

LF3 accepts applicants two ways, and the difference matters on day one.

Direct to the faculty. You apply yourself on the university portal and take the faculty's own sitting, in Prague, on 28 May 2027. You pay the faculty's CZK 2,000 fee and nothing else. You arrange your own travel to Prague for exam day.

Through a listed representative. The faculty publishes a list of representatives by country, and each one runs its own sitting of the same exam, usually in or near the applicant's own country. SHOS Med is the representative listed by LF3 for Canada and the USA. If that is you, your first step is to register for our sitting rather than to book a flight, and we tell you the date and the registration cut-off for your year. The faculty's own note on representatives is worth reading in full: agencies "may have their own additional requirements and/or fees set".

What SHOS charges, and what it is for. Registering for a SHOS-run sitting is $199, paid to SHOS Med. That covers organising and invigilating the exam near you instead of in Prague. It is not the faculty's CZK 2,000 admission fee, which you still owe Charles University.

Separately, we offer to handle that faculty fee for you for $149: we pay the CZK 2,000 to Charles University on your behalf and send you the faculty's confirmation. That one is optional. Paying the CZK 2,000 yourself by card inside the online application costs you nothing extra and takes two minutes, and for most applicants that is the sensible choice. Use the handling service if your card is rejected by the portal or you cannot make an international transfer, which is the situation it exists for.

Step 2: submit the application and save your application ID

Create your application on the Charles University portal from 1 November 2026. The programme you want is General Medicine, taught in English, at the Third Faculty of Medicine.

When you finish, write down your application ID. Everything afterwards is keyed to it: the fee payment reference, the documents you post, and any correspondence with the Study Division. People lose it and then spend a week getting it back.

Inside the application, under Proof of education, choose the option for a foreign secondary education document to be assessed. More on that in the documents section.

Step 3: pay the CZK 2,000 admission fee

CZK 2,000, or the equivalent in Euro. Pay by card inside the online application, or by bank transfer to the faculty's account. No cheques. If you transfer, you pay the bank charges, and the payment comment must carry your surname, your application ID and the words "Admission Fee", or the faculty cannot match it to you.

The fee and the application share one deadline: 30 April 2027. An application with no payment against it by that date is not an application.

Step 4: the entrance exam, and whether you can skip it

The written test is taken electronically, on your own device. Two sections, Biology and Chemistry, 30 questions each, 60 in total. Each question has four options and exactly one correct answer, and each is worth one point, so you are scored out of 60. There is no negative marking. The dean sets the pass mark each year, so it moves.

Pass it and you go to a 10-minute interview, scored on a text circle, an interview circuit and a recommendation, for a published range of 12 to 62 points.

Can you skip the exam?

Some applicants can. The faculty's published remission criteria are specific: A-levels of A, A, B in English, Chemistry and Biology; or IB Higher Level Chemistry, Biology and English totalling at least 19 points; or a comparable exam such as the SAT at the 85th percentile or better; or, for applicants schooled in Germany, a grade average better than 1.30; or an accredited foreign bachelor's degree in natural or health sciences. Most of those carry a requirement that the qualification is recent.

If you qualify, you still submit the online application, plus a signed remission form, to the Study Division by 30 April 2027, with the date of postage deciding. At most 25 applicants are admitted this way each year, so it is competitive even when you meet the criteria. Read the faculty's exam-waiver criteria in full before relying on them, and have a preparation plan in case you are not among the 25.

How to prepare for the 60 questions

The scope is a solid secondary-school Biology and Chemistry curriculum, with some areas weighted more heavily than American or British students expect. Ecology, botany and taxonomy come up more than they do on the MCAT; organic chemistry goes deeper than most national school-leaving exams. Our topic-by-topic breakdown of the LF3 entrance exam goes through all of it.

The faculty publishes its own material, and you should read it first because it is the only source that is definitionally correct: the official Biology and Chemistry syllabus (PDF), and a sample test from 2025 (PDF) with its answer key (PDF). One sample paper is not a preparation plan, but it will tell you quickly how far you are from the standard.

After that, the useful thing is volume with feedback. Try 40 free LF3 biology and chemistry questions, 20 of each, which is what a SHOS account includes the moment you create one. They are written to the official syllabus with worked explanations. Full access, which is $99 for six months, opens the rest of the bank, over 1,000 questions, with topic-level performance tracking so you can see which of the nine or ten biology areas is actually costing you marks.

Questions and timing are different skills, and the second one is where people lose marks they already knew. Ready to test your pacing? Take a full-length SHOS LF3 mock exam: 60 questions in 120 minutes, scored by section, no pausing, matching the structure of the real paper. Four are available, each labelled by the admissions cycle it corresponds to. The 2021/2022 mock is free once you have an account; 2022/2023 through 2024/2025 come with full access, along with the aggregated weak-topic report across every mock you have sat.

If you are still in your final year of school

You can apply before you have the certificate. You cannot enrol without it. The faculty's deadline for the documents is the day you enrol in September, which in practice means you apply in the spring with results pending, sit the exam, and send the certificate as soon as your school issues it. Build in time for the notary, the translation and the Apostille, because those are three separate queues in most countries and none of them is fast in August.

Vaccinations

LF3 requires vaccination against hepatitis B and measles before you start your practical training in the first study year. Not before enrolment, and not in year two. Hepatitis B in particular is a multi-dose schedule spread over months, so if you are reading this in the spring, that is the moment to start it rather than the week before term.

Visa and housing

Non-EU students need a long-stay study visa, and the faculty recommends applying by June for an October start. It is a separate process with its own document list, and we have written it up properly in the Czech student visa guide for US and Canadian medical students. For what Prague actually costs, including rent by district, see the full cost breakdown.

Enrolment, and the dates that end the process

After the interview, the faculty issues a formal decision on admission. That letter is the document your embassy will want for the visa, so keep the original and do not send your only copy anywhere: the visa application needs it, and so does the Foreign Police after you arrive.

Admitted applicants enrol in person, usually at the end of September, either at the faculty building or at the Dobronice camp for incoming students. Tuition for the first year, CZK 440,000, is due by 30 October, by bank transfer or by card at the Study Division. The latest possible enrolment date for the 2027 cycle is 30 October 2027. Teaching begins on 1 October, the start of the Czech academic year.

The honest summary

The administrative side of applying to LF3 is genuinely simple compared with a US medical school application. There is no MCAT, no committee letter, no personal statement, and the whole thing costs CZK 2,000 to enter. What that means is that the exam carries all the weight, and roughly 90 places are decided on 60 questions in a single morning. The applicants who get in are not the ones with the best paperwork. They are the ones who treated a secondary-school syllabus as something to be mastered rather than skimmed.
