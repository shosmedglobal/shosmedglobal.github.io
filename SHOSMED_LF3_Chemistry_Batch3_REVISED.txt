What is the molar mass of Ca(OH)2? (Ca=40, O=16, H=1)

  A) 57 g/mol
  B) 58 g/mol
  C) 74 g/mol
  D) 82 g/mol

### Correct Answer: C. 74 g/mol

Ca(OH)2 contains 1 Ca, 2 O, and 2 H atoms. Molar mass = 40 + 2(16) + 2(1) = 40 + 32 + 2 = 74 g/mol.

Remember to multiply the atoms inside parentheses by the subscript outside before summing.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. 57 g/mol:** Getting 57 might result from calculating Ca + O + H = 40 + 16 + 1 = 57, forgetting the subscript 2 outside the parentheses. Ca(OH)2 means there are 2 OH groups: Ca + 2O + 2H = 40 + 32 + 2 = 74 g/mol.

**B. 58 g/mol:** 58 g/mol results from 40 + 16 + 2(1), which counts only one oxygen. Ca(OH)₂ contains two oxygens and two hydrogens, so the correct total is 40 + 2(16) + 2(1) = 74 g/mol.

**D. 82 g/mol:** Getting 82 might come from an arithmetic error or from adding an extra atom somewhere. The correct calculation: 1 Ca (40) + 2 O (32) + 2 H (2) = 74 g/mol. Check each element's count carefully using the subscripts.

### Key Educational Points:

  - A subscript outside parentheses multiplies every atom inside them.
  - Ca(OH)₂ contains one Ca, two O and two H atoms per formula unit.
  - Its molar mass is 40 + 2(16) + 2(1) = 74 g/mol using the supplied values.

>> Bottom Line: Molar mass is calculated by summing atomic masses of all atoms in the formula.

For the reaction N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g)  2 NH₃(g), what is the expression for Kc?

### Correct Answer: C. [NH₃]^2 / ([N₂][H₂]^3)

The equilibrium constant expression Kc = products over reactants, each raised to the power of their stoichiometric coefficients. Kc = [NH3]^2 / ([N2][H2]^3).

Pure solids and pure liquids are omitted from the usual equilibrium expression because their activities are treated as constant. For this all-gas reaction, use the equilibrium concentrations of all three gases.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. [NH₃] / ([N₂][H₂]):** This omits the exponents required by the balanced equation. The coefficient 2 becomes the exponent on [NH₃], and the coefficient 3 becomes the exponent on [H₂].

**D. 2[NH₃] / ([N₂] + 3[H₂]):** Stoichiometric coefficients in the balanced equation become exponents in the equilibrium expression, not multipliers or addition factors. The correct form uses multiplication of concentrations raised to powers, never addition. This is a fundamental rule for writing any K expression.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Write products over reactants using equilibrium concentrations.
  - Balanced coefficients become exponents: Kc = [NH₃]²/([N₂][H₂]³).
  - Reversing the reaction replaces K by 1/K; it does not leave the expression unchanged.

  A) 5 min
  B) 10 min
  C) 15 min
  D) 20 min

### Correct Answer: B. 10 min

For a first-order reaction, t1/2 = ln(2)/k = 0.693/0.0693 = 10 min.

The half-life of a first-order reaction is constant and independent of the initial concentration, which is a distinctive feature of first-order kinetics.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**C. 15 min:** After 15 min, the fraction remaining is e^(−0.0693 × 15) ≈ 0.354. Half remains at 10 min.

**D. 20 min:** Twenty minutes is two half-lives for this rate constant, so about one-quarter remains. The half-life is ln 2/k = 10 min.

### Key Educational Points:

  - First-order half-life is t½ = ln 2/k.
  - 0.693/0.0693 min⁻¹ = 10 min.
  - At a fixed rate constant, each successive halving takes the same time, independent of the starting concentration.

>> Bottom Line: For first-order reactions, t1/2 = 0.693/k, independent of initial concentration.

The rate law for a reaction is rate = k[A]^2[B]. If the concentration of A is doubled while B remains constant, by what factor does the rate increase?

  A) 2
  B) 4
  C) 3
  D) 8

### Correct Answer: B. 4

Rate = k[A]^2[B]. If [A] is doubled: new rate = k(2[A])^2[B] = k(4[A]^2)[B] = 4 x original rate. The rate increases by a factor of 4.

The reaction is second order in A, so doubling A quadruples the rate (2^2 = 4).

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. 2:** A factor of 2 would be correct if the rate were first-order in A (rate = k[A][B]). Since the rate is second-order in A ([A]²), doubling [A] increases the rate by 2² = 4. The exponent determines how sensitively the rate responds to concentration changes.

**C. 3:** A factor of 3 has no basis in this rate law. When you double a concentration raised to the power n, the rate changes by 2ⁿ. Here n = 2 for A, so 2² = 4. The factor 3 would not arise from any standard integer-order rate law with doubling of concentration.

**D. 8:** A factor of 8 = 2³ would apply if the rate were third-order in A (rate = k[A]³[B]). Here the order with respect to A is 2, so doubling [A] gives 2² = 4. A factor of 8 would also result from doubling both [A] and [B] when the total order is 3.

### Key Educational Points:

  - The exponent of [A] is 2, so the reaction is second order with respect to A.
  - Doubling [A] at unchanged [B] and k multiplies the rate by 2² = 4.
  - If both [A] and [B] doubled, the rate would instead increase by 2² × 2 = 8.

>> Bottom Line: For a reactant with order n, multiplying its concentration by x changes the rate by xn.

For a zero-order reaction, which statement is correct?

  A) The half-life is independent of initial concentration
  B) The rate doubles when concentration doubles
  C) The rate is independent of reactant concentration
  D) A plot of ln[A] vs time is linear

### Correct Answer: C. The rate is independent of reactant concentration

For a zero-order reaction, rate = k[A]^0 = k. The rate is constant regardless of the concentration of reactant A over the range in which the zero-order rate law applies. Enzyme-catalyzed reactions near saturation and some surface reactions approximate this behavior over a concentration range; the rate cannot remain nonzero once the reactant is exhausted.

The half-life of a zero-order reaction is t1/2 = [A]_0 / (2k), which depends on the initial concentration.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. The half-life is independent of initial concentration:** For disappearance following −d[A]/dt = k, t½ = [A]₀/(2k). The half-life therefore depends on starting concentration; concentration-independent half-life is characteristic of a first-order law.

**B. The rate doubles when concentration doubles:** Doubling concentration doubles the rate for a first-order dependence. In the zero-order range, rate = k, so changing [A] does not change the rate.

**D. A plot of ln[A] vs time is linear:** A linear ln[A] vs. time plot is the hallmark of first-order kinetics. For zero-order reactions, a plot of [A] vs. time is linear (with slope = −k). This distinction between plotting [A], ln[A], or 1/[A] vs. time is the standard method for determining reaction order experimentally.

### Key Educational Points:

  - A zero-order law has rate = k over its applicable concentration range.
  - For −d[A]/dt = k, [A] = [A]₀ − kt, so [A] versus time is linear.
  - Its half-life [A]₀/(2k) depends on starting concentration, unlike a first-order half-life.

>> Bottom Line: Within its zero-order range, the rate is independent of [A]; the model must stop or change when that range no longer applies.

  - Here 24 days divided by an 8-day half-life gives 3 half-lives.

  - Decay is exponential rather than linear: each half-life halves the expected amount still present as the original isotope. Individual nuclei decay at random times.
  - For ordinary laboratory conditions, the isotope's half-life is usually treated as constant; ordinary changes in temperature or pressure do not appreciably change most radioactive decay rates.

>> Bottom Line: After n half-lives, the remaining amount = initial amount x (1/2)n.

Nuclear fission involves:

  B) Conversion of protons to neutrons
  C) Emission of γ rays only

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a heavy nucleus (like U-235 or Pu-239) into two or more lighter nuclei, usually accompanied by the release of neutrons and a large amount of energy.

Fission is used in nuclear power plants and nuclear weapons. The released neutrons can cause a chain reaction.

  - Uranium-235 and plutonium-239 are the standard fissile fuels.
  - Energy is released because the lighter products have a higher binding energy per nucleon than the heavy parent nucleus.
  - Fusion is the opposite process - combining light nuclei - and the two are easily confused.

How many unpaired electrons are present in a ground-state nitrogen atom?

  A) 1
  B) 2
  C) 3
  D) 5

### Correct Answer: 3

Nitrogen (Z=7) has the electron configuration 1s² 2s² 2p³. The 1s and 2s orbitals are completely filled, so all electrons there are paired. In the 2p subshell, there are three orbitals (2pₓ, 2py, 2pz) and three electrons.

By **Hund's rule**, electrons occupy each of the three equal-energy 2p orbitals singly before pairing up. Therefore, each of the three 2p orbitals contains one unpaired electron, giving **3 unpaired electrons** total.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. 1:** One unpaired electron would describe an element like hydrogen or sodium. Nitrogen's electron configuration is 1s² 2s² 2p³. According to Hund's rule, the three 2p electrons occupy three separate p orbitals with parallel spins before any pairing occurs. All three 2p electrons are unpaired, giving nitrogen a total of 3 unpaired electrons.

**B. 2:** Two unpaired electrons would apply to elements like carbon (2p²) or oxygen (2p⁴). Nitrogen has exactly three 2p electrons, and by Hund's rule (maximize spin multiplicity), each occupies a separate 2p orbital. This gives three unpaired electrons, not two.

**D. 5:** This might confuse the total number of electrons in the outer shell with unpaired electrons. Nitrogen has 5 valence electrons (2s² 2p³), but the two 2s electrons are paired. Only the three 2p electrons are unpaired. Always check which electrons are paired and which are not.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Ground-state nitrogen has configuration 1s² 2s² 2p³.
  - The three equal-energy 2p orbitals each receive one electron with parallel spins before pairing.
  - Nitrogen has three unpaired electrons, although it has five valence electrons in total.

>> Bottom Line: Hund's rule states that electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly with parallel spins before any pairing occurs.

Which of the following atoms has the largest atomic radius?

  A) Na
  B) Mg
  C) Al
  D) K

### Correct Answer: K

Atomic radius increases going **down a group** (more electron shells) and generally decreases going **across a period**, as the increasing effective nuclear charge draws the outer electrons closer.

Na, Mg, and Al are all in Period 3, with atomic radius decreasing in that order: Na > Mg > Al. Potassium (K) is in Period 4, Group 1, which means it has an additional electron shell compared to sodium. Therefore K has a larger radius than all the Period 3 elements listed.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. Na:** Na and K are in Group 1, but K has its outer electron in the next principal shell. K is therefore larger than Na in this comparison.

**B. Mg:** Mg is in the same period as Na and is smaller because its valence electrons experience a greater effective nuclear attraction. K also has an additional principal shell, making it the largest listed atom.

**C. Al:** Al is farther across Period 3 than Na and Mg and is smaller in this comparison. K lies in Period 4, Group 1, with a larger outer-electron distribution.

### Key Educational Points:

  - Moving down Group 1 adds an occupied principal shell, making K larger than Na.
  - Across Period 3, the listed radii decrease from Na to Mg to Al.
  - The resulting order is K > Na > Mg > Al, so K is largest.

>> Bottom Line: Atomic radius increases down a group due to additional electron shells and decreases across a period due to increasing effective nuclear charge.

What is the maximum number of electrons that can occupy the third principal energy level (n=3)?

  A) 8
  B) 18
  C) 32
  D) 9

### Correct Answer: 18

The maximum number of electrons in a principal energy level is given by the formula **2n²**.

For n=3: 2(3)² = 2 × 9 = **18 electrons**.

These are distributed among the subshells: 3s (2 electrons), 3p (6 electrons), and 3d (10 electrons). Total: 2+6+10 = 18.

Note: Although many elements do not fill the 3d subshell before starting 4s, the third shell can ultimately hold 18 electrons.

### Why the other choices are wrong:

**A. 8:** The "octet" (8 electrons) fills the 3s and 3p subshells but ignores the 3d subshell. The third shell contains 3s (2e), 3p (6e), and 3d (10e), totaling 18 electrons. The formula for maximum electrons in shell n is 2n² = 2(3²) = 18.

**C. 32:** This is the maximum for the fourth shell (n=4), where 2n² = 2(4²) = 32. For n=3, the calculation gives 2(3²) = 2(9) = 18. The subshells in n=3 are: 3s (2), 3p (6), and 3d (10), totaling 18 electrons.

**D. 9:** Nine is the number of orbitals in the n = 3 shell: n² = 9. Each orbital can hold two electrons with opposite spin, so the maximum is 2 × 9 = 18 electrons.

### Key Educational Points:

  - A shell with principal quantum number n has n² orbitals and capacity 2n² electrons.
  - The n = 3 shell contains 3s, 3p and 3d, with capacities 2, 6 and 10.
  - These add to 18 electrons; shell capacity is different from the order in which subshells are filled.
