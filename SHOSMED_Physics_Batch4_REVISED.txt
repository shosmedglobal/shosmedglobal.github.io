Q1  [phys-181]  Acoustics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Sound travels fastest through which of the following media?

  A. Air at room temperature
  B. Water at room temperature
  C. Steel at room temperature   <= key
  D. Vacuum

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Steel at room temperature
Among these choices, longitudinal sound travels fastest in steel. Sound speed depends on both the material's elastic stiffness and its density; density alone does not determine the speed.
Medium | Approximate sound speed | Steel | About 5000–6000 m/s for longitudinal sound, depending on the steel and wave mode | Water at room temperature | About 1480 m/s | Air at 20 °C | About 343 m/s | Vacuum | Not applicable: sound cannot propagate | Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. Air at room temperature: Sound in air (~343 m/s) is much slower than in steel or water.
  - B. Water at room temperature: Water transmits sound faster than air but slower than the longitudinal sound considered here in steel. Compare elastic stiffness relative to density, rather than particle spacing alone.
  - D. Vacuum: Sound is a mechanical wave and requires a medium. It cannot travel through a vacuum at all.
Key Concept: Sound requires a material medium. Its speed depends on elastic stiffness relative to density. For these examples, longitudinal sound is fastest in steel, then water, then air; sound cannot propagate in a vacuum.

Q2  [phys-182]  Acoustics  correct answer: A
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Using the reference intensity I_0 = 10^-12 W/m^2, what is the sound intensity level of a sound whose intensity is also 10^-12 W/m^2?

  A. 0 dB   <= key
  B. 1 dB
  C. 10 dB
  D. -12 dB

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 0 dB
The sound intensity level is L = 10 log_10(I/I_0). Here I/I_0 = 1, so L = 10 log_10(1) = 0 dB. This means the intensity equals the chosen reference, not that there is no sound. Actual hearing sensitivity depends on frequency and the listener.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - B. 1 dB: A level of 1 dB would require I/I_0 = 10^0.1, about 1.26. Here the ratio is exactly 1, whose logarithm is zero.
  - C. 10 dB: A level of 10 dB corresponds to an intensity ten times the reference. Here the intensity equals the reference.
  - D. -12 dB: The −12 is the exponent in the reference intensity, not its decibel level. Divide I by I_0 before taking the logarithm.
Key Concept: Sound intensity level compares I with a reference: L = 10 log_10(I/I_0). An intensity equal to I_0 gives 0 dB. Increasing intensity tenfold adds 10 dB; doubling it adds about 3 dB.

Q3  [phys-183]  Acoustics  correct answer: C   (KEEP: byte-identical in every field, heading normalization included)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: If the intensity of a sound is increased by a factor of 100, by how many decibels does the sound level increase?

  A. 2 dB
  B. 10 dB
  C. 20 dB   <= key
  D. 100 dB

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 20 dB
Using the decibel formula:
ΔL = 10 log_10(I_2 / I_1)
ΔL = 10 log_10(100) = 10 x 2 = 20 dB
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - 2 dB: This is just log_10(100) = 2 without the factor of 10.
  - 10 dB: This corresponds to a 10-fold intensity increase, not 100-fold.
  - 100 dB: The decibel scale is logarithmic, so a 100-fold increase is only 20 dB, not 100 dB.
Key Concept: The decibel scale compresses large intensity ranges into manageable numbers. A factor of 10 in intensity = 10 dB. A factor of 100 = 20 dB. A factor of 1000 = 30 dB. This logarithmic pattern is essential for exam calculations.

Q5  [phys-185]  Doppler Effect  correct answer: B   (distractor arithmetic: a receding source gives 537 Hz, not 548)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A siren emits sound at 600 Hz while moving directly toward an observer at 40 m/s. The observer is stationary relative to still air, where sound travels at 340 m/s. What frequency does the observer hear?

  A. 548 Hz
  B. 680 Hz   <= key
  C. 600 Hz
  D. 706 Hz

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 680 Hz
When a source approaches a stationary observer, use the Doppler formula:
f_obs = f_source x v_sound / (v_sound - v_source)
f_obs = 600 x 340 / (340 - 40)
f_obs = 600 x 340 / 300
f_obs = 204,000 / 300 = 680 Hz
The observed frequency is higher than the emitted frequency because the source is approaching.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. 548 Hz: An approaching source raises the received frequency, so 548 Hz is too low. A source receding at 40 m/s would instead give 600 × 340/(340 + 40) ≈ 537 Hz, not 548 Hz.
  - C. 600 Hz: 600 Hz is the emitted frequency. The approaching source compresses the wavefront spacing ahead of it, so the stationary observer receives a higher frequency.
  - D. 706 Hz: Substitute the stated speeds into 600 × 340/(340 − 40). The denominator is 300 m/s and the result is 680 Hz, not 706 Hz.
Key Concept: For a source moving directly toward a stationary observer through still air, f' = fv/(v − v_s). For direct recession, use v + v_s in the denominator. The observer and sound speed are defined relative to the air.

Q6  [phys-186]  Doppler Effect  correct answer: A   (distractor arithmetic: approach gives 425 Hz, not 424)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A source emits sound at 400 Hz while moving directly away from an observer at 20 m/s. The observer is stationary relative to still air, where sound travels at 340 m/s. Approximately what frequency does the observer hear?

  A. 378 Hz   <= key
  B. 400 Hz
  C. 424 Hz
  D. 422 Hz

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 378 Hz
For a source receding directly from a stationary observer, f' = fv/(v + v_s). Thus f' = 400 × 340/(340 + 20) = 136000/360 ≈ 377.8 Hz, which rounds to 378 Hz. The receding source increases the wavefront spacing reaching the observer.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - B. 400 Hz: 400 Hz is the emitted frequency. Direct recession makes the received frequency lower.
  - C. 424 Hz: 424 Hz is higher than the emitted frequency, so it has the wrong direction of change. If the source were approaching at 20 m/s, the calculation would give 400 × 340/(340 − 20) = 425 Hz, not 424 Hz.
  - D. 422 Hz: 422 Hz is also an increase, whereas this source is receding. Use the larger denominator 340 + 20 = 360 m/s, giving about 378 Hz.
Key Concept: A source receding directly from an observer stationary in still air gives f' = fv/(v + v_s), so the observed frequency is lower than the emitted frequency.

Q7  [phys-187]  Acoustics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: What is the relationship between the frequency (f) and the period (T) of a sound wave?

  A. f = T
  B. f = 1/T   <= key
  C. f = 2πT
  D. f = T^2

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: f = 1/T
Frequency and period are reciprocals of each other:
f = 1/T    and    T = 1/f
If a wave has a period of 0.002 seconds (the time for one complete cycle), then its frequency is 1/0.002 = 500 Hz (500 cycles per second).
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. f = T: This would mean a wave with period 0.5 seconds has frequency 0.5 Hz, but it actually has frequency 2 Hz.
  - C. f = 2πT: The factor 2π appears in angular frequency: ω = 2πf = 2π/T. It does not give f = 2πT; ordinary frequency is the reciprocal of period.
  - D. f = T^2: There is no squared relationship between frequency and period.
Key Concept: f = 1/T is one of the most fundamental relationships in wave physics. The period T is measured in seconds, frequency f in hertz (Hz = 1/s). Combined with v = fλ, you can relate wave speed, wavelength, frequency, and period.

Correct Answer: 0.5 m
Using the wave equation:
v = f x λ

Q9  [phys-189]  Microscope Optics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In the standard two-converging-lens model of a compound microscope, the specimen is just beyond the objective focal point. The objective lens creates:

  A. A virtual, upright, magnified image
  B. A real, inverted, magnified image   <= key
  C. A virtual, inverted, reduced image
  D. A real, upright, reduced image

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: A real, inverted, magnified image
In this simplified compound-microscope model, two converging lenses work in series:
Lens | Image Type | Purpose | Objective | Real, inverted, magnified | Creates a magnified intermediate image inside the tube | Eyepiece | Virtual, upright (relative to intermediate), magnified | Further magnifies the intermediate image for the eye | The specimen is placed just outside the focal point of the objective, so it produces a real, inverted, and enlarged image at the other end of the tube.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. A virtual, upright, magnified image: This describes the image from a simple magnifying glass, not from the objective of a compound microscope.
  - C. A virtual, inverted, reduced image: For the real specimen in this setup, the objective forms a real, inverted, enlarged image. A virtual image produced by a single converging lens viewing a real object inside its focal length would be upright.
  - D. A real, upright, reduced image: With this real specimen just beyond its focal point, the objective forms an inverted, enlarged image. It does not form an upright, reduced real image.
Key Concept: The objective creates a real image (which can be projected) that the eyepiece then magnifies. Total magnification = M_objective x M_eyepiece. The final image seen by the eye is virtual, inverted relative to the original specimen, and highly magnified.

Q10  [phys-190]  Microscope Optics  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: For a conventional light microscope with a fixed numerical aperture, which quantity sets the diffraction-limited separation of two points that can be resolved?

  A. The magnification of the eyepiece
  B. The thickness of the glass lenses
  C. The wavelength of light used   <= key
  D. The brightness of the light source

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The wavelength of light used
A common estimate for the diffraction-limited separation is d_min ≈ 0.61λ/NA. Here λ is the wavelength in vacuum, and NA = n sinα, where n is the refractive index of the imaging medium and α is the collection half-angle. At fixed NA, shorter wavelengths allow smaller separations to be resolved. With visible light and a high-NA objective, this separation is roughly a few tenths of a micrometer; it is not a universal limit for every microscopy technique.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. The magnification of the eyepiece: Higher magnification beyond the resolution limit only produces larger but blurrier images ('empty magnification'). It does not reveal finer detail.
  - B. The thickness of the glass lenses: While lens quality matters, the fundamental limit is diffraction, which depends on wavelength.
  - D. The brightness of the light source: Adequate illumination helps make details visible. However, increasing brightness alone does not change the diffraction limit at fixed wavelength and numerical aperture.
Key Concept: Resolution distinguishes nearby details; magnification only enlarges the image. In the conventional diffraction-limited model, shorter wavelength or higher numerical aperture reduces the minimum resolvable separation.

Q11  [phys-191]  Microscope Optics  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Why is immersion oil placed between the specimen cover glass and an objective lens designed for oil immersion?

  A. To reduce the brightness of the light
  B. To increase the numerical aperture and improve resolution   <= key
  C. To magnify the specimen before it reaches the objective
  D. To protect the objective lens from scratches

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: To increase the numerical aperture and improve resolution
Immersion oil has a refractive index close to that of glass, about 1.5. Replacing the air gap with the appropriate oil allows more high-angle light to reach an oil-immersion objective. This permits a higher numerical aperture, NA = n sinα, and a smaller diffraction-limited separation, d_min ≈ 0.61λ/NA. The improvement depends on the objective and its design; simply adding oil to any objective does not guarantee a fixed 1.5-fold gain.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. To reduce the brightness of the light: The intended effect is to collect useful high-angle light, not to dim the image. Oil reduces the loss of these rays at the glass-to-air boundary.
  - C. To magnify the specimen before it reaches the objective: Oil is not a separate magnifying lens. Its role here is to improve light collection and numerical aperture with the appropriate objective.
  - D. To protect the objective lens from scratches: The oil bridges the optical gap; the objective glass should not need to touch the cover glass. Its purpose is improved imaging, not scratch protection.
Key Concept: Use the specified immersion medium with an objective designed for it. Oil reduces the refractive-index mismatch with glass, allowing higher numerical aperture and better resolution.

Q12  [phys-192]  Microscope Optics  correct answer: C   (relaxed-eye adjustment and the optical interval now justify 400x)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In an ideal two-lens compound microscope adjusted for relaxed-eye viewing (final image at infinity), the objective focal length is 4 mm and the eyepiece focal length is 25 mm. The optical interval, measured from the objective’s rear focal point to the eyepiece’s front focal point, is 160 mm. Using the conventional reference distance D = 250 mm, what is the magnitude of the total magnification?

  A. 100x
  B. 200x
  C. 400x   <= key
  D. 40x

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 400x
For this optical interval L and relaxed-eye adjustment, the objective magnification has magnitude L/f_o = 160/4 = 40. The eyepiece angular magnification is D/f_e = 250/25 = 10. Their product is M = 40 × 10 = 400. The objective inverts the intermediate image; the question asks for the positive magnitude of magnification.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - A. 100x: 100× is one-quarter of the required product. The objective gives 40× and the eyepiece gives 10×, so multiply them to obtain 400×.
  - B. 200x: 200× is half the required magnification. Both focal-length ratios must be included: (160/4)(250/25) = 400.
  - D. 40x: 40× is only the objective magnification. The eyepiece provides another factor of 10.
Key Concept: For this ideal microscope adjusted to a final image at infinity, the magnification magnitude is M = (L/f_o)(D/f_e). L is the optical interval between the specified focal points, and D = 25 cm is the conventional reference distance.

Q13  [phys-193]  Microscope Optics  correct answer: A   (COMPETING-ANSWER REPAIR: option D replaced; collimation is a real eyepiece function)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In a compound microscope, the eyepiece functions as a:

  A. Simple magnifying glass viewing the intermediate image   <= key
  B. Projector that casts the image on a screen
  C. Filter that selects specific wavelengths of light
  D. Lens that forms the first real image directly from the specimen

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Simple magnifying glass viewing the intermediate image
The eyepiece acts as a magnifying glass for the intermediate image formed by the objective. If that image is just inside the eyepiece focal plane, the eyepiece forms a magnified virtual image at a finite distance. If it lies in the focal plane, rays from each image point emerge parallel, allowing relaxed-eye viewing with the final image at infinity. Both arrangements use the eyepiece as a magnifier.
Why the other answers are wrong:
  - B. Projector that casts the image on a screen: A projector casts real images. The eyepiece creates a virtual image for the eye to view directly.
  - C. Filter that selects specific wavelengths of light: The eyepiece does not filter light. Separate filters can be added to a microscope for this purpose, but that is not the eyepiece's function.
  - D. Lens that forms the first real image directly from the specimen: Forming the first real image directly from the specimen is the objective's role. The eyepiece magnifies the intermediate image that has already been formed.
Key Concept: The microscope's optical chain is: specimen → objective (creates real magnified image) → eyepiece (magnifies that image again as a virtual image for the eye). The eyepiece is essentially a magnifying glass for the intermediate image.

Q14  [phys-194]  Semiconductor Diodes  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In an ordinary PN junction diode under appreciable forward bias, the depletion region:

  A. Widens, and no current flows
  B. Narrows, and current flows easily   <= key
  C. Disappears completely, and the diode behaves like a superconductor
  D. Stays the same width, and a small leakage current flows

  A. XL = 1/(wL)
  B. XL = wL   <= key
  C. XL = w^2 L
  D. XL = L/w

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: XL = wL
Inductive reactance is the opposition to current flow offered by an inductor in an AC circuit. It is directly proportional to both the angular frequency (w = 2 π f) and the inductance (L):
XL = wL = 2 π f L
The unit of inductive reactance is the ohm (Ω), the same as resistance. As frequency increases, the inductor opposes current more strongly because the changing current produces a larger back-EMF.

D. XL = L/w: This inverts the frequency dependence. It would imply that higher frequencies lead to lower reactance, which is the opposite of how inductors behave. Inductors resist rapid changes in current, so higher frequencies mean greater opposition.
Key Educational Points:
  - At DC (w = 0), XL = 0, meaning an ideal inductor behaves like a short circuit (zero resistance to steady current).
  - In the ideal-inductor model, increasing frequency increases reactance. At the same applied voltage amplitude, the AC current amplitude is therefore smaller.
  - An inductor stores energy in its magnetic field: E = (1/2)LI^2.
Bottom Line: For an ideal inductor in sinusoidal steady state, X_L = wL = 2πfL. Increasing frequency or inductance increases its reactance.

Q48  [phys-228]  AC Circuits - Series RLC Impedance  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: For a series circuit containing ideal R, L and C in sinusoidal steady state, with R > 0, which expression gives the impedance magnitude Z in terms of R, X_L and X_C?

  A. Z = R + XL + XC
  B. Z = sqrt(R^2 + (XL - XC)^2)   <= key
  C. Z = sqrt(R^2 + (XL + XC)^2)
  D. Z = R + (XL - XC)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Z = sqrt(R^2 + (XL - XC)^2)
In a series RLC circuit, the impedance combines resistance and net reactance using vector (phasor) addition. The resistance R is along the real axis, while the net reactance (XL - XC) is along the imaginary axis. The magnitude of impedance is:
Z = sqrt(R^2 + (XL - XC)^2)
This is because the voltage across the resistor is in phase with the current, while the voltages across L and C are 90 degrees out of phase (and opposite to each other).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Z = R + XL + XC: Simple arithmetic addition is incorrect because R, XL, and XC are not in phase with each other. The voltage across the inductor leads the current by 90 degrees, the voltage across the capacitor lags by 90 degrees, and the voltage across the resistor is in phase. They must be combined as phasors, not scalars.
C. Z = sqrt(R^2 + (XL + XC)^2): The reactances of L and C should be subtracted, not added. Since the inductor voltage leads by +90 degrees and the capacitor voltage lags by -90 degrees, they are 180 degrees apart and partially cancel each other. The net reactance is their difference.
D. Z = R + (XL - XC): While (XL - XC) correctly represents the net reactance, you cannot simply add it arithmetically to R because they are perpendicular in the impedance phasor diagram. The Pythagorean theorem (vector addition) must be used.
Key Educational Points:
  - The phase angle between voltage and current: tan(φ) = (XL - XC)/R. If XL > XC, the circuit is inductive (voltage leads current). If XC > XL, the circuit is capacitive (current leads voltage).
  - At series resonance, X_L = X_C and the impedance magnitude is R. For fixed source voltage and R > 0, current is then greatest.
  - Complex impedance is R + j(X_L − X_C), where j^2 = −1. The square-root expression in this question is its magnitude, not the complex impedance itself.
Bottom Line: Z = sqrt(R^2 + (XL - XC)^2). The reactances subtract (they oppose each other), and the net reactance combines with resistance by the Pythagorean theorem because they are 90 degrees out of phase.

w0 L = 1/(w0 C)
Solving: w0 = 1/sqrt(LC)

  - Resonance is exploited in radio tuning circuits to select specific frequencies.
Bottom Line: Series resonance occurs at X_L = X_C, or f_0 = 1/(2π√LC). The input is then purely resistive. With fixed source voltage and resistance, current is greatest and voltage and current are in phase.

Q50  [phys-230]  AC Circuits - Inductor Phase  correct answer: B   (phase causality repaired via v = L di/dt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In sinusoidal steady state for an ideal inductor, what is the phase relationship between its voltage and current?

  A. Voltage and current are in phase
  B. Voltage leads current by 90 degrees   <= key
  C. Current leads voltage by 90 degrees
  D. Voltage leads current by 180 degrees

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Voltage leads current by 90 degrees
The voltage across an ideal inductor is v = L(di/dt). For a sinusoidal current, its rate of change is one-quarter cycle ahead of the current itself. Thus voltage leads current by 90°. The positive voltage peak occurs as current crosses zero while increasing; current reaches its positive peak one-quarter cycle later, when voltage is zero.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Voltage and current are in phase: This describes a purely resistive circuit. In a resistor, V = IR at every instant, so voltage and current rise and fall together with zero phase difference.
C. Current leads voltage by 90 degrees: This describes a purely capacitive circuit, not an inductive one. In a capacitor, current flows in response to voltage changes (I = C dV/dt), so current peaks before voltage. The mnemonic is "ELI the ICE man": E (voltage) Leads I (current) in an Inductor; I (current) leads E (voltage) in a Capacitor.
D. Voltage leads current by 180 degrees: A 180° difference would make the two sinusoids opposite in sign at corresponding times. The derivative relation for an ideal inductor gives a quarter-cycle, or 90°, difference instead.
Key Educational Points:
  - Mnemonic: "ELI the ICE man" -- In an inductor (L), E leads I. In a capacitor (C), I leads E.
  - The 90-degree phase difference means that in a pure inductor, the average power dissipated is zero (reactive power only). Energy oscillates between the circuit and the inductor's magnetic field.
  - A real coil has winding resistance. In the simple series R–L model, its current lags the total applied voltage by an angle between 0° and 90°, rather than exactly 90°.
Bottom Line: For an ideal inductor with sinusoidal current, voltage leads current by 90° because v = L(di/dt). Remember ELI: E (voltage) leads I (current) in L (an inductor).

A. V = IR, where R is the resistance only: V = IR is valid across an ideal resistor in AC as well as DC. It also describes a circuit whose total impedance is purely resistive, but resistance alone does not cover a general circuit with net reactance.
B. V = I x XL, where XL is the inductive reactance only: V_L = IX_L gives the voltage amplitude across an ideal inductor at the specified frequency. For a more general circuit, use its total impedance magnitude.
D. V = I/(XL - XC), where the reactances determine the current: I/(X_L − X_C) has units A/Ω, not volts and not siemens. Voltage is current multiplied by impedance. In the series RLC case, that magnitude is √[R^2 + (X_L − X_C)^2].
Key Educational Points:
  - For a sine wave, V_rms = V_peak/√2 and I_rms = I_peak/√2. Keep the voltage and current conventions consistent.
  - For sinusoidal signals in a passive RLC circuit, average power is V_rmsI_rms cosφ. Ideal inductors and capacitors return their stored energy; resistance dissipates energy.
  - In a series RLC circuit, the same current passes through all three elements: V_R = IR, V_L = IX_L and V_C = IX_C. Their voltage amplitudes cannot simply be added because their phases differ.
Bottom Line: For sinusoidal steady-state amplitudes, V = IZ with Z the impedance magnitude. For a series RLC circuit specifically, Z = √[R^2 + (X_L − X_C)^2]. Use both peak quantities or both RMS quantities.

Q54  [phys-234]  Acoustics - Sound Velocity  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: The speed of sound is fastest in which of the following media?

  A. Air at room temperature
  B. Distilled water at room temperature
  C. A steel rail   <= key
  D. A vacuum

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: A steel rail
Among the listed media, longitudinal sound travels fastest in steel. Speed depends on elastic stiffness relative to density, not on density alone.
Medium | Approximate sound speed | Steel | About 5000–6000 m/s for longitudinal sound, depending on the steel and wave mode | Water at room temperature | About 1480 m/s | Air at 20 °C | About 343 m/s | Vacuum | Not applicable: sound cannot propagate | Steel's large elastic stiffness allows longitudinal disturbances to travel faster than in water or air. A vacuum cannot support an ordinary sound wave because it has no material medium.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Air at room temperature: Air has much lower elastic stiffness than steel. Considering stiffness together with density gives a sound speed around 343 m/s at 20 °C, well below the longitudinal speed in steel. Low density alone is not an explanation for a low speed.
B. Distilled water at room temperature: Water is much less compressible than air. Its stiffness relative to density gives a speed around 1480 m/s at room temperature, faster than air but slower than longitudinal sound in steel.
D. A vacuum: A vacuum has no material medium to carry sound. Its sound speed is therefore not assigned a value of zero; sound does not propagate there. Light can cross a vacuum because it is an electromagnetic wave.
Key Educational Points:
  - For the listed examples, longitudinal sound travels faster in steel than in water or air. This is not a universal ordering for every solid, liquid, gas and wave mode.
  - Near ordinary temperatures, the speed of sound in air is approximately v = 331 + 0.6T m/s, where T is in degrees Celsius.
  - If sound travels equal distances through a steel rail and through air, the longitudinal disturbance in the rail arrives sooner because its speed is greater.
Bottom Line: For these examples: longitudinal sound in steel is faster than sound in water, which is faster than sound in air. Sound needs a material medium and cannot propagate in a vacuum.

Q56  [phys-236]  Acoustics - Doppler Effect  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An ambulance travels directly toward an observer who is stationary relative to still air. Its speed is less than the speed of sound, and its siren emits a constant frequency. The observer hears a frequency that is:

  A. Lower than the actual frequency emitted by the siren
  B. Equal to the actual frequency emitted by the siren
  C. Higher than the actual frequency emitted by the siren   <= key
  D. Alternating between higher and lower than the actual frequency

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Higher than the actual frequency emitted by the siren
The Doppler effect states that when a sound source moves toward an observer, the observed frequency is higher than the emitted frequency. This occurs because each successive wavefront is emitted from a position closer to the observer, effectively compressing the wavelength and increasing the frequency perceived.
f_observed = f_source x (v_sound)/(v_sound - v_source)
Since v_source is subtracted in the denominator (source approaching), the denominator is smaller than the numerator, making f_observed > f_source.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Lower than the actual frequency emitted by the siren: A lower perceived frequency (redshift in sound) occurs when the source is moving away from the observer. As the ambulance passes and recedes, the observer would then perceive a drop in frequency. This explains the familiar pitch change as an ambulance drives by.
B. Equal to the actual frequency emitted by the siren: For a source and observer both stationary in the air, the received and emitted frequencies would match. Here the approaching source compresses the spacing between arriving wavefronts, so the received frequency is higher.
D. Alternating between higher and lower than the actual frequency: During the stated direct approach, the received frequency stays above the emitted frequency. Nothing in the question describes repeated changes between approaching and receding motion.
Key Educational Points:
  - The Doppler effect applies to all waves, not just sound. In light, approaching sources appear blueshifted (higher frequency) and receding sources appear redshifted (lower frequency).
  - In medicine, Doppler ultrasound uses this principle to measure blood flow velocity: blood flowing toward the transducer reflects higher-frequency echoes.
  - The moving source changes the wavefront spacing in the air. The sound speed relative to that air remains set by the medium, while the stationary observer receives a higher frequency during approach.
Bottom Line: Approaching source = higher perceived frequency (wavefronts compressed). Receding source = lower perceived frequency (wavefronts stretched). This is the Doppler effect, used in medical ultrasound, radar, and astronomy.

Q57  [phys-237]  Acoustics - Frequency and Period  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: If the frequency of a sound wave is doubled, what happens to its period?

  A. The period doubles
  B. The period is halved   <= key
  C. The period remains unchanged
  D. The period is quadrupled

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The period is halved
Frequency (f) and period (T) are inversely related:
T = 1/f   or equivalently   f = 1/T
If frequency doubles (f → 2f), the period becomes: T' = 1/(2f) = T/2. The period is halved.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. The period doubles: This would require a direct proportionality between frequency and period, which contradicts their inverse relationship. If frequency increases, period must decrease. Doubling the period would actually require halving the frequency.
C. The period remains unchanged: Period and frequency are reciprocals. If frequency changes from f to 2f, the period must change from 1/f to 1/(2f), so it cannot remain unchanged.
D. The period is quadrupled: This would imply T is proportional to f^2, which is not the case. The inverse relationship means if f is multiplied by any factor n, T is divided by the same factor n.
Key Educational Points:
  - Period is the time for one complete oscillation (measured in seconds). Frequency is the number of complete oscillations per second (measured in hertz, Hz).
  - The wave equation connects frequency, wavelength, and speed: v = f x λ. If frequency doubles in the same medium (constant v), the wavelength is halved.
  - This inverse relationship applies to all periodic phenomena: sound waves, electromagnetic waves, pendulums, and any oscillatory motion.
Bottom Line: T = 1/f. Doubling frequency halves the period. Tripling frequency gives one-third the period. Frequency and period are always inversely proportional -- they are reciprocals of each other.

Q58  [phys-238]  Semiconductors - Doping  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An N-type semiconductor is created by doping a silicon crystal with atoms from which group of the periodic table?

  A. Group IIIA (e.g., boron, gallium)
  B. Group VA (e.g., phosphorus, arsenic)   <= key
  C. Group IA (e.g., sodium, potassium)
  D. Group VIIA (e.g., chlorine, bromine)

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Group VA (e.g., phosphorus, arsenic)
N-type semiconductors are produced by doping silicon (Group IVA, 4 valence electrons) with elements from Group VA (5 valence electrons), such as phosphorus, arsenic, or antimony. These donor atoms contribute four electrons to covalent bonds with silicon and have one extra electron that is loosely bound and easily freed to become a conduction electron. The "N" stands for negative, referring to the excess negative charge carriers (electrons).
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Group IIIA (e.g., boron, gallium): Group IIIA elements have only 3 valence electrons. When they substitute for silicon, they create an electron deficiency (a "hole") in the crystal lattice. This produces a P-type semiconductor, where the majority charge carriers are positive holes. These dopants are called acceptors because they accept electrons from neighboring bonds.
C. Group IA (e.g., sodium, potassium): Sodium and potassium are not the standard substitutional donor dopants used to make the N-type silicon described here. The intended mechanism uses a five-valence-electron atom such as phosphorus, with four electrons involved in bonding and one available as a donor electron.
D. Group VIIA (e.g., chlorine, bromine): Chlorine and bromine are not the standard donor dopants for this silicon question. Phosphorus and arsenic provide the required five-valence-electron substitutional donor mechanism.
Key Educational Points:
  - N-type: Group VA dopant (donor) → extra electron → majority carriers are electrons.
  - P-type: Group IIIA dopant (acceptor) → electron hole → majority carriers are holes.
  - Doping changes the concentration of mobile carriers. N-type and P-type describe the majority carrier, not a net bulk charge: the semiconductor remains electrically neutral overall.
  - Joining N-type and P-type regions forms a PN junction, used in junction diodes and bipolar transistors.
Bottom Line: N-type = Group VA dopant (5 valence electrons, 1 extra → donor → excess electrons). P-type = Group IIIA dopant (3 valence electrons, 1 missing → acceptor → excess holes). Silicon is Group IVA with 4 valence electrons.

Q59  [phys-239]  Semiconductors - Forward Bias  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An ordinary PN junction diode is given enough forward bias to carry appreciable current. Which of the following occurs?

  A. The depletion region widens and current flow is negligible
  B. The diode has high resistance and blocks current flow
  C. The depletion region narrows and a relatively large current flows   <= key
  D. Only holes can cross the junction while electrons are blocked

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The depletion region narrows and a relatively large current flows
In forward bias, the positive terminal of the battery is connected to the P-side and the negative terminal to the N-side. This arrangement:
  - Pushes holes from the P-side toward the junction
  - Pushes electrons from the N-side toward the junction
  - Narrows the depletion region by reducing the potential barrier
  - Allows forward current to increase continuously and strongly with applied voltage; the actual current depends on the diode, temperature and external circuit
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. The depletion region widens and current flow is negligible: Reverse bias widens the depletion region and strongly limits current below breakdown. Forward bias instead lowers the barrier and narrows the depletion region.
B. The diode has high resistance and blocks current flow: Forward current rises strongly as the forward voltage increases. There is no abrupt universal threshold at which a previously perfect insulator switches on; a constant forward drop is a simplified circuit model.
D. Only holes can cross the junction while electrons are blocked: In forward bias, both charge carriers contribute to current flow. Holes move from P to N across the junction, while electrons simultaneously move from N to P. The conventional current direction is from P to N (anode to cathode).
Key Educational Points:
  - Forward bias reduces the junction barrier and depletion width, allowing appreciable forward current under suitable bias.
  - Reverse bias widens the depletion region. Below breakdown, only a relatively small leakage current flows.
  - A diode preferentially conducts in one direction under ordinary operating conditions, which makes it useful for rectification. Reverse leakage and breakdown are limits to the ideal one-way-valve model.
Bottom Line: Forward bias connects P to + and N to −, lowering the barrier and narrowing the depletion region. A silicon drop around 0.6–0.7 V is an approximate operating model, not a universal threshold; current also depends on temperature and the circuit.

Q60  [phys-240]  Semiconductors - Reverse Bias  correct answer: C   (CRITICAL: a wider depletion layer LOWERS junction capacitance)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An ordinary PN junction diode under reverse bias, below its breakdown voltage, exhibits:

  A. Low resistance and large forward current
  B. A narrowed depletion region and increased conductivity
  C. High resistance and only a very small leakage current   <= key
  D. Zero resistance due to quantum tunneling effects

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: High resistance and only a very small leakage current
In reverse bias, the positive terminal is connected to the N-side and the negative terminal to the P-side. This pulls majority carriers away from the junction: electrons in the N-region are pulled toward the positive terminal, and holes in the P-region are pulled toward the negative terminal. The result:
  - The depletion region widens substantially
  - The potential barrier increases
  - The diode presents very high resistance
  - Only a small leakage current flows, associated with thermally generated carriers; its magnitude depends on the device and temperature
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Low resistance and large forward current: Large forward current requires forward bias. Under the stated reverse bias below breakdown, the junction instead strongly limits current.
B. A narrowed depletion region and increased conductivity: The depletion region widens under reverse bias, not narrows. The widened depletion region acts as an insulating layer, decreasing conductivity rather than increasing it.
D. Zero resistance due to quantum tunneling effects: Tunneling can contribute to Zener breakdown in a sufficiently reverse-biased, heavily doped junction, but that does not make the junction a zero-resistance conductor. This question specifies operation below breakdown.
Key Educational Points:
  - Above the reverse breakdown voltage, Zener or avalanche mechanisms can allow a much larger reverse current. Excess current or power can damage a diode; devices intended for breakdown operation still require suitable current and power limits.
  - Reverse leakage generally increases as temperature rises, but a fixed doubling for every 10 °C is not a universal law for all diodes and operating conditions.
  - Increasing reverse bias widens the depletion layer and lowers junction capacitance. Approximately, C_j = εA/W: a larger depletion width W gives smaller capacitance. Varactor diodes use this voltage-dependent capacitance for tuning.
Bottom Line: Below breakdown, reverse bias widens the depletion region and strongly limits current, while allowing some leakage. The wider depletion layer also reduces junction capacitance.
