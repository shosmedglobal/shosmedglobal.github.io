Q1  [phys-121]  Gravitation  correct answer: A
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A planet has twice the mass and twice the radius of Earth. What is the gravitational acceleration on its surface compared to Earth's g?

  A. g/2   <= key
  B. g
  C. 2g
  D. 4g

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: g/2
g' = GM'/R'² = G(2M)/(2R)² = 2GM/4R² = (1/2)(GM/R²) = g/2.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. g: Doubling the mass alone would double the gravitational acceleration, but the radius also doubles. Its square appears in the denominator, giving an overall factor of 2/4.
C. 2g: This includes the doubled mass but ignores the doubled radius. Use both changes in g = GM/R^2.
D. 4g: This is the factor by which R^2 increases. It belongs in the denominator, so it reduces the effect of the increased mass rather than multiplying it.
Key Concept: Surface gravity g = GM/R²; it depends on both mass and radius.

Q2  [phys-122]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Two point charges of +3 μC and −6 μC are 0.3 m apart. What is the magnitude of the electrostatic force? (k = 9 × 10^9 N·m²/C²)

  A. 0.6 N
  B. 1.2 N
  C. 1.8 N   <= key
  D. 5.4 N

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 1.8 N
The magnitude is F = k|q_1q_2|/r^2 = (9 × 10^9)(3 × 10^−6)(6 × 10^−6)/(0.3)^2 = 0.162/0.09 = 1.8 N. The force is attractive because the charges have opposite signs.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0.6 N: This is one-third of the correct force. The numerator k|q_1q_2| is 0.162 N·m^2, and dividing by r^2 = 0.09 m^2 gives 1.8 N.
B. 1.2 N: At the stated separation, 1.2 N would result if the second charge had magnitude 4 μC. Its actual magnitude is 6 μC, giving 1.8 N.
D. 5.4 N: This is three times the correct force. Multiply the charge magnitudes once and divide by the squared separation; no additional factor of 3 is needed.
Key Concept: Coulomb force magnitude is F = k|q_1q_2|/r^2. Like charges repel and opposite charges attract.

Q3  [phys-123]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: What is the magnitude of the electric field 0.5 m from a point charge of +2 × 10^−6 C? (k = 9 × 10^9 N·m^2/C^2)

  A. 18,000 N/C
  B. 36,000 N/C
  C. 72,000 N/C   <= key
  D. 144,000 N/C

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 72,000 N/C
The field magnitude is E = k|q|/r^2 = (9 × 10^9)(2 × 10^−6)/(0.5)^2 = 18,000/0.25 = 72,000 N/C. The field points radially away from this positive charge.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 18,000 N/C: This is the field at 1 m from the same charge. At 0.5 m, the inverse-square relationship makes the field four times larger.
B. 36,000 N/C: This comes from dividing 18,000 by 0.5 rather than by (0.5)^2. Electric field from a point charge varies as 1/r^2, not 1/r.
D. 144,000 N/C: This is twice the correct field. The distance contributes a factor of four relative to 1 m, giving 72,000 N/C, not a factor of eight.
Key Concept: A point charge produces a field of magnitude E = k|q|/r^2, directed away from a positive charge and toward a negative charge.

Q4  [phys-124]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω, and 6 Ω are connected in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance?

  A. 0.5 Ω
  B. 1 Ω   <= key
  C. 3.67 Ω
  D. 11 Ω

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 1 Ω
1/R_eq = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 3/6 + 2/6 + 1/6 = 6/6 = 1. So R_eq = 1 Ω.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0.5 Ω: The reciprocal sum is 1 Ω^−1, whose reciprocal is 1 Ω. A resistance of 0.5 Ω would require a total conductance of 2 Ω^−1.
C. 3.67 Ω: This is approximately the arithmetic mean of 2, 3 and 6 Ω. Parallel resistance is found by adding reciprocals, and must be less than the smallest branch resistance.
D. 11 Ω: This is 2 + 3 + 6 Ω, the equivalent resistance of the three resistors in series. The question specifies parallel branches.
Key Concept: For parallel resistors: 1/R_eq = Σ(1/R_i).

Q5  [phys-125]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: A
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An ideal parallel-plate capacitor has vacuum between plates of area 0.02 m^2 separated by 0.001 m. Neglecting edge effects, what is its capacitance? (ε_0 = 8.85 × 10^−12 F/m)

  A. 1.77 × 10^−10 F   <= key
  B. 8.85 × 10^−10 F
  C. 1.77 × 10^−11 F
  D. 8.85 × 10^−11 F

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 1.77 × 10^−10 F
C = ε_0A/d = 8.85×10^−12 × 0.02 / 0.001 = 8.85×10^−12 × 20 = 1.77 × 10^−10 F ≈ 177 pF.
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. 8.85 × 10^−10 F: This is five times the correct capacitance. The geometric factor is A/d = 0.02/0.001 = 20 m, not 100 m.
C. 1.77 × 10^−11 F: This is ten times too small. Dividing the plate area by the separation gives 20 m, so multiplying by 8.85 × 10^−12 F/m gives 1.77 × 10^−10 F.
D. 8.85 × 10^−11 F: This is half the correct capacitance. The parallel-plate formula has no factor of ½; that factor appears in the capacitor energy formula, ½CV^2.
Key Concept: For a parallel-plate capacitor with vacuum between the plates and negligible edge effects, C = ε_0A/d. Larger area increases capacitance; larger separation decreases it.

  A. 2.4 μF   <= key
  B. 5 μF

  D. 1.2 μF

1/C_eq = 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 → C_eq = 12/5 = 2.4 μF.

B. 5 μF: This is the arithmetic mean of 4 and 6 μF. For series capacitors, add reciprocal capacitances rather than averaging them.

D. 1.2 μF: This is half of the correct result. The reciprocal sum is 5/12 μF^−1, so its reciprocal is 12/5 = 2.4 μF, with no further division by two.
Key Concept: Capacitors in series: 1/C_eq = Σ(1/C_i).

Q7  [phys-127]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A 10 Ω resistor carries a current of 3 A. What power is dissipated?

  A. 30 W
  B. 90 W   <= key
  C. 300 W
  D. 9 W

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 90 W
P = I²R = 9 × 10 = 90 W.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 30 W: The product IR is 30 V, the voltage across the resistor. Power requires another factor of current: P = I(IR) = 90 W.
C. 300 W: This value would correspond to a larger current, about √(300/10) = 5.48 A. With the stated 3 A, I^2R gives 90 W.
D. 9 W: This is the numerical value of 3^2, but the resistance still has to be included. Multiply 9 A^2 by 10 Ω to obtain 90 W.
Key Concept: Power in a resistor: P = I²R = V²/R = IV.

Q9  [phys-129]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Two resistors of 3 Ω and 6 Ω are connected in series across an ideal 12 V battery. What is the voltage across the 6 Ω resistor?

  A. 4 V
  B. 6 V
  C. 8 V   <= key
  D. 12 V

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 8 V
Total resistance = 3 + 6 = 9 Ω. Current = 12/9 = 4/3 A. Voltage across 6 Ω: V = IR = (4/3) × 6 = 8 V.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 4 V: This is the voltage across the 3 Ω resistor: (12/9) × 3 = 4 V. The 6 Ω resistor takes twice that voltage because the same current passes through both.
B. 6 V: An equal 6 V split would require equal resistances. In series, voltage divides in proportion to resistance, here in a 1:2 ratio.
D. 12 V: The full 12 V is across both resistors together. Only a resistor directly across the battery terminals would have that entire voltage across it.
Key Concept: Voltage divider: in series, V across each resistor is proportional to its resistance.

Q10  [phys-130]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: C   (now asks for the electric potential-energy increase)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A positive charge of +5 C is moved to a point whose electric potential is 24 V higher. What is the increase in its electric potential energy?

  A. 4.8 J
  B. 29 J
  C. 120 J   <= key
  D. 0.21 J

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 120 J
The change in electric potential energy is ΔU = qΔV = (5 C)(24 V) = 120 J. A positive charge gains potential energy when moved to a higher potential. The electric field does −120 J of work during this displacement.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 4.8 J: Dividing 24 by 5 gives 4.8, but voltage is already energy per unit charge. Multiply the potential difference by the charge to obtain an energy.
B. 29 J: This adds 5 and 24. Charge and voltage have different units and cannot be added; their product has units of joules.
D. 0.21 J: This is approximately 5/24. Dividing charge by voltage has units of capacitance, not energy; the required operation is qΔV.
Key Concept: Electric potential difference is potential-energy change per unit charge: ΔU = qΔV. Work done by the electric field is −ΔU.

Q12  [phys-132]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: According to Kirchhoff's junction rule, at any junction in a circuit:

  A. The sum of voltages is zero
  B. The sum of currents entering equals the sum leaving   <= key
  C. The total resistance is conserved
  D. Power input equals power output

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The sum of currents entering equals the sum leaving
Kirchhoff's junction rule (current law) states that the total current flowing into a junction equals the total current flowing out. This is based on conservation of charge.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. The sum of voltages is zero: The algebraic sum of potential differences around a closed circuit loop is the voltage rule. The junction rule concerns charge flow into and out of a junction.
C. The total resistance is conserved: Resistance is a circuit property, not a quantity conserved at a junction. Kirchhoff's junction rule follows from conservation of electric charge.
D. Power input equals power output: Power balance concerns energy conservation. It is not the statement of the junction rule, which equates incoming and outgoing currents.
Key Concept: Kirchhoff's current law: ΣI_in = ΣI_out at any junction.

Q13  [phys-133]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: D
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: An incandescent light bulb is rated 60 W at 120 V. What is the resistance of its filament at normal operating temperature?

  A. 2 Ω
  B. 60 Ω
  C. 120 Ω
  D. 240 Ω   <= key

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 240 Ω
P = V²/R → R = V²/P = (120)²/60 = 14400/60 = 240 Ω.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 2 Ω: The numerical ratio 120/60 is 2, but V/P has units of inverse current, not resistance. The required ratio is V^2/P.
B. 60 Ω: This repeats the power rating as a resistance. Watts and ohms measure different quantities; use both the voltage and the power.
C. 120 Ω: This repeats the voltage as a resistance. Squaring 120 V and dividing by 60 W gives 240 Ω.
Key Concept: For a resistive filament at its operating condition, R = V^2/P. The filament resistance changes with temperature, so its cold resistance can differ.

Q14  [phys-134]  Electricity & DC Circuits  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Four identical resistors of 8 Ω each are connected: two in series forming a branch, and two such branches in parallel. What is the total resistance?

  A. 2 Ω
  B. 4 Ω
  C. 8 Ω   <= key
  D. 16 Ω

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 8 Ω
Each series branch: 8 + 8 = 16 Ω. Two branches in parallel: 1/R = 1/16 + 1/16 = 2/16 → R = 8 Ω.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 2 Ω: Four 8 Ω resistors all in parallel would give 2 Ω. Here they are arranged as two series pairs, so that shortcut does not apply.
B. 4 Ω: Two individual 8 Ω resistors in parallel would give 4 Ω. Each branch in this circuit contains two resistors in series and therefore has 16 Ω.
D. 16 Ω: This is the resistance of one series branch. Adding an identical parallel branch halves that equivalent resistance to 8 Ω.
Key Concept: Identify which components actually form series or parallel groups. Here each series pair gives 16 Ω, and the two equal parallel branches give 8 Ω.

Q15  [phys-135]  Vibrations, Waves & Sound  correct answer: B
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A mass on a spring oscillates with a period of 0.4 s. What is the frequency?

  A. 0.4 Hz
  B. 2.5 Hz   <= key
  C. 4 Hz
  D. 25 Hz

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 2.5 Hz
f = 1/T = 1/0.4 = 2.5 Hz.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 0.4 Hz: This copies the period's numerical value into the frequency. Period is seconds per cycle, while frequency is cycles per second, so they are reciprocals.
C. 4 Hz: Four oscillations per second would have a period of 0.25 s. A period of 0.4 s gives 1/0.4 = 2.5 oscillations per second.
D. 25 Hz: This would be correct for a period of 0.04 s. The stated period is ten times longer, so the frequency is ten times smaller.
Key Concept: Frequency and period are reciprocals: f = 1/T.

Q43  [phys-163]  AC Electricity & Magnetism  correct answer: C
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: In a series RLC circuit with R > 0, driven by a sinusoidal voltage of fixed amplitude, what is true at resonance in steady operation?

  A. Impedance is maximum
  B. Current is minimum
  C. Impedance equals the resistance R only   <= key
  D. The circuit is purely inductive

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Impedance equals the resistance R only
At series resonance, the inductive and capacitive reactances are equal: X_L = X_C. Their opposite phase contributions cancel, so the impedance magnitude is Z = √[R^2 + (X_L − X_C)^2] = R. With the source voltage amplitude fixed, the current amplitude is greatest at this frequency.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Impedance is maximum: In a series RLC circuit, cancellation of the reactances makes the impedance magnitude minimum, equal to R. A maximum-impedance resonance belongs to a different circuit arrangement.
B. Current is minimum: With source voltage amplitude fixed, I = V/Z is greatest when Z is smallest. At series resonance it is R, not a large reactance, that limits the current.
D. The circuit is purely inductive: The inductive and capacitive contributions cancel at resonance. The overall circuit is purely resistive at that frequency, although both the inductor and capacitor are still present.
Key Concept: For an ideal series RLC circuit at resonance, X_L = X_C and Z = R. The current is in phase with the source voltage and is maximal for a fixed source voltage amplitude.

Q54  [phys-174]  Nuclear Physics  correct answer: B

STEM: The mass defect of a nucleus is:

  A. The mass of the electrons
  B. The difference between the mass of individual nucleons and the actual nuclear mass   <= key
  C. The mass of the neutrons only
  D. Always zero for stable nuclei

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: The difference between the mass of individual nucleons and the actual nuclear mass
The mass defect Δm is the total mass of the separated protons and neutrons minus the mass of the bound nucleus. The bound state has lower rest energy by Δmc^2. This is the binding energy released when that nucleus forms from separated nucleons, and the energy required to separate it again.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. The mass of the electrons: Electron masses are not what nuclear mass defect measures. Compare the nucleus with the same number of separate protons and neutrons.
C. The mass of the neutrons only: Neutron masses contribute to the mass of the separated nucleons, but the mass defect is the difference between that total and the actual nuclear mass.
D. Always zero for stable nuclei: A stable bound nucleus generally has a positive mass defect. Its lower rest energy is evidence of binding, not an error or instability.
Key Concept: Nuclear binding energy is E_b = Δmc^2, where Δm is the mass of separated nucleons minus the mass of the bound nucleus.

STEM: Water flows steadily through a pipe with no leaks, narrowing from a cross-sectional area of 0.04 m^2 to 0.01 m^2. Treat water as incompressible. If the average speed in the wider section is 2 m/s, what is the average speed in the narrower section?

  B. 8 m/s   <= key
  C. 0.5 m/s
  D. 16 m/s

Correct Answer: 8 m/s

STEM: An incompressible fluid flows steadily along a horizontal pipe. Neglect friction and assume no pump or turbine lies between the two points considered. Along the same streamline, if the speed is higher at the second point, how does its static pressure compare?

  A. Pressure increases
  B. Pressure decreases   <= key
  C. Pressure stays the same
  D. Pressure first increases then decreases

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: Pressure decreases
Under the stated assumptions, Bernoulli's equation along a horizontal streamline is P + ½ρv^2 = constant. The faster point has a larger kinetic-energy term per unit volume, so its static pressure P is lower. This comparison depends on the stated flow conditions; it is not a rule for every pair of points in every moving fluid.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. Pressure increases: This contradicts Bernoulli's equation. Many students assume faster flow means 'more force' and thus more pressure, but it is the opposite.
C. Pressure stays the same: Pressure only stays the same if speed stays the same. Changing speed requires a pressure change.
D. Pressure first increases then decreases: The question compares two locations under the stated conditions, not a compulsory two-stage process. The location with greater speed has lower static pressure.
Key Concept: For steady, incompressible flow with negligible friction and no added or extracted mechanical energy, P + ½ρv^2 + ρgh is constant along a streamline. At equal height, higher speed then means lower static pressure.

Q58  [phys-178]  Bernoulli's Equation  correct answer: A   (distractor B arithmetic corrected: 2000 Pa against 4000 Pa)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: Water of density 1000 kg/m^3 flows steadily along a horizontal pipe. Neglect friction and assume no pump or turbine between two points on the same streamline. At A the pressure is 200,000 Pa and speed is 1 m/s; at B the speed is 3 m/s. What is the pressure at B?

  A. 196,000 Pa   <= key
  B. 198,000 Pa
  C. 204,000 Pa
  D. 192,000 Pa

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 196,000 Pa
Using Bernoulli's equation for a horizontal pipe:
P_A + 1/2 ρv_A^2 = P_B + 1/2 ρv_B^2
200,000 + 1/2(1000)(1)^2 = P_B + 1/2(1000)(3)^2
200,000 + 500 = P_B + 4,500
P_B = 200,500 - 4,500 = 196,000 Pa
Why the other choices are wrong:
B. 198,000 Pa: This gives a 2000 Pa drop, which would result from ½ρ(v_B − v_A)^2 = ½(1000)(3 − 1)^2. The required difference is ½ρ(v_B^2 − v_A^2) = ½(1000)(9 − 1) = 4000 Pa.
C. 204,000 Pa: This adds the pressure difference instead of subtracting it, contradicting Bernoulli's principle.
D. 192,000 Pa: This doubles the pressure drop, possibly by forgetting the factor of 1/2 in 1/2ρv^2.
Key Concept: In this horizontal Bernoulli comparison, P_B = P_A − ½ρ(v_B^2 − v_A^2). Use the difference of the squared speeds, not the square of their difference.

Q59  [phys-179]  Bernoulli's Equation  correct answer: B   (distractors A and C arithmetic corrected)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEM: A large open tank contains water. A small hole is 5 m below the water surface and discharges into the same atmospheric pressure as the surface. Neglect frictional losses and the speed of the falling water surface. What is the water's exit speed? (g = 10 m/s^2)

  A. 5 m/s
  B. 10 m/s   <= key
  C. 50 m/s
  D. 100 m/s

EXPLANATION:
Correct Answer: 10 m/s
This is Torricelli's theorem, a special case of Bernoulli's equation. The speed of efflux from a hole at depth h below the surface is:
v = √(2gh)
v = √(2 x 10 x 5) = √100 = 10 m/s
This is exactly the same speed as an object would have if it fell freely from height h. Bernoulli's equation reduces to this when the tank surface moves negligibly slowly.
Why the other choices are wrong:
A. 5 m/s: An exit speed of 5 m/s would correspond to a depth v^2/(2g) = 25/20 = 1.25 m under this model. The stated depth is 5 m, giving 10 m/s.
C. 50 m/s: The product gh is 50 m^2/s^2, which is not a speed. Use v = √(2gh) = √100 = 10 m/s.
D. 100 m/s: This uses v = 2gh directly (2 x 10 x 5 = 100) without the square root.
Key Concept: For a large open tank discharging to the same atmospheric pressure, negligible surface speed and negligible losses give v = √(2gh). Under these conditions the exit speed equals the free-fall speed from height h.
